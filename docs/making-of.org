#+TITLE: Making of "Hello World"
#+SUBTITLE: The reference demo taken apart
#+SETUPFILE: setup.org
#+OPTIONS: toc:3

[[file:index.org][↑ Back to the index]]

The reference demo (=demos/hello-world/main.c= and =demos/hello-world/shaders/=)
is an intro of about 279 seconds in three scenes and an end card. It plays once
and stops. It also serves as a tour of the engine. This page takes it apart.
It starts with what the show is and when each thing happens. Then it goes
through how each scene and the end card are drawn, the music and how to change
it, the notes that apply across the scenes, and how the program ends.

The linked video and the repository stills show the demo as it is now. They
were rendered offline with Mesa on Intel UHD 620 integrated graphics, at
3840x2160 internally and downsampled to 1920x1080 for 60 fps playback. The
16,722-frame master took about 57 minutes to render before encoding.

Some performance measurements and image comparisons on this page were made on
an earlier version. It still had cumulus clouds and dust motes in the jungle
and beach reflections of nearby objects, and its seabed placement, predator
path, camera work, materials, animation, music and end-card wording were
different. Unless a figure is identified as current, it hasn't been measured
again since.

* The concept
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-concept
:END:

The demo plays three scenes and an end card back to back, then stops:

- Scene 1, the big bang (50.3 s). A single bright dot hangs in a dark void with
  faint stars. On the first kick drop it bursts outward: the exposure kicks, a
  shock front races through the sky and over the floor, lighting up the dark
  universe behind it, and the camera jolts. The burst leaves about 180
  procedural shapes in pastel plastic, iridescent film and tinted glass, which
  orbit, spin and pulse to the beat over a glowing synthwave grid. At the second
  hook's downbeat the surviving shapes resolve into a five-turn spiral facing
  the camera, then loosen back into their orbits. Near the end
  the crowd recedes and the title MICROCOSM assembles itself out of flying
  cubes, under the blob. A synthwave sun rises behind the title and a light
  sweeps across it. Then everything collapses back to a dot as the camera pulls
  away, until the seed is a lone star in the black, up to the right.
- Scene 2, the descent and the deep (104.5 s). A sun rises where the star went
  out, and a blue-and-green planet appears in space beside it. The camera falls
  toward the planet, through the cloud deck and the sky and into the sea, and
  sinks into the deep, where the blob from Scene 1 is waiting without a face.
  The blob sets off on a wandering swim across the seabed and the camera
  follows. As the murk clears, the view fills with sand, boulders, swaying weed
  and circling schools of fish, all placed so that nothing intersects. A blue
  whale comes out of the clearing water and passes overhead with her calf. The
  camera eases back to give them room as the music opens out, and a realism
  push makes everything denser and more detailed. Moon jellies pulse with cyan
  light as the water darkens briefly. Near the end a big, dark predator
  comes out of the murk and stalks straight at the blob, which hasn't noticed.
  It lunges with its jaws wide open and only just misses. Only then does the
  blob panic and bolt, and the predator turns to chase it. Once it has got away,
  the blob changes. The camera circles it while its eyes grow from nothing to
  their Scene 1 size (it has none until the encounter is over), and its lumpy
  shape rounds out into a sphere. Then, still in a panic, it bolts on across the
  ocean, further and faster. It spirals outward into fresh water and uncovers
  more and more complex structures: reef formations, kelp forests, coral
  gardens, crabs and schools of fish. One is a big school that it bolts straight
  through, the fish parting round it. By the end it is racing through a complete
  reef full of life, its coral massed on a bed of its own growth, and the
  picture burns out to white.
- Scene 3, the jungle (114.2 s). The white clears to the floor of a rainforest.
  Giant buttressed trees are hung with lianas, and there are palms and saplings,
  ferns, broad leaves and red heliconias over a litter of fallen leaves, with
  shafts of sun slanting through the humid haze. The camera looks round the
  clearing and climbs the trunks to the canopy as scarlet macaws cross a gap in
  the leaves. Then it comes down to a broad leaf beside us, where a seven-spot
  ladybug is walking. We go in close, and the ladybug opens its wing cases and
  flies up into the light. The camera lets it go and glides off between the
  trunks, stopping well back among them where two trunks frame a wide view of a
  beach. Tiny glimpses of a tyrannosaurus appear between the trunks before its
  head and shoulders emerge, about 21 m away. As it comes, the camera crouches
  low in the ferns on a longer lens, and its footfalls jolt the picture over
  a low, dissonant pulse. It walks out of the crowns' shade into a pool of sun,
  stops to look almost straight at us and opens its jaws with two snorts. We
  recoil, then hold still as it turns away and disappears behind a trunk. The
  dark music and quiet forest hold until it has gone. Then the camera steps out
  to the last row of trees. The blob from Scenes 1 and 2 rises out of the surf,
  now with two tiny new arms. As it hops up the beach the camera walks on out of the trees to meet
  it, and the lens lengthens until the blob stands about a fifth of the frame high. It
  settles on the wet sand, looks round and waves at us. Then it turns to the sea
  and plays in the swash. It hops down after a draining wave, jumps over the
  foaming edge of the next and lands in it in a burst of spray. The spray throws
  up a straw sun hat, a striped beach parasol and a folded deckchair. The blob
  catches them: the hat lands on its head and the parasol springs open over it.
  It stamps and splashes, and comes out of the water with them for the last
  time. It shivers itself dry on the sand and sits under its parasol watching
  the sea as a last wave laps round it. Meanwhile the camera goes back up among
  the trees and widens for a last wide view, and the scene fades to black on it.
- The end card (9.7 s). After the black we're back in Scene 1's world, with its
  violet sky, glowing grid and cubes. "HELLO MICROCOSMIC WORLD" flies together
  on three lines to a reprise of Scene 1's tune. The blob drops in from above,
  looking as it did on the beach: glossy blue jelly with big eyes and its little
  arms. It bounces along the words like the ball in a karaoke video, one
  syllable a beat, and each syllable lights up as it lands. Then it somersaults
  up onto HELLO, puffs itself up, flings its arms up and winks at us. The sun
  behind the words sets, the show fades out and the program exits.

Each scene plays once, with its own look and its own music, and hands over to
the next without a visible cut. Scene 1 goes black round a last star, and Scene
2's sun rises in its place. Scene 2 burns out to white, and Scene 3 opens out of
the white. Scene 3 fades to black, and the end card comes up out of the black.

Everything is generated at runtime, and there are no asset files. The meshes are
procedural and drawn instanced, an HDR post stack finishes the picture, the
camera follows a spline and a software synth plays the soundtrack.

* Scene 1: the big bang
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scene-1-the-big-bang
:END:

Scene 1 is the show's first 26 bars, 0-50.3 s. A seed of light hangs alone in
the dark and bursts on the first kick into a crowd of 182 shapes over a glowing
synthwave grid. Later the crowd makes way for the title MICROCOSM, built from
flying cubes, and at the end everything collapses back into the seed, which the
camera leaves behind as a lone star. [[#the-concept][The concept]] tells the
story, and [[#timeline][the timeline]] lines it up with the music. This section
is about how it's drawn. The end card, the show's last five bars
(269.0-278.7 s), comes back to the same world to say hello. It has a section of
its own after Scene 3's, [[#the-end-card][The end card]].

The C side is all in =demos/hello-world/main.c=. =animate= places the shapes,
=sim_step= runs their collisions, =camera_at= flies the camera and =s1_draw=
draws the world. Both =render_scene1= and the end card's =render_end= draw
through =s1_draw=. Five shader programs, all in =demos/hello-world/shaders/=,
do the drawing:

| Program    | Shaders                      | Draws                                                    |
|------------+------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
| =obj=      | =object.vert=, =object.frag= | the shapes, the seed, the logo's cubes, the blob's eyes  |
| =shadowsh= | =object.vert=, =shadow.frag= | the solid shapes into the key light's depth map          |
| =sky=      | =far.vert=, =sky.frag=       | the backdrop: gradient, glow, stars, sun and shock front |
| =grnd=     | =ground.vert=, =ground.frag= | the grid floor                                           |
| =blobsh=   | =blob.vert=, =blob.frag=     | the blob                                                 |

** How a frame is drawn
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: how-a-frame-is-drawn
:END:

=render_scene1= works out the look of the moment, and =s1_draw= draws it in a
fixed order:

1. =animate= fills one instance list per shape type: the seed, the crowd, the
   title's cubes and the blob's eyes. On the end card, =render_end= fills them
   with its own cubes and eyes instead.
2. The key light's depth pass draws the shapes, seen from the light, into
   Scene 2's sun map. The solids go through =shadowsh= and the glass through
   =object.frag=, which casts a third of a shadow
   ([[#the-key-light-shadow][The key light's shadow]]).
3. The Frame block goes up with the camera's view and the moment's colours,
   and =mc_renderer_begin= binds the HDR target.
4. The sky, a full-screen triangle with the depth test and depth writes off,
   so everything draws over it.
5. The grid, added on top. It's depth-tested but writes no depth, so bodies
   hide it and it can't hide them.
6. The solid shapes, one instanced draw call per shape type.
7. The blob, blended by a constant alpha. Like the grid, it's depth-tested
   without writing depth, so nearer shapes still hide it.
8. If there's any glass in the lists, a copy of the picture so far, and then
   the glass, which looks through the copy.
9. =mc_renderer_end= runs the engine's post stack with the moment's exposure,
   bloom and fade ([[#hdr-bloom-a-filmic-tonemap-and-taa][Exposure, bloom and fades]]).

** The acts and the envelopes
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: smooth-per-sequence-motion
:END:

Scene 1 is split into four acts that follow the music's sections. Each has its
own sky (=SEQ_TOP= above the horizon, =SEQ_BOT= below it) and its own pace
(=SEQ_SPEED=):

| Act       | Music          | Show time   | Sky          | Pace |
|-----------+----------------+-------------+--------------+------|
| I dawn    | intro, build   | 0-15.5 s    | a dusky rose | 0.9  |
| II play   | hook A         | 15.5-23.2 s | deep blue    | 1.15 |
| III deep  | breakdown      | 23.2-31.0 s | calm teal    | 0.7  |
| IV finale | hook B, outro  | 31.0-50.3 s | deep violet  | 1.1  |

The orbiting motion runs on =motion_time(t)=, a warped clock that adds up
real seconds, each scaled by its act's pace. It only ever climbs, and at a
boundary only its rate changes, so nothing jumps. =atmosphere()= cross-fades
the sky into the next act's over the 2.5 s before each boundary, so the change
of colour lands on it.

The first two acts have deep, dark skies, so the pastel crowd stands out
against them. The bottom colour is also the fog's, so the far side of the crowd
sinks into a darker distance. The ambient light on the bodies' shaded sides
comes from the same colours, so it stays low and they model well. With the
tight glow of the hooks ([[#the-sky-sky-frag][The sky]]), the middle of the
crowd is a warm hotspot against a deep sky. =s1_draw= brightens the colours
with the energy (below): the top by 0.30 + 0.22 × energy and the bottom by
0.7 + 0.5 × energy.

The motion is kept smooth. Each body breathes by 5% on a sine of its own. The
beat is a continuous sine (=0.5+0.5*sin(beats*TAU)=) that only makes the
emission and the bloom shimmer, with no pop on each beat and no pump of the
field of view. A smooth =energy()= envelope follows the sections. It rises over
the 5 s after the drop, dips to half through the breakdown, comes back for hook
B and dies away through the outro. Much of the brightness and motion is scaled
by it.

=expansion()= is how big the universe is, from 0 (the dot) to 1. It creeps up
to 0.02 before the bang, bursts open with an elastic overshoot over the 2.2 s
from the kick, and holds. Between =T_COLLAPSE= (44.6 s) and =T_BLACK= (49.2 s)
it eases back to 0. Every orbit's radius is scaled by it, and so is the
camera's path. =body_scale()= sizes the bodies on the same cues. They spring in
over 1.2 s with a little overshoot and shrink away through the collapse.

** Procedural meshes and instancing
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: procedural-meshes-instancing
:END:

=object.vert= builds six shape types from =gl_VertexID=: a ball, a rounded box,
a torus, a cone, a cylinder and a spiky sea urchin. Their meshes hold no vertex
data, only a vertex count (=mc_mesh_procedural=, see
[[file:architecture.org::#building-geometry-out-of-arithmetic][Building geometry out of arithmetic]]).
A per-draw uniform, =uK=, picks the shape: the shape type plus one, or 7 for
Scene 1's balls. For the programs that share =object.vert=, =uK= 0 is Scene 2's
seabed, built from the heights that =uwplace.comp= worked out.

- The ball and the rounded box come from =cube()=, which builds the six n x n
  grids of a cube. Scene 1's ball uses 12 x 12 (=uK= 7: a 48-sided outline and
  5,184 vertices), and the rounded box and the urchin's core use 8 x 8 (2,304).
  Each grid coordinate is bent by the odd polynomial u·(e + (1 - e)·u²). With
  e = 0.75 the cells come out even once they're pushed onto a ball. With e = 1.5
  they crowd toward the edges of the rounded box, which is a 0.6 core grown by a
  0.2 radius. The polynomial keeps 0, 1 and -1 exact and the grid coordinate
  is (2i - n)/n, so neighbouring faces meet bit for bit. A =pow()= warp would give
  NaN at 0 on the GPU, and cracks.
- The torus (28 x 14), the cone and the cylinder (28 steps round, in base, side
  and top bands) and the urchin's 18 spikes come from =lathe()=, which turns a
  profile about +Y. The spikes have 16 steps round, which with the core brings
  the urchin to 7,488 vertices. Each spike points along its own Fibonacci
  (golden-angle) direction, so the spikes spread evenly over the ball. Each is
  also sunk 0.1 into the ball (centred 0.7 out, with a half-height of 0.3 and
  the tip at 1.0). So the depth test cuts the junction in a curve, and no lip
  stands out round the spike's base.
- In a plain triangle list, a quad's corners are =corner(v)= = (22, 52) >> v % 6 & 1.

The box stops at 8 x 8 because at 12 x 12 its bevels cost 5 ms more on the
UHD 620 with the earlier end card's 350 letter cubes, for no visible difference. The
ball's mesh itself stays at 6 x 6 (=uK= 1, 1,296 vertices), because Scene 2
draws its bubbles, its specks of marine snow and the blob's eyes from it. At
12 x 12 those would come to about 2.9 M vertices a frame instead of 0.7 M, for
specks a few pixels across. So =s1_shapes= draws Scene 1's balls (the crowd's,
the seed and the blob's eyes) with four times the mesh's vertex count and
=uK= 7. The mesh carries no vertex data, so asking it for more vertices needs
no new buffer. =animate= skips the bodies while they have no size, before the
bang and after the collapse, since a shape of zero size would still cost the
depth and lit passes all its vertices.

=ground.vert= makes the 140 m floor the same way, from six vertices. Each frame
the animation fills one per-instance array per shape type (=mc_instance=: a
model matrix and a colour whose fourth channel is a code, see
[[#the-instance-colour-codes][The instance colour's codes]]). =s1_shapes= draws
each array with one instanced call
([[file:toolkit.org::#gpu-meshes-instancing][GPU meshes & instancing]]). It runs
four times a frame (see [[#how-a-frame-is-drawn][How a frame is drawn]]), and
only the first pass uploads the arrays, so each goes to the GPU once a frame.
The whole crowd, and later the title's 142 cubes or the end card's 323, takes
six draw calls a pass. The seed, the cubes and the blob's eyes are more
instances of the ball and the rounded box, so they need no geometry of their
own.

Each body has a fixed random character: its shape, orbit radius (2-8.5 m), spin
axis and speed, colour, glow and base scale. =build_bodies= draws it from a
fixed seed (=mc_rng_seed=), so the universe is the same on every run. An orbit
is a base direction turned about Y over time, plus a vertical sway, and
=expansion()= scales its radius from about 0 (the dot) up to full and back.

During the breakdown's last bar, from 29.03 s (=T_FORM_IN=), =animate= blends
the drawn positions and scales into a five-turn spiral, 14 m in front of the
moving camera. It resolves on hook B's downbeat at 30.97 s, holds one beat
through 31.45 s (=T_FORM_OUT=), then releases by =T_LOGO_IN= at 32.67 s.
The 159 surviving shapes in the reference release fit within the frame and
remain separated at the peak. Original body indices assign the slots, leaving
holes where bodies were absorbed instead of jumping survivors into new slots.
Their types, colours and spin remain, and the collision simulation keeps
running on the live orbits beneath this rendering transform.

** Collisions (sim_step)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: collisions-sim-step
:END:

The base motion stays analytic, but a light fixed-timestep simulation
(~SIM_DT = 1/120 s~, advanced by =sim_advance=) keeps a small physics offset per
body (=poff= and =voff=). A damped spring pulls the offset back to zero, so a
body that gets shoved flies off and then eases back to its orbit. From 2 s
after the bang until the collapse, the sim also watches for real
intersections. When two bodies overlap, meaning their centres are closer than
the sum of their radii, it rolls once:

- 25% of the time they combine. One body becomes a new random shape and
  colour, with a 40% pop in scale and a flash of glow, and the other vanishes.
- 25% of the time they repel. They get equal and opposite impulses and fly
  apart, and the spring eases them back toward their orbits.
- The other 50% they pass through each other.

Both bodies then wait 1.6 s before they can roll again, so a contact doesn't
roll every frame. Combines stop once only 105 bodies are left (=MIN_ALIVE=), so
the scene stays full. It's all deterministic, with a fixed timestep and one
seed set by =reseed_loop= at startup, so Scene 1 always plays out the same
collisions. That's also what lets the dev build's scrubber rewind it: going
back, =sim_seek= reseeds the sim and replays it up to the new time (see
[[#engineering-notes][the engineering notes]]).

** Lighting (object.frag)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: lighting-object-frag
:END:

The lighting model is small and analytic, and its output is linear HDR, which
the post stack tonemaps later. It has these terms:

- Hemisphere ambient: =mix(groundColor, skyColor, 0.5 + 0.5*N.y)=, from the
  act's sky colours.
- Light from the glow. The glow round the origin, where the bang happens,
  lights the faces turned toward it. It falls off as cos θ / (2 + r²), with r
  the distance from the origin, at the sky glow's intensity (=uGlow= ×
  =uGlowAmt= × 8). At the bang it lights up the inner faces as the shapes are
  thrown out. Through the hooks it's a faint warm light on the shapes near the
  centre, and it swells again with the collapse's last flare. The seed's faces
  all point away from it, so the seed is untouched.
- Key diffuse: a warm directional light 39° up, whose direction circles once
  every 31 s. It casts shadows ([[#the-key-light-shadow][The key light's shadow]]).
- Blinn-Phong specular with a clear coat: =pow(max(dot(N,H),0), 56) * 0.5= gives
  a broad sheen and =pow(..., 600) * 3= a small hot glint inside it, both
  shadowed. The pastel plastic looks glossy, like candy or a lacquered toy, and
  not like matte clay. The TAA and the bright pass's Karis weights keep the
  glints on small shapes from sparkling.
- Rim: =pow(1 - max(dot(N,V),0), 3)=, tinted by the sky colour.
- Emission, which pushes bright bodies past the bloom threshold on the beat
  ([[#the-instance-colour-codes][The instance colour's codes]]).
- Exponential fog toward the sky's bottom colour.

Scene 1 runs no ambient occlusion and no depth of field. The shapes rarely come
within the occlusion's one-metre reach of each other, and the sky and the grid
write no depth to focus by.

*** The instance colour's codes
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-instance-colour-codes
:END:

The instance's fourth colour channel is a code as well as an amount, and
=object.frag= reads it that way:

- From 0 up it's emission, at 4 × base². The logo's cubes, the end card's
  syllables and the seed use this.
- Below 0, down to -1.6, it's the crowd's softer glow. =build_bodies= gives a
  third of the crowd an emission between -0.8 and -0.3, which =animate= scales
  with the beat and the energy. A combine's flash takes it further, down to
  -1.6, and makes a plain body glow the same way for a moment. =object.frag=
  turns it into 3 × base² × -emission × (0.3 + 0.7 cos θ).
- Anything under -3 is a bubble of air under water. Scene 2's bubbles use -4,
  well clear of the crowd's codes (see
  [[#what-draws-it][What draws it]]).

The base colour is squared so that a glowing pastel keeps its hue through the
tonemap. Squared, the palette's pink (1, 0.42, 0.62) glows as (1, 0.18, 0.38).
So it stays pink instead of burning to a white tile with a tint, and the bloom
carries the colour. The crowd's glow is full in the middle of a face and a
third of that toward the silhouette. That way the key light, the clear coat and
the rim still model the glowing bodies: they glow and bloom on the beat and are
shaded plastic in between.

*** Three materials
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: three-materials
:END:

The crowd has three materials. A quarter of the bodies are tinted glass, a
quarter are iridescent and the rest are pastel plastic. =build_bodies= makes
every other odd-numbered body glass and the rest of the odd ones iridescent.
The even ones, and whatever a combine makes, are plastic, and so are the logo's
and the end card's cubes. The material is carried in the signs of the colour,
set once at load: a negative red means glass and a negative green means
iridescent. That way =animate=, the sim and =object.vert= don't need to know,
and =object.frag= takes the colour's absolute value. Everything else it draws
(Scene 2's eyes and marine snow) is plastic, except Scene 2's bubbles, which
are air (above).

- The iridescence is a thin film over the plastic, about 0.4 µm thick with index
  1.33, like a soap film or a beetle's shell. Light reflected off its two faces
  meets again out of step by 4π n d cos θ_t / λ. So its reflectance, 0.5 - 0.5
  cos of that, comes and goes with the wavelength (650, 550 and 450 nm for the
  three channels) and with the angle. Face on it's magenta, going through gold
  and green to cyan at the edge. It's lifted to 0.3-1 and mixed 60% over the
  body's pastel, so it stays in the palette.
- The glass is drawn last, after the blob, through a copy of the picture so far,
  the way Scene 2's jelly is. The copy is Scene 2's =jelly_copy=, the whole
  frame in RGBA16F on unit 9. The glass reads it along the ray bent into the
  surface (index 1.33), tinted by its pastel more the deeper it looks in
  (Beer-Lambert, exp(-2 (1 - base) cos θ)). It keeps 15% of the plastic's
  light, as if frosted, and mirrors the sky toward its rim by (1 - cos θ)³,
  which is Fresnel's shape. The clear coat's glint and the beat's emission go
  on top.
- It reads the copy 0.7 m × cos θ / (1 + k) along the ray, where k is the
  surface's curvature: one over its radius, from the derivatives of the normal
  and the position. The cos θ makes the edge, which turns the ray fastest, bend
  it least. Dividing by 1 + k keeps the picture behind from being magnified
  past a fold, such as a cone's tip or a box's bevel, which would focus the
  copy's pixels into stair-steps. The distance stays at 0.7 m because k comes
  from derivatives, so it's one value per 2x2 pixel block. The point read jumps
  from block to block, and the jump grows with the distance. At 0.7 m the steps
  stay at a pixel or two, and the torus and the ball still clearly bend the grid
  behind them.
- =s1_shapes= sorts each shape type's glass to the end of its instance list and
  draws instance ranges (=mc_mesh_draw_instance_range=): the solids in the lit
  pass, and the glass after the blob and the copy. When there's no glass, as on
  the end card, no copy is made. On the UHD 620 at 2160 lines the copy, which is
  bound by bandwidth, costs about 5 ms a frame, and the glass's shading about
  2 ms.

*** The key light's shadow
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-key-light-shadow
:END:

The key light casts shadows, so the bodies shade each other, and the end card's
letters do too. Each frame =s1_draw= first draws the shapes again, from the
light, into Scene 2's sun map, =uw_shadow=. Scene 1 and the end card never share
a frame with Scene 2, so the map is free while they run (see
[[#the-renderer-evolves-the-uq-ramp][The renderer evolves]]).
The map is 2048 x 2048 texels over an orthographic 32 m box round the origin,
which makes 1.56 cm texels. That's big enough for the logo's cubes as they fly
in from up to 16 m out. =object.frag= reads it through =keyShadow()=, which
takes nine bilinear depth-compared taps 2 texels apart. Each tap's 2-texel
filter meets the next, so the penumbra, about 9 cm wide, ramps smoothly. 4x4
taps look the same and cost 1.8 ms more on the UHD 620. The taps start from the
point nudged 4 cm off the surface along its normal, so a face doesn't shadow
itself.

In shadow the key light doesn't fall to nothing but to 0.4 × the albedo
squared. So the shade stays the shape's own colour, only deeper, as if the
light had soaked into the plastic. A plain shadow would turn the dawn act's
pinks slate grey.

The glass casts a third of a shadow, which gives a lighter pool on the floor
and a lighter shade on the bodies behind it. The key light's depth pass draws
it separately (pass 3 of =s1_shapes=) through =object.frag=. That shader sees
that the camera is the light (=uViewProj= = =uLightVP=) and returns at once,
after discarding two of every three diagonals of the map's texels.
=keyShadow='s 3x3 taps lie 2 texels apart, so their diagonal offsets, 2 (i + j),
land on each of the three diagonals three times. Wherever they fall, they
average the pattern to exactly a third. A pattern with a period of 4 would
leave a ripple of up to 0.06 that crawls as the light goes round. That's
stripes 2 on and 2 off, or a 2x2 checker, which also gives half a shadow. The
solids keep the plain depth program (=shadowsh=, pass 0).

** The sky (sky.frag)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-sky-sky-frag
:END:

The backdrop is one full-screen pass, drawn first with depth off: a gradient
from the act's bottom colour to its top colour, a glow round the origin made of
two exponentials, and twinkling stars. =far.vert= (the underwater backdrop's
vertex shader) hands it each pixel's view ray, so the sky is fixed in the world
and turns as the camera flies its loop. That also suits the TAA and the motion
blur, which reproject a pixel with no depth as a point on the far plane. The
gradient follows the ray's elevation, from -0.3 to 0.45, so the level logo shot
and the shots looking down on the origin both keep a horizon. The glow follows
the angle from the origin, so it stays round and sits on the seed before the
bang.

The stars are a 3D lattice of cells 1/180 across, cut through by the sphere of
directions. One cell in a hundred is lit, as a soft ball round the cell's
centre, and it looks as wide as the sphere passes near that centre. That gives
stars of many sizes, spread evenly in every direction, a few hundred of them in
any view. Each twinkles at its own phase, and they look the same on screen at
any resolution.

The glow's spread follows its brightness. It's exp(-4 d) at the bang's flare
(brightness over 1.4) and tightens to exp(-8 d) as it calms (0.6 and under). It
goes back to exp(-4 d) for the faint glows under 0.1, the title's and the end
card's. So through the hooks the glow is a warm hotspot in the middle of the
crowd, where one width for everything would lay a milky haze over the whole
frame. In the collapse it's a tighter halo round the shrinking crowd.

*** The dark, the shock front and the outro
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-dark-the-shock-front-and-the-outro
:END:

Scene 1 uses =uAtmo= for three things. It's Scene 2's sky ramp, a field of the
Frame block that Scene 1 has no other use for.

First, before the bang the universe is dark. The whole sky, glow included, sits
at a quarter of its level, so a lone seed with a tight halo hangs in a
near-black void with faint stars. The bang is then a hit from dark to light.

Second, on the bang =uAtmo= runs from 0 to 1 over the 0.4 s after the kick, and
=sky.frag= draws the shock front. That's a ring racing out from the seed's
direction, with a sharp outer edge and a short trail inside. At the front is a
bright band whose rim glows rose white, (4, 2.8, 3) × its strength, the dawn
act's colour. It's the bang's flash, sweeping across the frame. A longer trail
would light the whole inside evenly, as a hard flat disc. As it passes, the
ring bends the sky like a lens, pulling each ray in toward the seed. So the
origin's glow is dragged out into the rim and the stars swim through it. It
weakens as it grows and leaves the frame in about 0.2 s. Behind it the light
ramps back to full over 0.25 rad. Ahead of it the dark only creeps up (0.6 ×
=uAtmo=), so outside the ring the light only ever grows, and =floor(uAtmo)=
holds it at full from then on. The origin's glow flares for 0.8 s after the
kick. Its wide halo lasts about 0.15 s, and the crowd's colours show within
half a second of the kick.

Third, over the outro =uAtmo= runs from 1 to 2 in the 3 s before =T_BLACK=
(46.2-49.2 s). The sky, its glow and its stars go down to black with it, while
the seed, which =object.frag= draws, goes on shining alone. The world goes dark
round the star the camera has backed away to
([[#the-catmull-rom-camera][The Catmull-Rom camera]]). At 1 nothing changes,
and the end card sets that value itself ([[#the-end-card][The end card]]).

*** The finale's sun
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-finale-sun
:END:

Behind the title and the end card a synthwave sun stands on the horizon,
directly behind the origin from wherever the camera is. It's a disc 0.4 rad
(23°) across, gold at the top and hot pink at the bottom. Its lower part is cut
by bars that thicken toward the horizon, which hides the part that has set. The
bars are a quarter of a radius apart, each with a gap of 0.5 × (0.6 - y) of
that, with y going up the disc from -1 to 1. A pink haze lies along the horizon
and round the disc. The sun is blended over the sky at about the sky's own
level, not added, because added it would wash out to a pale peach in the
shoulder of the filmic tonemap. The floor (=ground.frag=) reflects it the way a
wet road would, as a narrow streak running down from the horizon under it.

The sun's height over the horizon and its presence travel in =uSun=, which no
Scene 1 shader has another use for. =render_scene1= ties both to the title's
presence (=lv=, which also dims the glow). As the cubes gather, the sun's centre
rises 0.32, from 0.24 below the horizon to 0.08 above it, and it sets again as
they scatter. No act before the finale sees it or its haze. The end card sets it
over its five bars, from 0.1 to -0.14, under its fade. The finale's sky above
the horizon (=SEQ_TOP=) is a deep violet, (0.40, 0.20, 0.85), so the pastel
letters and the blob stand out against it.

** The additive synthwave grid (ground.frag)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-additive-synthwave-grid-ground-frag
:END:

The grid is drawn with additive blending (=GL_ONE, GL_ONE=). Between the lines
it's black, which adds nothing, so only the lines glow and the void shows
through around them. A fine and a coarse set of lines, 2 m and 10 m apart, are
anti-aliased with =fwidth=, pulsed by a travelling sine and faded with
distance. The grid is depth-tested, so bodies hide it, but it's drawn with
depth writes off, so it never hides them. It lies 4.5 m below the origin. It
fades in over the 6 s from 2 s after the bang, scaled by the energy, and out
again through the collapse.

The floor takes the key light's shadow too. Under the crowd the grid lines dim
and the floor itself darkens, as though it had been catching the light. The
additive pass adds a negative amount there, -0.6 × =uBottom= × (1 - shadow),
which works because the HDR target is float and its blending is unclamped. Soft
pools gather under the shapes and drift with them, and on the end card the
letters' shadow lies on the floor behind them. Bodies below the floor are
farther from the light than the floor is, so they cast nothing on it, and the
floor isn't a caster, so it can't shade itself. The lookup is =keyShadow()=,
written identically in =object.frag= and =ground.frag= so that it packs once.

The bang's shock front runs out over the floor before the grid fades in. It's
driven by =uAtmo= going from 0 to 1 over 0.4 s, as in the sky
([[#the-dark-the-shock-front-and-the-outro][The dark, the shock front and the outro]]).
The lines light up behind a front racing out to 60 m. They're brightest at the
front and dimmer behind it (e^(0.12 x), with x in metres from the front,
negative behind it), and the whole flare fades as the front slows. So the grid
is first seen as the shock runs over it out to the horizon. =s1_draw= draws the
floor while the shock is under way and skips the shadow taps while
=uGridFade= is still 0. For those 0.4 s the frame costs what the floor costs
from 9.7 s on, about 5 ms more on the UHD 620 at 2160 lines.

** The Catmull-Rom camera
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-catmull-rom-camera
:END:

=camera_at(t)= samples a looping Catmull-Rom path through 8 control points
(~mc_spline_sample(..., loop=1)~), picked by hand at varied radius and height.
It goes once round the loop over the scene, on the warped clock. The path
scales out from a tight framing on the dot (0.35 of its size) as the universe
expands, and it bobs gently, while the field of view breathes between 51° and
55°. For the finale the camera blends (=lerp=) the eye and target toward a fixed
head-on view of the logo plane and then lets go back to the fly-through. The
blend eases in and out with =smoothstep= around =T_LOGO_IN= and =T_LOGO_OUT=.
The view is aimed 1 m above the middle of the logo, so the title sits a little
below the centre of the frame. The blob floats in the sky above it and the grid
lies below.

Through the collapse, from =T_COLLAPSE= to =T_BLACK= with a smoothstep, the
camera eases from its flight to a second fixed pose. The eye is 25 m out,
looking about 15° down past the seed, so that the seed ends up where Scene 2's
sun stands on the screen (=uSunUV=, 0.68, 0.72). Over the last second it stays
within 3 px of that at 1080p. The crowd balls up into the seed as the camera
backs away, and the seed ends as a star about 15 px across. That's as wide as
the disc of the sun that rises in its place (see
[[#scene-2-from-orbit-to-the-deep][Scene 2]]).

On the bang the camera's aim jolts. A damped pair of sines (55 and 70 rad/s,
e^(-7 t)) knocks the target a few degrees off, upward first, and it shudders
back over half a second. Knocking the aim moves the seed in the frame, so the
jolt shows. A shake of the eye round a target fixed on the seed would keep the
seed still and go unnoticed.

** Exposure, bloom and fades
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: hdr-bloom-a-filmic-tonemap-and-taa
:END:

The engine's renderer finishes the picture with an HDR target, bloom, a filmic
tonemap, a grade, TAA and grain (see
[[file:architecture.org::#the-renderer-and-its-post-stack][The renderer and its post stack]]).
Scene 1 and the end card render at 2160 lines, 200% of a 1080p picture, for
the reasons in [[#engineering-notes][the engineering notes]].
=render_scene1= moves a few of the post's settings over time:

- The bang is an exposure kick of +1.5 before the tonemap, falling off as
  e^(-12 t). So the seed and the burst flare up, while the dark lifts only as
  the shock front runs out through the sky and over the floor. The flash is the
  shock's rim sweeping the frame. A white flash after the tonemap would lay a
  flat grey veil over the dark for a few frames before the ring brought the
  light.
- The bloom is high through the intro (0.32, against 0.07-0.11 in the hooks)
  and falls to its running level over the second after the kick. Through the
  hooks the bloom and the exposure follow the energy and the beat.
- The master fade (=post.fade=) brings the picture up over the first 2.5 s. At
  the end it holds until a beat after =T_BLACK= and then dims the star, to
  about a quarter by the cut at 50.3 s.
- While the blob is present it lifts the bloom a little and softens the
  vignette.

** The 5x7-font logo
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-5-7-font-logo
:END:

The title is laid out from a small built-in bitmap font: eleven glyphs (=FONT=),
each 7 rows of 5 bits. =M I C R O S= are for the title and =H E L W D= for the
end card's greeting. =build_text()= walks the letters of ="MICROCOSM"=, and
after it the end card's three lines ([[#the-card-words][The card's words]]). It
emits one cube target per lit pixel, 142 for the title. Each is paired with a
scattered origin 8-16 m out that the cube flies in from, and takes its colour
from the palette by its letter and column. From =T_LOGO_IN= (32.7 s) each cube
eases from its start to its place in the grid with a per-index stagger,
stopping its tumble and growing as it lands. Meanwhile the crowd shrinks away to
give the letters room. The cubes are more instances of the rounded-box mesh,
drawn in the same instanced pass. From =T_LOGO_OUT= (43.0 s) they fly back out
over 3 s.

When the title is complete, on the downbeat of bar 19 (36.8 s), a light sweeps
across it in about a beat, left to right on a slant. It's a band of x + y/2
moving at 24 m a second. Each cube it passes flares (emission +7 at the middle
of the band) and hops 12 cm, which marks the finished title before the crowd
returns. While the title stands, the letters glow at 0.6 (4 × base² × 0.6).
That's low enough for them to glow with a halo and keep their colours, and for
the sweep to stand out. Much brighter, they bloom to near white.

** The blue blob
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-blue-blob
:END:

A friendly round blob drifts in the background. It's a smooth ball that
=blob.vert= makes from =gl_VertexID=: a cube with 48x48 cells on each face
(82,944 vertices, the same count as the planet), pushed out onto a sphere. The
end card's arms need that many cells, and elsewhere they only smooth the
outline. It wobbles along its normal by a 3D noise field, scaled by =uWobble=
(1 in Scene 1), and breathes a little. Its own small shader draws it
(=blob.frag=). Its colour is the first of =BLOB_COLORS=, blue (0.15, 0.40, 1.0).
In Scene 1 it's a deep, glossy blue, brightest where it faces us and darker
toward its edge (0.25 + 0.65 cos θ), so that the round body and its lumps show.
Its crown catches the bright window of the sky above as a soft glint.

Its eyes are two dark flattened spheres (=blob_eyes=), set low on its face, 0.37
of its radius up and 0.96 out, clear of the lumps. Each has a white catchlight,
a small bead sunk into the front of the eye, up and to the left. Only its cap
shows, so it stays on the eye from any side. The eyes are instances of the ball
in the shapes' lists, so whatever is in front of the blob hides them properly.
The title's blob, the blob in Scene 2's deep and the one on the end card all
have these eyes. =blob_eyes= takes a size: 1.2 on the title and the card, and
up to 1.11 in the deep.

A shut eye is a dark lash line with a faint rim. As the eye squashes down to a
sliver, a lid grows just behind its front. The lid is as wide as the eye and
(1 - openness) × 0.22 of it high, in a blue (0.25, 0.42, 0.75) that doesn't
glow, darker than the body. The eye, its lid and its catchlight are drawn in
one loop. In the deep, a bare sliver would be lost among the jelly's dark
caustic patches, and a pale lid would look like the white slits of a visor.

It appears three times (=blob_presence=), each more strongly than the last. It
gives a faint glimpse as hook A arrives (14.0-17.0 s), drifts through the
breakdown (23.7-30.2 s) and, most of all, floats above the MICROCOSM logo in the
finale (33.0-43.0 s). Its eyes show only in the last two. At =T_LOGO_IN=
(32.7 s), while it's out of sight, it moves 12.5 m higher and 14 m further back.
So it looks a little smaller, and it sits in the sky over the title with its
wink in full view, instead of glowing behind the letters and washing them out.
It fades in with =GL_CONSTANT_ALPHA= blending at its presence × 1.15 and is
nearly opaque at its fullest (0.98), so the sky doesn't grey it. It stays
depth-tested, so near shapes hide it. While it's present it lifts the grade a
little, with a bit more bloom and a slightly softer vignette.

Near the end of the finale, at =T_WINK= (39.4 s), the blob winks: its right eye
closes under its lid and opens again over half a second while the left stays
open. It comes back, the same blue, in the deep of Scene 2, where it has no
face until the scare is over (see
[[#scene-2-from-orbit-to-the-deep][Scene 2]]).

** Two frames, a flash and a waterline
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: two-frames-a-flash-and-a-waterline
:END:

Landing a camera on a sphere and then standing it on a flat sea takes two
different coordinate systems, and joining them properly is a lot of trouble, so
the demo doesn't. It runs two separate frames and cuts between them on a flash.
Up to the cloud break it's a planet frame, a ball scaled to =PLANET_R= at the
origin. The camera dives in from about 150 units out to 2.5 units above the
surface, and the lens widens on the plunge. After that it's a flat-sea frame, a
plane at =y = 0=, for the fall to the water and everything under it. A Gaussian
flash in the post (=cloudFlash=) covers the switch. It goes fully white for the
frame of the cut, so neither frame shows across it.

The fall (=camera_sea=) starts at 55 m and slows as it nears the water. Its
height is 55 (1 - u)(1 - 0.74 u), never under 12 cm. It levels out, pitching
from -41° to -7°, and glides forward, reaching about 8 m/s at the end. Over its
last 6 m it rides the swell. =sea_level= evaluates =ocean.vert='s sines at the
two grid vertices on the line the camera falls along (x = 0) and blends them the
way the rasterised surface does. So the lens follows the surface that's actually
drawn there, and it's a hand's breadth above it as it goes under. Above a mean
sea level instead, the lens would dip in and out of crests that rise more than
2 m, and the glints would strobe.

The crossing takes 0.17 s either side of =S2_BREACH= (=S2_DIP=), with no cut.
Each of those frames draws the sea above (the sky and the ocean), copies it to
texture unit 9 (=jelly_copy=, the whole frame), clears, and draws the sea below.
The underwater act is held at its first moment until the breach. Then =dip.frag=
lays the sea above back over the picture, above a wavy waterline that sweeps up
the lens. A bright film of light runs along the line, with the meniscus's
shadowed lip just under it. Near the line the image above is lifted a little,
and the underwater grade comes in with it. The frame's first camera, the one the
TAA's history and the motion blur follow, is the underwater one, so that it
matches the depth buffer. Drawing two seas costs 1.1-1.7 ms more for those
0.34 s, which still keeps them under 11.2 ms. Over the fall and the first
seconds under, the mean luma never changes by more than about 5 levels (out of
255) from one frame to the next.

The copy's texture (=jelly_tex=, RGBA16F, 37 MB at 2880x1620) is made again
whenever the renderer's size has changed. The size changes at the cut, from
Scene 1's 200% to Scene 2's 150%. So =render_scene2= checks the size on
Scene 2's first frame and makes the texture again there, where the picture is
near-black (the fade-up) and the renderer is resizing anyway. Otherwise the
texture would be made at its first use, the dip, and that frame would stall in
full view.

Going under (=camera_uw=), the lens enters level and 6.5 m higher than its
place, in the brighter, clearer water near the surface. Over 3.5 s it sinks into
its place behind the blob as the water darkens. The 40 bubbles from its entry
are anchored in the water where it came in, and they rise past and away over
about 3 s. They're beads of air like the blob's own (see
[[#bubbles-and-marine-snow][Bubbles and marine snow]]), 0.7-1.7 cm across for
every metre they are from the lens. So going under looks like a burst of silver instead of empty
teal.

** The underwater camera
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-underwater-camera
:END:

=camera_uw= is the picture's camera. It's =camera_at2='s trail, reframed
(below), plus the dive's entry (above), the glance up at the whale (see
[[#the-water-light][The water's light]]) and the flight's chase. The seabed's
props are placed and timed against this camera too (see
[[#the-seabed-and-its-props-uwplace-comp][The seabed and its props]]), so the
boulders keep out of its way and every prop grows in out of its sight.

The trail (=camera_at2=, under the water) follows the blob 8-14 m back and 3 m
above it, along its level heading. =blob_frame= gives that heading as the
direction to where the blob will be 0.12 s later, with the height dropped. The
level heading matters where the blob all but stops, at the far end of a loop in
its path. There its full heading would be mostly its gentle bob, and the trail
would rise high above it and look steeply down. Kept level, it stays low, and
the whale leaves the frame whole, by its right edge. =camera_uw= reframes the
trail in three ways:

- Until the encounter draws near, the camera stands 4 m further back and is
  turned 17° left and 3.4° up. That puts the blob low in the right third, with
  the seabed and the open water (and the whale's pass) beside and above it. It
  eases back onto the trail over story seconds 44-48 (=ENC_IN= - 12 to
  =ENC_IN= - 8, 107.1-110.4 s of the show). Trailed dead centre and half the
  frame high, the blob would be a pale, faceless lump in murk for the act's
  first 20 s.
- Through the transformation (story seconds 72-84, 129.1-139.7 s) the camera
  orbits the blob. The orbit is blended in around the blob in bearing (the
  short way round), horizontal distance and height, so the camera cranes down
  and in from the chase shot, from 17.5 to 8.9 m. It sweeps round to the
  bearing the trail takes at =TRANS_END=, going whatever part of a lap that
  takes plus half a lap, wrapped into 0.5-1.5 laps (0.88 here). It eases in
  and out (smoothstep), so the hand-back to the flight is the same blend
  undone. The view turns at most 0.85° a frame at 60 fps. A straight-line blend
  toward an orbiting point would cut a chord through the swing and swoop in
  until the blob filled the frame. With a lap or more, the reef would whirl
  past. A wider orbit on a narrower lens wouldn't slow the background, because
  the background turns with the view and a narrower lens spreads each degree
  over more pixels.
- From =FLIGHT_IN= + 2 (story second 86, 141.1 s of the show), over 3 story
  seconds, the flight's camera eases down into a chase. It sits 11 m behind the
  blob along its path and 3 m above it, 75° wide, looking down past the blob at
  the reef 2.2 m under it. The direction back is the mean direction to the
  points 8-16 m back along the path by arc length (in 0.03 s steps). So the
  distance never changes (11.40 m), and a bend or a burst of speed turns the
  view smoothly (at most 2.35° a frame at 60 fps). A single point a fixed
  straight-line distance back would jump where the path bends. The camera
  swings 23° to one side and then the other, on the same sway that the blob's
  glances back follow (=sin(0.4 u + 0.5)=). So we see the blob 3/4 from
  behind, with its eyes showing. On the path the camera stays inside the tube
  the props are cleared from, and the big school parts round it. The kelp, the
  coral heads and the school stream past close below and beside it. The image
  motion builds up into the school's parting, from 6 to 19 px a frame at
  960x540.

Over the flight's first 5 story seconds (139.7-143.4 s), the reach of the camera
motion blur opens from 0.25% of the screen each way to 1% (=mc_post.blur=). The
close kelp and coral cross the frame at 30-45 px a frame at 60 Hz. With only
about 5 px of blur they would keep sharp edges and step from frame to frame, and
with the wider reach their edges smear along the motion. That costs about 2 ms
more in the composite there. The shutter is 120°, since a wider one smears the
school that swims along with the camera. The shutter limits the blur more
than the reach does, so the near seabed still strobes a little at about
151-152 s.

We checked every 5 ms of the act against every prop, every fish and a CPU copy
of the big school. Nothing comes within 2.77 m of the lens (the closest is a
circling fish, during the stalk), and nothing of the big school within 5 m.

** What draws it
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-draws-it
:END:

Scene 2 has its own shaders, all of them procedural, and genshaders bakes them
in like the rest. =dip.frag= draws the crossing's waterline (see
[[#two-frames-a-flash-and-a-waterline][Two frames, a flash and a waterline]]).
The others are described below, from space down to the reef, followed by the
blob, its bubbles, the moon jellies and the placement of the seabed's props.

*** Space and the sky
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: space-and-the-sky
:END:

=space.frag= (full-screen, sharing =sky.vert=) draws deep space and cross-fades
it to a blue sky as its =uAtmo= climbs, so one shader carries us from orbit down
through the atmosphere. Space is near black, with a field of steady stars: one
cell in 40 of a grid 200 cells to the screen's height holds a star. A star can
sit anywhere in its cell. Its brightness is a steep power of a hash, so most are
faint and about 2% are bright, and its colour runs from red-orange to
blue-white. The stars don't twinkle, since there's no air to twinkle through.
One cell in 30 of a grid eight times coarser holds a brighter star, with a glow
and the lens's four spikes. The sun is a sharp disc 14 px across at 1080p,
darker toward its limb. It sits in a tight glare that falls off with the square
of the angle, plus a faint veiling glow. Those distances are measured in screen
heights, so the glare is round.

A faint band of the galaxy crosses the sky diagonally behind the planet, clear
of the sun. Its unresolved starlight is an fbm stretched along the band, warm
white at the heart and bluer at the edges. A finer fbm, stretched 4:1 along the
band, splits it down the middle with dark lanes of dust, and more stars crowd
it, one cell in 15 at its heart. Knots of faint pink glow along it, where its
gas is lit. At its brightest it's only 0.05, so it shows as structure without
fogging the sky, and it fades with the rest of space as the sky turns blue. The
band is separate code and could be dropped on its own.

On the plunge the sky is laid out on the screen, pale at the horizon and deeper
blue overhead. From the cloud break on (=floor(uAtmo)= = 1, under the
white-out), it's laid out by the direction each pixel looks, through
=inverse(uViewProj)=. That's =mix(uSky, deep blue, sqrt(3 × elevation))=, a
pale band at the horizon and blue from about 20° up. Its glow sits round the
true sun (=uSun=), up to the left above the glitter path, and the sea mirrors
the same sky (see [[#the-sea-from-above][The sea from above]]). So the glow
stays put as the camera pitches, and sky and sea meet at the horizon in one
colour.

*** The planet, its air and its moon
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-planet-its-air-and-its-moon
:END:

=planet.vert= makes the planet from =gl_VertexID=: 48x48 cells on each face of
a cube, pushed out onto a sphere. In =planet.frag=, continents, oceans, ice caps
and drifting cloud are all 3D fbm noise over the object-space direction. That's
six octaves of value noise, each turned as well as scaled so that the lattice
never lines up. Each octave is one fetch from the demo's 32³ lattice of random
bytes. That lattice is =vn3=, the value noise every shader shares (see
[[#engineering-notes][Engineering notes]]). One broad field
warps the rest. That gives coasts that wander and break into islands, a deep
ocean with a paler shelf along the coasts, forest or desert where the broad
field says wet or dry, and bare uplands. The ice caps have a ragged edge and
reach further down over land than over the deep sea.

The cloud streams in bands. Its field is squeezed north-south, pushed by the
land's two fields, swirled into fronts and eddies by two fbms of its own, frayed
by one six times finer and cut by a narrow step. It drifts, shades the ground
under it (it's read again a step toward the sun) and is darker on its own side
away from the sun. The cloud's three fbm fields cost about 2 ms at 1080p as the
planet fills the frame.

The sun lights the planet from the backdrop sun's side, about 68° off the eye
(=uSun= × (1.7, 1.5, 0.1)), so in space a third of the disc is night behind a
soft terminator. On the plunge the sun swings round behind the eye with
=uAtmo=, so no night side hangs in the blue sky and we fall into a sunny noon.

The air is Rayleigh scattering plus ozone, in closed form. =air(s)= is sunlight
reddened over an air mass of 0.1 / (s + 0.1)². Rayleigh takes blue first, and
the ozone's Chappuis band takes a fifth to a quarter more of the green and red.
The extinction is (0.0073, 0.0176, 0.033) where Rayleigh's alone is (0.006,
0.014, 0.033), the column ratios of Bruneton's 2017 model. The light is white
overhead, faintly warm at 30°, orange low down, red at the terminator and dark
past it, and it lights the ground. Without the ozone, the low sun would turn
yellow and then green against the air's bluish scatter, one more band of
rainbow on the way down.

The air between the ground and the eye dims the ground by its transmittance
along the view, e^(-τ / n·v), where τ is the Rayleigh depth straight up. It also
adds the light it scatters on the way. That light is lit by =air()= at the
ground's sun, but it takes only half the tint: (A + A.r) / 2. The other half is
the red's strength as a grey, which is how the sky's own light would look once
scattered again. The result is a blue haze that thickens to a pale limb,
faintly warm at the terminator and gone at night. Red is the last channel to go,
so the glow fades out where the sunlight does. The full tint would go wrong near
the limb, where the scattering saturates every channel. There the air would send
out the sun's own colour at each height of the sun, and =air()= runs through the
whole sunset within about 0.2 of the sun's cosine. So each end of the terminator
would wear a saturated rainbow sliver a few pixels wide. With the half tint the
rim fades from the blue-white limb through a faint peach and lavender tip into
the night.

Only the sea has a sheen, broad and faint and weighted by Schlick's Fresnel.
It's a Blinn lobe of exponent 60, scaled by 5, to match the 0.17 rms slopes of
wind-roughened water (Cox and Munk's figure at 5 m/s). A tighter, brighter lobe
looks like plastic. Where there's no cloud, the land's height above the sea
bends the normal the ground is lit by, through its screen-space slope. That's
the surface gradient, taken from the fine derivatives, because the coarse ones
show 2x2 blocks. Mountain ranges catch the sun on one side and fall into shade
on the other, most of all near the terminator, while the air keeps the smooth
normal. The footprint's area under that slope is floored at 10⁻⁹. Edge-on at
the limb it reaches 0 over a sea whose slope is 0 too, and the TAA's history has
no NaN guard, so it would keep the 0/0.

=atmo.frag= (sharing =planet.vert=) draws the same sphere 6% larger and adds it
on with the front faces culled, so it only shades the ring round the disc. Each
pixel's ray scatters 1 - e^(-t) of the sunlight, where t is the Rayleigh depth
along the ray. That depth falls off exponentially with how high the ray passes
over the ground, so the halo hugs the limb. It's 66 τ e^(-50 h), with h in
shell radii, matched to the disc's limb. Near the limb the depth saturates every
colour and gives a pale band. Higher up it saturates only blue, which leaves a
blue fringe fading to black. The light it scatters is =air()= where the ray
passes, with half the tint as on the disc. It's white on the day side, a faint
peach at the terminator's two ends and nothing on the night side. Its sun turns
with the disc's, so the two meet at the limb.

In the planet frame the post's lateral chromatic aberration scales as
(1 - =uAtmo=)², so it's gone where the sky is blue and unchanged in space, over
the sea and under it. At full strength it would pull red from the bright sky
into the edge pixels of the planet's dark limb, as a red line a pixel wide.

The moon (=moon.frag=, drawn on =planet.vert='s ball) is a fifth of the planet's
size. It sits 65 units out toward where the camera comes from, and 23 units off
the camera's line of approach. As the camera drifts in, the moon hangs low to
the right of the planet and slides outward against it, which is the shot's only
parallax, since the stars are at infinity. When the plunge passes it, the moon
swells and sweeps out past the right edge, never coming nearer the lens than
23 units. Its surface is dust over old lava plains: broad darker seas and
mottled highlands, with a gentle relief taken from the mottling's own field.
From this far the moon shows mostly through its albedo. A relief strong enough
to read as rock makes it look like an asteroid, and the post's colour fringes
run purple round it. It's lit by the planet's sun, turned the way =planet.frag=
turns it, so the two show the same phase. With no air, the terminator is hard.

*** The sea from above
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-sea-from-above
:END:

=ocean.vert= / =ocean.frag= draw the sea surface. =ocean.vert= makes a 900 m
plane of 140x140 cells from =gl_VertexID= and raises it into a swell with a sum
of four directional sines, and =ocean.frag= sums the same sines again per pixel,
with their slopes. On top it adds five finer octaves of chop, each turned 2.4
rad from the last at 1.9 times its frequency and moving at deep-water speed,
and a value-noise cat's-paw on the slope. The finest two octaves make the
ripples close up. So near the eye the surface is smooth and lively, instead of
showing the grid's 6 m facets.

Each of these layers fades out by =fwidth= where it would alias. The cat's-paw's
0.6 m cells fade between 0.25 and 0.8 cells a pixel. Any later, and through the
fall's mid-distance they'd be 1-2 px across and sparkle like grains of salt. The
variance of a faded octave's slopes, which is how far it would have tilted the
surface, goes into the roughness instead (LEAN / Toksvig-style normal
filtering). The sun's glint is GGX: D F / (4 n·v) n·l, with α² = 0.64 r over a
base of 0.003 and Schlick's Fresnel at the half vector. With the filtering it
gives a sharp sparkle up close, a smooth glitter path far off and clear ripple
highlights in the skim.

The sea reflects the sky by Schlick's Fresnel (F0 0.02). It's the same sky
=space.frag= draws behind it, glow and all, so the sea brightens round the
glitter path. From 200 to 440 m the far sea melts into the haze at the horizon.
Sky and sea meet in one colour before the edge of the 900 m plane can cut the
sky in a line. The water's body runs from deep blue troughs to turquoise crests
that the light shines through, more so looking toward the sun.

Foam breaks only on the higher crests (1.4-1.7) and thins out inside them, its
opacity ramping over 0.45-0.75 of its noise. A finer octave frays it into lace,
and it's torn into streaks along the wind, in broken patches a metre or two
across. Its cells are about 2.9 by 0.8 m and drift downwind. Much larger cells
give one soft blob per crest, like paint. The noise evens out where it would
shrink under a pixel. Inside, foam is bubbles. A fourth octave of its noise,
round and about 15 cm across, drifts with the foam and crowds and thins it. It
uses two lattices turned against each other, because one alone shows square
cells that look like pixels. The foam is brighter where the bubbles are packed,
the water shows through where they're thin, and they fray its lace edges too.
This octave also evens out where it would shrink under a pixel. The slope the
foam lies on, the chop's normal, turns it toward the sun or away. The foam's
bubbles cost about 1.4 ms a frame over the fall.

*** The water from below
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-water-from-below
:END:

=water.frag= is the underwater backdrop: the water along each pixel's view ray
(see [[#the-water-light][The water's light]]). It's full-screen, and =far.vert=
puts it on the far plane and hands it that ray. The light it scatters brightens
toward the surface and toward the sun. Shafts cross it, the caustic web's light
carried down as sheets. The ray samples them six times, crowded into the near
metres. They're sharp near the surface and blur with depth: the contrast is 1
at the surface and 0.75 at 10 m, and never drops under 0.4. They sway with the
swell and fan down from above the frame. The shafts hang from the eye's path,
smoothed over the half second around now, and give way to a broader, softer
pattern when the eye flies or swings fast.

Looking up, the ray meets the surface from below. Inside Snell's window the
whole sky and the sun are squeezed into a disc 97° across. Its rim splits into a
thin rainbow, because each colour has its own critical angle (n 1.331 / 1.334 /
1.340). Outside the window, total internal reflection mirrors the dark water.
Eight ripples on a geometric spectrum break both into moving facets, whose
curvature focuses and spreads the light through each one. Each ripple fades out
before it gets too fine for a pixel to hold. When the eye moves fast the rim
softens, and the facets and the sun's hot spot calm down along with the shafts.

Specks of particulate drift in the water, fixed to their view directions. One
cell in 25 holds a speck, each with its own size up to half a cell, and the
larger ones are dimmer. So the small ones are sharp points and the large ones
soft, dim discs, as if they were nearer and out of focus. Specks of one size and
brightness would look like a stencil laid over the open water.

*** Sand and boulders
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sand-and-boulders
:END:

=uwsurf.frag= (with =object.vert= for the seabed and =rock.vert= for the
boulders) shades the underwater surfaces. A per-instance material id picks the
look: baked sand or rock textures, sampled triplanar, or a live procedural rock
colour. So the scene mixes textured and procedural surfaces. The sampling is
really biplanar: of the three planes it skips the one the surface faces least,
fetch and all. It runs the shared physically based model of GGX, analytic IBL,
detail normals and the water's extinction, with the caustic web folded into the
sun's radiance (see [[#the-renderer-evolves-the-uq-ramp][The renderer evolves
(the uQ ramp)]]). The seabed's base sand breaks up its tiling with =tnt()=, as
the beach does in Scene 3. A second ramp, =uReal=, layers finer detail textures
over the base, a 4x octave and then, as the ramp nears its end (=uReal=²), an
11x micro-grain, while =uQ= blends the whole thing up from a flat early look.
Both ramps are complete by story second 48 (110.4 s), so the floor and the rocks
gain a lot of definition over the act's first half.

The rock is a warm grey stone (0.20-0.50 baked, 0.16-0.42 procedural,
brown-grey). Silt has settled on its upper faces as a pale, sandy film, deepest
in the hollows of its relief, so the rocks belong to the floor they sit on. The
silt is a constant colour, since at these sizes a silt textured like the sand
looks no different. Underneath, the rocks are lifted by the sand's light bounced
back up, which the analytic environment carries (see
[[#the-renderer-evolves-the-uq-ramp][The renderer evolves]]). =uwsurf.frag= also
lights the seabed and the boulders with the jelly's caustic (see
[[#the-jelly-blob-frag][The jelly]]).

Where the coral patches, the landmark reefs and the flight's last coral stand,
the floor itself turns to reef bed, so the heads sit in one reef instead of on
pale sand. For each 3.5 m square of the sand, the placement leaves two things:
how much bed the coral heads around it lay down, and the time from which it may
grow in (see [[#the-seabed-and-its-props-uwplace-comp][The seabed and its
props]]). The sand blends the bed of the four nearest squares
bilinearly, up to 1, and grows it in over 4 story seconds. Two noise octaves
fray its edge into the sand, because with one, a straight 3.5 m edge shows. The
bed is rubble and crusts of growth, mottled ochre, olive and dusty rose and
darker in its crevices, with a low relief of its own through the detail normal.
Through the flight it costs 2.7 to 4.3 ms a frame at 150% on the UHD 620, for
the sand's four fetches and five noises a pixel.

=rock.vert= (which feeds =uwsurf.frag=) gives the boulders progressive geometry.
Three octaves of crags are baked into the rock at load by =uwplace.comp=, and
the mesh's indices pick its vertices from that buffer. On top of those, a
per-instance 3D-noise displacement grows along the surface normal as =uReal=
rises, and its higher octaves fade in later. The rocks gain crags and fine
relief as the scene goes on. A three-tap finite difference rederives the normal
from the noise gradient, so the new geometry is lit correctly. Each boulder is
also squashed and stretched per axis (0.7-1.1, seeded by its position) and
dented by one broad noise octave that only ever pushes inward, so no two share a
silhouette. Because the placement leaves headroom for this growth, the rocks
still never touch anything. The shadow pass uses the same vertex shader, which
keeps the shadows matched.

Each boulder darkens toward its foot, where it's bedded in the sand. Its centre
stands 0.35 of its scale above the sand (=uwplace.comp='s seat), and a vertex's
tint runs from 0.3 at 0.45 of the scale below the centre to 1 at 0.25 above.
That's the shade, silt and wet where the rock meets the sand, which the GTAO,
with a metre's reach at half resolution, barely draws under a convex rock. The
tint rides in =vColor=, the albedo tint =uwsurf.frag= already applies, so the
shadow pass is unaffected.

The sand darkens round each boulder's foot as well. For the sand only
(=uSandOnly=), =uwsurf.frag= walks its square's list. For each boulder that's
born, grown in and not yet overgrown by a landmark, it darkens all of the sand's
light by up to half. How much depends on how near the point lies in the
boulder's own frame. To work in that frame it undoes the boulder's turn
(Rodrigues) and divides out its size and =rock.vert='s squash, so the shade is
an ellipsoid that hugs the rock's outline. It's full at 0.6 of the rock's size,
which is always inside the rock, and fades smoothly to nothing by 1.8, as
1 - smoothstep(0.6, 1.8, h). On the side away from the sun, where the cast
shadow already darkens the sand, it's up to 15% tighter. Without it the rocks
would look set down on the sand instead of bedded in it. The foot's tint stops
at the rock's own edge, and the cast shadow lies on one side only and fades out
past 35-40 m. With it each rock sits in a soft skirt, near and far, that follows
the caustic web and doesn't cut across it. The shade dims all of the sand's
light, because dimming the ambient alone blackens the web's dark cells beside a
rock while its bright lines stay. Rock on rock, coral and weed get no contact
shade. The sand's shade costs 0.6 to 2.8 ms a frame at 150% on the UHD 620.

=uwbake.frag= is the material baker. Once at load it renders four tiling
textures into repeat-wrapped, mipmapped 2D textures: sand and rock for the
seabed, plus a fish-scale skin and a whale skin. The two skins are sampled
through real mesh UVs (below). The sand and rock are filtered 4x
anisotropically, like Scene 3's tiles, so the floor and the rocks stay sharp
where they slope away. The skins aren't, because on the scales it only sharpens
the moiré (see [[#fish-and-the-big-school][Fish and the big school]]).

*** The animals' meshes
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-animals-meshes
:END:

The fish, the crabs, the predator and the whale are all sculpted at load by the
creature kit that also makes Scene 3's tyrannosaurus (=jr.comp=, described in
[[#the-tyrannosaurus-s3-rex-c-jr][The tyrannosaurus]]). Each is
a smooth union of round cones, ellipsoids and flat fins in its rest pose. The
fish's body is an ellipsoid whose section tapers toward nose and tail, and its
fins are thin triangles. The whale's body is a loft, section by section. The
predator's body is cut off at the hinges of its jaws, and each jaw is sculpted
shut and then turned open to the rest gape =fish.vert= assumes (see
[[#the-predator][The predator]]). Each animal is meshed by surface nets on a grid
of its own. The cells are 0.06 for a fish, 0.07 for a crab, 0.015 for the
predator and 0.014 for the whale, in model units, which the instances scale.

They all go into one shared vertex buffer. Each vertex carries the two bones
that hold it, from among the body, the upper and lower jaws, their fangs and the
fins. On the predator it also carries a mark for whatever lies inside the mouth.
That's about 3,100 triangles a fish, 3,200 a crab, 22,000 for the whale and
60,000 for the predator. =fish.vert= poses all four, reading that buffer by
=gl_VertexID= in the usual instance batches, and a per-draw flag tells it which
animal it's posing. The fish and crabs swim with a wag that travels down the
body, the predator works its jaws as well, and the whale beats up and down.

*** Fish and the big school
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: fish-and-the-big-school
:END:

=fish.vert= / =fish.frag= draw the fish. The body tapers to points at the nose
(+Z) and the tail (-Z), and a forked tail, a dorsal and two pectorals give it a
proper silhouette. The vertex shader wags the tail with a sine wave down the
body. The fish swim in circling schools, seated at even angular spacing on the
ring so that school-mates never collide. They point nose-forward along their
direction of travel (the yaw is =atan2= of a one-step look-ahead), so the fin
trails at the back and they move the way they face. They're shaded
procedurally, with a dark back, a light belly and faint banding, plus a specular
glint and an iridescent Fresnel that both tighten and strengthen as =uReal=
rises.

The body samples the baked scale texture at coordinates taken from its
rest-pose position, cylindrically round the +Z axis, so the scales stay on the
body as it swims. The texture drives the albedo, the scales' relief (as a
detail-normal height) and the roughness, all blended in by =uReal=, so early
fish look procedural and late fish are fully textured. As a scale shrinks from 6
to 3 pixels across, the scales are eased to their mean, the texture's last mip
(=scales()=, by the UVs' =fwidth=). Any finer and their rows beat against the
pixels. The beating is faint in the albedo and strong in the detail-normal
relief, whose slopes grow with the frequency, and mip-mapping alone doesn't stop
it.

=scales()= takes the footprint's scale as well. The predator's scales are three
times finer than a school fish's, and they're eased to their mean from 18 to 9
pixels across. At those sizes their fine, even net looks like chain mail and
crawls as the predator sweeps past the lens. So its flank is dark slate, and its
scales only show where they're big, on the head as it passes the lens. The
flank gets a quiet texture (0.8-1.2 of the albedo, with a faint relief of
0.02 + 0.04 =uQ=) and the fins get none, so they show only the rays of their
spines. Where the scales are faded, the rim light their relief would catch on
average is added back, 0.1 of the rim colour, so the skin keeps its brightness
as they come and go.

The fins have their own bone and are shaded apart from the body. Each is a thin
membrane in the fish's tint over rays fanning out from its root, turned to the
viewer and lit through from behind. It's brightest looking toward the sun (a
forward lobe after Barré-Brisebois) and has none of the body's scales or sheen.

Besides the circling schools there's a big school of 300 small fish, holding
station round the blob's path where the blob passes at story second 92.5
(=BIG_U=, 146.0 s of the show). The blob bolts straight through it. The fish
dart aside and a channel opens, the camera flies down it, and it closes behind.
=fish.vert= places the school itself (=uK='s 32) as an analytic flow round the
known path, with no flock to simulate. Instance i is a slot on Vogel's spiral
(the golden angle round, the square root out), in four layers of 75 fish 1.1 m
apart, over a disc 16 m across. The disc is centred on the last school record
=uw_world= writes, and all of it lies under the camera's way.

Each fish finds its closest approach to the path from the nearest of 26 of the
path's samples in the placement's buffer (binding 0) and how far along the next
segment it lies. From 1.5 s before that moment it darts away from the path at
right angles, by 5.5 m less a fifth of its distance from the path. An outer fish
starts 0.15 s sooner for every metre out, so none catches up with another. From
2.5 s after, once the camera has passed, it drifts back. Each fish heads where it
moves, on top of a common swim, and its model matrix is built in the shader.

We measured the school offline over the act, with a copy of the shader's maths
in 0.01 s steps. The fish keep 5.55 m from the blob's centre, 5.1 m from the
camera, 1.96 m from every prop's reach and 0.98 m from the circling schools'
fish. As ellipsoids of a fish's length and height, they never overlap. The
school is one instanced draw, made only past story second 60 (119.9 s) and only
while its disc is in the frustum. It appears unseen, with the camera turned
136-176° away. It casts no shadow, since that would cost the whole draw again.
It costs 4.4 ms a frame at 150% on the UHD 620 with the school in view and 9 ms
inside it.

The crabs (sculpted, drawn with the fish PBR shader) are small, reddish and
eight-legged, with no scales. They turn up on the floor in the second half of
the act and are kept clear of the boulders.

*** The whale
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-whale
:END:

The whale (sculpted, posed by =fish.vert=, painted by =whale.frag=) is a blue
whale about 30 units long. Its body is a loft of cross-sections. There's a
broad, flat, U-shaped rostrum with the throat sagging below it, the deepest
girth just behind the flippers, and then a long, slender tail stock, flattened
side to side. It has swept, notched flukes, two slim flippers thick enough to
show edge-on, and a smooth back with no dorsal fin. The vertex shader swims it
the way cetaceans swim, with an up-and-down beat. A slow wave runs from head to
flukes, growing down the tail stock, and the normals bend to follow it.

The skin is painted from the rest-pose position (the angle round the body and
the distance from the flukes to the snout), so it moves with the animal. It's
countershaded blue-grey, mottled with lighter spots and darker blotches. Long
throat grooves run back from the chin, the mouth line sweeps down to a small
eye, and the blowhole is on top. The baked skin texture adds fine relief and
roughness as =uReal= rises.

The whale makes one slow pass in a lane of its own at z -42, which the props and
schools keep clear of at all times. So it never touches the blob, the rocks or
the fish. The pass runs 150 m along +X from =WHALE_IN= to =WHALE_OUT=, story
seconds 4 to 44 (73.1-107.1 s of the show), about 4.4 m/s. It's high in the
water, 3 m up at the top of its arc and 5.5 m under the surface, and it comes
and goes out of sight and out of the sun's map. It first shows at about 76.5 s,
some 90 m off, coming into the upper half of the frame at its left edge as the
water clears. It drifts across above the blob's trail, from three-quarters on to
nearly side-on. At the held glance up at 91.8 s, the camera is about 48 m from
her centre (see
[[#the-water-light][The water's light]]). It's left behind as the blob turns
away: she is still visible around 95.3 s and out of frame by about 97 s.
For roughly 20 s she is the centre of attention. Where the whale can be seen
depends on the blob's heading, which the camera follows, more than on the
whale's own timing.

Seen that large at 1080p, three of its details need care. The throat grooves
fade out as they shrink toward 4 pixels apart (=fwidth= 0.1-0.25), since finer
grooves crawl as a knitted grid of bright dashes. The caustic web is at 0.4 on
the whale and off on its fins, because seen from below, the web's cells line up
along the curved back as evenly spaced dark bars. And the fins keep the flat
diffuse look (=mix(flatc, lit, uQ × body)=). The near flipper, seen end-on from
below, would otherwise catch the IBL's grazing sheen and the rim as a pale,
stair-stepped teardrop at its root. Instead it shows as a soft, lighter patch on
the flank.

*** The predator
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-predator
:END:

The predator (sculpted, drawn with the fish PBR shader) is a big, lean,
menacing fish with a forked tail and a tall dorsal sail of spines with a zigzag
membrane. It's dark, scarred and pale-bellied, with pale, glowing,
slit-pupilled eyes. It has no snout, and its front ends where its jaws begin.
The two jaws are deep and rounded, the lower one longer (an underbite), and each
has four long, uneven fangs a side, raked back. The upper fangs sit on the rim's
inner edge and the lower ones on its outer edge, leaning out, so with the mouth
shut they cross outside the upper lip. On the middle of their rim, the lower
fangs' tips would lie by the upper jaw in the rest pose and take its bone, and
posed they'd stretch into bars across the mouth. The jaws open on a wet,
near-black gullet.

The kit cuts the body at the hinges, sculpts each jaw shut as a hollow shell and
opens it to its rest gape the same way =fish.vert= swings it, so the jaws close
the way they were made. =uGape= swings the upper jaw and its fangs up and the
lower jaw down about those hinges (=pred_gape=). While the predator stalks, the
jaws are ajar at the sculpted pose, 0.55, with the fangs on the rims. From a
second before =ENC_LUNGE= to =ENC_PEAK= - 0.35 (120.9-122.6 s of the show)
they're flung open to 1.55 (66° each), a tall dark maw ringed with fangs. The
predator sweeps past the lens with them wide open, its maw filling the frame at
the near miss (122.9 s), and they slam shut on the near miss's kick, from
=ENC_PEAK= to 0.15 s after it. Opened only to 1 (43° each), the jaws look like a
toothy box, and shut any sooner they close before they fill the frame.

A vertex within 2 cm of a jaw's hollow is marked as inside the mouth by adding
256 to its bones word. =fish.vert= reads the two bones from the low bits and
passes the mark on, and =fish.frag= paints such a vertex as the gullet, a wet,
near-black red with no eye decal, and cuts its lit colour to 15%. So the water's
sheen, rim and ambient light don't light the inside like the hide, and the open
mouth is black under the fog. The earlier 64,365-byte version was checked against the
posed mesh every 5 ms, with minimum jaw and whole-body clearances of 2.08 m
and 0.61 m respectively. Those are historical full-mesh measurements, not
new measurements of the fitted path.

The current predator path is fitted to the original rounded release's fixed
chase, rather than simulated at startup. It has 33 adaptive cubic Hermite
knots: positions in 1/128 m as signed 16-bit integers, velocities in 1/4 m/s
as signed bytes, and 32 span lengths in 60 Hz ticks as unsigned bytes. The
tables occupy 329 raw bytes. =pred_build= converts each segment's tangents
to the existing Catmull-Rom evaluator and reconstructs the same 60 Hz path
table that the rendering and seabed placement use.

A knot at story second 64 preserves the near miss's timing. The fitted
release path deviates by at most about 6 cm at its centre and 1.23 degrees in
heading from the old rounded release; banking can differ by about 5.9 degrees.
This preserves the stalk, lunge, miss, chase and break-off, with no steering
or collision-push solver in the shipped path builder. Clearance checks sample
three spheres along the body at 256 Hz, and the placement shader keeps props
away from the reconstructed path. The camera blow, cold desaturated darkening
and vignette retain their original cues. The fit is an approximation of the
release animation, not a claim of identical frames or full-mesh clearance.

=algae.vert= / =algae.frag= draw the seaweed, a two-sided ribbon of 16 segments

thin into a sub-pixel stick that crawls under the jitter. A blade 0.24 m wide at

Blades sprout from the floor at staggered times and ease up to full height, so
more weed turns up as the journey goes on. A wrap-lit subsurface glow and a wet
sheen come up with =uReal=.

A tall blade (from 3-5 m up, by its instance's height) is a kelp frond, up to
about 0.9 m across its middle, with a rippling edge. It sways three times as far
and is tinted golden brown, and =algae.frag='s translucency takes the tint too.
It still turns to the eye about its spine. The thickets' kelp is 6-11 m tall and
some cells double their loose blades to 3.2-6.4 m, rising toward the surface.

blade over its height and sway, which keeps the kelp clear of the camera and of

*** Coral heads
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: coral-heads
:END:

=coral.vert= / =coral.frag= draw coral in four forms, built in =coral.vert= from
=gl_VertexID=. The placement picks the form for each head (=aColor.a=, about a
quarter each). The staghorn is a short trunk forking four ways, and each fork
forks twice more, which makes 29 tapered six-sided tubes of 36 vertices, 1,044
in all. Each child starts at its parent's tip with the tip's radius, so the
joints overlap. The other three forms are one surface of revolution built from
the same 174 quads, 16 sides round by 10 rings. It's an ellipsoid with its lower
half pulled down onto the sand, by the sixth power of how far below the middle a
ring lies, and two waves round its rim to make it irregular. From it come a
brain coral's half-sunk dome, a table coral's plate (a tenth of its size thick)
on the cone of its stalk, and a sea fan's thin upright disc. All four stay
inside the unit reach the placement gives a head, so every clearance holds, and
all cast through the one shadow program (=shadowcoral=). A staghorn alone would
read as a sprig at flight speed.

Each head is turned and scaled by its instance and carries its own shade in its
colour, as =rock.vert='s foot tint does for the boulders. Whatever faces down,
like a table's underside and stalk or a dome's foot, falls to 0.3 of the colour.
The whole head darkens toward the sand, to 0.4 at its foot, and the heads vary
in lightness by a factor of 0.7-1.2. A head's hue is one of seven saturated
ones: pink, orange, purple, yellow, teal, red and rose (the water takes red
first). On a table the hue is muted to 45% and the rest is the table's grey,
warmed, which gives olive, ochre, dusty purple and rose. A table's growing rim
is paler. On a dome the hue is muted a little, to 80%, and on a fan not at all.

=coral.frag= lightens the upper parts by the height up the head, which
=vColor.a= carries along with the form. On the shared PBR model it adds a polyp
speckle (0.75-1.25 of the albedo, a dotted top up close), detail normals and a
subsurface glow. It cuts a fan into lace where a 3D noise strays from its
middle, and ridges a dome with meanders, the bands of a sine of a noise. The
pigments also fluoresce faintly in the blue light, at 0.15 =uQ= of the albedo
squared, so the reef keeps its colour further into the water. Much more than
that, and the tables become flat, evenly lit neon discs. Heads grow in from a
point as the blob flees into the richer water. They add about 1-2 ms a frame at
150% on the UHD 620 through the flight.

*** The jelly (=blob.frag=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-jelly-blob-frag
:END:

The blob is Scene 1's (=blob.vert= / =blob.frag=, see
[[#the-blue-blob][The blue blob]]), and as the renderer grows up
it turns into jelly. That's driven by =uQ=, the act's renderer-evolution ramp,
which runs from 0 at the breach to 1 at story second 30 (69.7-95.3 s; see
[[#the-renderer-evolves-the-uq-ramp][The renderer evolves]]). Once =uQ= passes
0.1, at 74.6 s, the blob is drawn last, after the seabed, the water backdrop and
the moon jellies. Just before it, the demo copies the picture drawn so far into
a texture (=jelly_copy=, texture unit 9), the whole frame in one
=glCopyTexSubImage2D=. A rectangle round the ball wouldn't save much, because
the lens reads up to about 2.3 radii from the ball's centre, so the rectangle
would be most of the frame anyway. The copy takes about 0.3 ms on the UHD 620 at
1536x864, and next to nothing on a compo machine.

The jelly is a clear ball lens. Each colour's ray is bent into the ball, crosses
its chord (2 R cos θ) and is bent out again. The exit normal is the entry normal
reflected about the ray, so the shader needs no centre. The copy is read 1.2 R
beyond the exit point, so the reef behind shows through magnified and upright.
Along that chord the blue body absorbs red and green by Beer-Lambert. A ray
straight through the middle keeps e^(-1.2 (1 - c)) of each channel, where c is the
body's colour, (0.15, 0.40, 1). Only a tenth of the milky shading remains. The
ball's relative index is 0.88 (an index of 1.136 against the water), and each
colour's index differs from the next by 0.004 times the Fresnel term. So the
colours split a little toward the rim and not at all face-on. Split across the
whole face, they would break each magnified caustic line into red, green and
blue contours, like an oil slick. A Fresnel reflection mirrors the water around
it, bright above and foggy below, with the sky contribution scaled by 2.6.
The sun makes a broader, dimmer glint (=pow(..., 300) * 4=), so small changes
in the skin's normal do not make it flicker as sharply. The caustic web ripples
over the top of the ball.

While the blob is lumpy (=uWobble= 1, until the transformation rounds it out
over 129.1-139.7 s), each lump is a small lens of its own. At full strength the
lumps would focus the seabed's caustic lines into concentric blue rings across
the body, like thumbprints. So while it's lumpy, its rays bend by a normal
halfway between the surface's and the view direction. Once it has rounded they
bend by its own normal (0.93 of the way there at =uWobble= 0.15), and the seabed
shows through as clear, warped glass. In the flight the lens shows the rushing
seabed, magnified and changing every frame. Measured at about 150-152 s, it
changes no faster than the seabed beside it (24 against 28 levels a frame at
30 fps).

The jelly's share J is =smoothstep(0.1, 0.5, uQ)= squared, so it's only a
quarter at the halfway point. With an even blend, the dark seabed seen through
the lumps would show as dark blue bruises in the half-milky body. Squared, the
milky body holds until the jelly can show clearly through it, and the change
still finishes on time: J reaches 1 when =uQ= reaches 0.5, at story second 15
(82.4 s).

Until the end of the transformation the face is turned to the camera. The sun's
glint has its half-vector just above the point facing the view, so it would sit
on or between the eyes, like a third eye or a white nose. So there's no glint
within about 37° of that point, and it grades back in by 53°, scaled by how much
of the eyes shows. =render_scene2= works out how far the face is turned from the
camera and sends the eyes' share in =uGridFade=, Scene 1's grid knob, which is
unused under water. This way the glint also leaves the face when it glances
back in the flight, and returns when the back of its head turns to us.

The blob writes depth with =gl_FragDepth=. As jelly it writes the depth of its
centre. While it's milky (until 74.6 s) it writes the depth of a point half its
radius in, because with the centre's depth the TAA would leave a ghost of its
rim inside its edge. The bubbles, its eyes and the marine snow draw after it, so
they aren't in the copy. That way the eyes, 0.9 of its radius out, show through
its front half however the lumps lie, and the lens never smears ghosts of them
across the body. While it's milky, trail bubbles and specks of snow within 1.2
radii of its centre aren't drawn, since they would show through it.

As a lens, the blob casts no shadow, but it focuses the sun. =uwsurf.frag= works
out that light from the ball's centre and radius, which ride in the Frame
block's =uGlow= and =uGlowAmt=, two fields Scene 2 has no other use for. Scene
1's object shader, which draws Scene 2's bubbles, eyes and marine snow, uses the
same two fields for its glow at the origin. So the block is sent again with
=uGlowAmt= 0 just before that draw. Otherwise the specks would be lit by a
"glow" coloured by the ball's coordinates, negative channels and all.

Sunlight through a ball of index 1.136 converges about 4.2 radii behind its
centre (f = n R / 2 (n - 1)). A soft-bodied ball with a wobbling skin focuses
loosely, so the sand under it gets a soft Gaussian spot (1.2 radii across at
1/e²) of up to 2.65 times the sun's light, carrying the caustic web. It sits in
a soft, dim penumbra (0.65 at its centre, back to 1 by 1.1 radii). It fades in
below the ball, fades out again as the ball rises 3-7 radii above what it
lights, and fades out beyond 20-45 m from the eye. This applies to the direct
light on the seabed and the boulders, and fades in with =uQ= like the shadows.
At 1.5 times the sun's light, the spot wouldn't show over the web and the sky's
light. The fish, coral, weed and whale don't get it, but they rarely pass
under the ball.

*** The blob's face
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-blob-face
:END:

The blob has no face through the journey and the whole encounter, so the stalk,
the near miss, the bolt and the chase all play on its body alone. Scene 1's eyes
(=blob_eyes=, 0.9 of its radius out) only grow back once it has survived the
scare. They grow from nothing at =TRANS_IN= (129.1 s) to Scene 1's size over 9
story seconds (to 137.4 s), wide open, while the camera circles the blob.

The face is turned by an angle with no roll. Until the flight it turns toward
the camera, tilted to the camera's height, and the hand-over turns it the short
way round. In the flight the angle comes from the direction to the camera
alone. It never comes from the blob's heading, which weaves faster than the
chase camera, so a turn measured from it would whip round as the heading crossed
the camera's line. The face looks away, toward the side the chase's sway swings
the camera out to, and as the sway peaks it glances back to a tenth of that
angle, about 18° off the camera. The eyes shrink away while the face is 23-50°
from the camera. The camera is close, so the outline it sees lies about 75°
round from it, and the eyes sit about 25° out from the middle of the face.
Every appearance and disappearance takes 0.27-0.33 s, and while the eyes show,
the face never turns faster than 3° a frame at 60 fps.

*** Bubbles and marine snow
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: bubbles-and-marine-snow
:END:

The bubbles are more instanced balls: the 14 rising out of the top of the blob
and the dive's 40. The blob's bubbles are offset -1 to 1 along its heading, so
they rise out of it and don't trail back between it and the lens. They carry
alpha -4 (see [[#lighting-object-frag][Lighting (object.frag)]]
for the codes), and =object.frag= shades them as air in water. Past the critical
angle, where the view meets the skin at a sine over 0.75, the water's light is
totally reflected. So the rim, graded in from a sine of 0.6 to full at 0.8, is a
silvery mirror of the bright water around it: 3 times the fog's colour below, 7
times the light from above. With that grading, a bubble only a few pixels across
still shows. Inside, they're clear apart from a sheen that keeps 20%, so the
picture behind shows through, and the sun glints off them. =render_scene2= draws
the ball batch blended and premultiplied, after the jelly, so the jelly shows
through the bubbles too. Everything else in the batch has alpha 1, so the eyes
and the snow stay opaque. The dive's bubbles are 0.7-1.7 cm across per metre of
distance from the lens, so they stay a few pixels wide as they rise past it.

The marine snow is instanced balls too: 500 specks of silt (5-18 mm) in a dim
grey-teal with a touch of emission. They hang still in the water in a 10 m box
that wraps round the eye. A speck shrinks away within 1.5 m of the lens (a
smoothstep over 0.4-1.5 m on its size), since one passing 0.2 m from the lens
would fly by as a white egg 120 px across. The backdrop has specks of its own,
but they're fixed to view directions on the far plane, so they only show against
open water and never move against anything. The snow hangs in front of the
seabed, the rocks and the blob. It slides past with parallax as the camera
trails the blob, streaks under the motion blur in the flight and goes soft in
the depth of field. It's hard to see in a still and clear in motion. The specks
take their own seed. The dive's burst draws from the bubbles' stream only while
it lasts, so sharing that stream would move every speck when the burst ends.
The 14 + 40 + 6 (two eyes, their lids and catchlights) + 500 instances stay
within the ball mesh's 694.

*** Moon jellies (=jelly.vert= / =jelly.frag=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: moon-jellies-jelly-vert-jelly-frag
:END:

Between the whale and the predator, at story seconds 40-55 (103.7-116.2 s), the
blob would otherwise cross nothing but sand and boulders, in the same framing.
So a swarm of 56 moon jellies drifts in the light above its path. =jelly.vert=
builds each one from =gl_VertexID=. There's a bell of 16x6 cells on a flattened
dome, with its rim drawing in and out about every 1.6 story seconds, and there
are 12 strands of 10 segments, turned to face the eye. Eight are fine tentacles
from the rim and four are broad, frilled oral arms from the middle, and all of
them sway. Each jelly is tilted its own way, by up to about 15°, and its bell is
0.9-1.6 m in radius. =jelly.vert= places it by =gl_InstanceID=, 4-12 m to one
side of the point on the blob's path (in =uwplace.comp='s buffer) that the blob
passes at that jelly's own moment.

=jelly.frag= adds it over the water, on both sides and writing no depth, from
story second 36 to 57 (100.4-117.7 s), fading in and out over the first and the
last 3. It draws the clear tissue, which shows where it's seen edge-on, the rim
and four pink horseshoe gonads round the crown. They're lit by the sunlight left
at the jelly's depth and dimmed by the water's extinction with distance. The
light the water scatters is already drawn behind it.

Eight cyan radial canals run out across each bell, with faint light on the
trailing strands. Their glow pulses slowly with a phase that travels outward,
and =fwidth= softens the fine lines at the pixel scale. That derivative is
evaluated before the bell/strand branch so its footprint remains valid.
The glow rises over
story seconds 38-43, holds through 51 and fades by 56. Over the same envelope,
the water's scattered-light colour dims by up to 30% and exposure drops by
0.10, so the canals show against a quieter background. This uses the existing
jelly draw and bloom; the 56 jellies keep their geometry, paths and clearance.

The jellies' height keeps them clear of everything else whatever the hash gives,
which matters because sines differ between GPUs, so the layout may too. At story
second 48 the bell's centre is 3.9-5.9 m up, and it rises 0.1 m a second. That
keeps its crown 0.8 m under the surface and the tips of its arms at least 1 m
above the camera, the blob and the schools' fish. None of those rises above
-0.8 m from story second 34 to 58. We measured every anchor, side, distance and
height the shader can pick. One of the load's warm-up frames (half bar 105, at
101.6 s) draws the jellies, so their program is built during the load.

*** The seabed and its props (=uwplace.comp=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-seabed-and-its-props-uwplace-comp
:END:

The seabed is a large, finely subdivided heightfield (=mc_mesh_terrain='s,
computed on the GPU) sunk well below the blob. The boulders are a dense ball
displaced by three layers of noise into craggy rock. Both are computed once at
load by the shader that then places the props.

Every boulder, weed blade, coral head and crab in the act is placed once at load
by a compute shader, =uwplace.comp=. It works against one buffer that holds
everything the props must keep clear of. That's the blob's whole path (which is
deterministic), the picture's camera (=camera_uw=) every quarter of a story
second, the predator's path, the fish schools and the seabed's heights. A
candidate's id says where it may stand. The 240 m seabed is split into 34x34
cells with 48 slots each. The current recipe uses two slots for fill boulders,
six for weed and three for coral around a shared clump. Slots 11-33 scatter a
mix of loose boulders, coral, crabs and blades, using the four =LOOSE= ranges
for their sizes and seed times. Slots 34-47 form late coral patches with one
hue per cell. The recipe shares its placement and sizing code instead of
giving each cell separate boulder formations, kelp groves, two-hue patches and
age-dependent size variations. The scene's larger structures still come from
18 landmarks of 96 slots each: six rock outcrops, six reefs and six
kelp thickets. A landmark reef uses 56-85 of its slots, three quarters of them
coral heads ringed by kelp.

The shader runs in 21 passes. The first works out the sand and the boulder. The
sand is the 201x201 heights of =mc_mesh_terrain='s fBm, with the engine's noise
ported exactly. The boulder is a cube's six grids (20, 10 and 5 cells a side for
its three LODs), pushed out by three layers of that noise, and each vertex gets
the normal of the surface around it. Both stay in the buffer for the frames.
=object.vert= builds the seabed from the heights and =rock.vert= builds the
boulders from their vertices, so each mesh is only the indices of its grid.

The next pass sites the landmarks beside the flight one after another, each with
room for an outcrop's core and each born unseen once the predator has gone. Each
site tries up to 99 points.

One pass seats every candidate on the sand by replaying the seabed's triangles.
It keeps a candidate if it clears the blob's path and the fish schools,
including the big school's whole disc, 12.5 m round it and 3.5 m above and
below. The middle's props must also keep out of the whale's lane. The fill is
seeded from about story second 60, long after the whale has gone. A boulder must
also clear the camera's and the predator's paths, and its radius leaves headroom
for =rock.vert='s growth. A blade is tested over its height and sway, just as
coral heads and boulders are tested as they stand, so a short blade may stand
under the path.

Sixteen passes settle conflicts the same way placing the props one by one would.
The middle's props come before the fill, boulders before coral, crabs and weed,
and after that the lower id wins. The decisions are double-buffered, and they
settle by about the tenth pass. The one exception is a head in a coral patch. It
makes way for the middle's boulders, coral, crabs and weed, which are all there
before it, though a blade may stand at its edge, at 0.9 of its reach. It also
packs tighter itself, overlapping the other heads of its patch (0.2 of the two
reaches, against 0.32 between other coral heads). So a patch forms one mound,
and nothing that stood there before it has to go.

One pass sets the timing of every prop that's kept. Each prop grows in from the
first moment after its seed time at which, for the whole grow-in, the camera
stays more than 70 m away or keeps the prop out of a 60° cone (=born()=). The
range is that long because the water clears early, and at a shorter range a
boulder far off could be seen swelling up from a dot. A prop dies when a
landmark grows over its spot. One that's never out of sight for that long grows
in at its seed time if that's before story second 50, and is dropped if it's
later.

A boulder seeded before story second 40 may be born from the start of the act.
Without that, at 70 m they'd all be born late, and under the whale's pass the
lower two-thirds of the frame would be bare sand. Over the first 3 story
seconds, in the murk, out of sight means further than 25 m, since at clarity 0
the water passes only about a tenth of the light from 30 m away. A boulder born
then has its birth set 3 s earlier, so it stands fully grown from the first
frame and never visibly grows. That puts 9-11 boulders in frame within 60 m over
74-96 s. The price is a sparser early act, since some props come later.

The last pass works on 68x68 squares of the sand, 3.5 m each (half a cell). For
each square it lists the kept boulders whose contact shade reaches into it, up
to 16. They're stored as the places of their records in the buffer, which stays
bound on binding 0 for the frames. The squares are half a cell wide to keep the
lists short, since the sand reads its square's list at every pixel. The pass
also works out the reef bed at the square's centre. That's how much bed is laid
down by the heads of the coral patches, the landmarks' coral and every head born
after story second 80, leaving out any head a landmark grows over. Each head
lays down a disc that fades out from half its reach to twice it, wider the later
the head is born (up to about 4.25 times for the last). And the pass works out
when the bed may grow in. That's the first moment after the last of those heads
is born at which the square, too, stays out of sight for the whole grow-in, by
the props' own rule. If that never happens the square gets no bed, which is the
case for a handful of squares.

The CPU reads the result back into one list of props, which =uw_prepare= draws
in one loop (growth, death, culling and the boulders' LOD). The 25 circling fish
schools stay on the CPU, and it's the props that keep clear of them. A 26th
record holds the big school, which =fish.vert= places. All this takes about
155 ms of GPU time at load in the earlier 64,365-byte version, about 60 of it for the
lists, since every square scans every record. That version held 4,337 props.
The current integrated verification retains 4,916: 636 boulders, 725 coral
heads, 277 crabs and 3,278 weed blades. It checks all 4,050 late growth
intervals and the path, camera, fish, predator and boulder clearances.
The revised population recipe changes the layout. Its 21 passes
retain the path-clearance tests, ranked conflict resolution, offscreen
grow-in search, landmark death timing, contact-shade lists and reef-bed
generation. That version's clearance measurements and load timings
should not be treated as measurements of this revised layout.

The camera's path runs through the ring of one circling school, 3.35 m round,
coming within 3.5 m of its centre at 111.8 s. That school keeps its ring, so the
props still keep clear of it, but it's drawn empty, or one of its fish would
cross the lens. A school shows no fish if any of the camera's samples comes
inside a circle a metre wider than its ring, and only that one does.

The fill is seeded from about story second 60 and the landmarks are born once
the predator has gone, so the reveal brings in the biggest structures last.
Meanwhile the counts of visible rocks, fish, crabs, weed and coral keep climbing
right through the closing flight. Props are seeded up to story second 96 and the
circling schools fill up until 98 (148.5-150.0 s). The blob speeds up and
spirals outward too: =blob_phase=, plus a widening of its wander during the
flight, makes it really go further and faster. So the picture gets more detailed
and more crowded with life as the blob travels, and it ends on a fully populated
reef that shows what the engine can do.

** The renderer evolves (the uQ ramp)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-renderer-evolves-the-uq-ramp
:END:

Three ramps on the story clock change the act's look as it runs.
=render_scene2= works them out from =uw_u()= and hands them to every underwater
shader in the Frame block. The water's =clarity= (=uClarity=) is
=smoothstep(0, 9, u)=. It takes the fog and the light from murk to clear over
the first 9 story seconds, and it's done by 77.3 s. The realism push, =real=
(=uReal=), is =smoothstep(8, 48, u)= and runs over 76.5-110.4 s, finishing as
the encounter draws near. It layers finer detail textures and relief onto the
surfaces, sharpens their speculars and brings in the fish's baked skin. The
third, =uQ=, is =smoothstep(0, 30, u)= and runs over 69.7-95.3 s.

=uQ= changes the rendering model itself. It feeds every underwater shader and
the post stack, and it blends a flat, cheap early look into a full modern one.
It also turns the blob to jelly (J = =smoothstep(0.1, 0.5, uQ)=, squared), so
the jelly is fully clear from 82.4 s. The shadows, the occlusion and the PBR are
nearly all in by about 90 s (=uQ= 0.9) and fully in at 95.3 s. So the renderer
visibly grows up over the act's first 26 s, from murk to clear and from flat to
lit. By the finale the engine is doing all of this at once:

- Physically based shading. Every underwater surface (the seabed, boulders,
  fish, crabs, predator, whale, weed and coral) runs the same Cook-Torrance
  microfacet BRDF: a GGX normal distribution, a Smith geometry term and
  Fresnel-Schlick reflectance, over a per-material albedo, roughness and F0. It
  conserves energy, so the speculars tighten and the ground looks like a real
  material instead of ad-hoc Lambert.
- Analytic image-based lighting. There's no room for cubemaps, so the ambient
  light is an analytic underwater environment (=uwEnv=). It's bright toward the
  surface, and below it's the sand's light bounced back up, (0.04, 0.11, 0.14).
  So over bright, sunlit sand, anything facing down (a rock's flank, a fish's
  belly, the whale's side) is lifted instead of going black. The shaders sample
  it for diffuse irradiance, and along the reflection vector, blended by
  roughness, for a specular "probe", and mix the two by Fresnel. That's what
  makes the wet surfaces look lit by the water instead of a lamp.
- Detail normals. A procedural height field, applied without tangents through
  screen-space derivatives, adds micro-relief that grows with =uQ=: pores and
  grain the mesh never had.
- Wavelength-selective extinction. The water takes red first, then green, like
  Jerlov's oceanic water types. Extinction is per channel and goes from murky
  (0.18, 0.07, 0.05 per metre) to clear (0.06, 0.018, 0.013) as the act clears.
  It's the cue that most clearly says you're under water. The light scattered in
  along a ray is integrated in closed form (=waterFog=) and gets brighter as the
  ray climbs toward the light. The fog's colour is set by the camera's own
  depth.
- Caustics and shafts from one field. The light the surface's ripples focus is
  the caustic web, read where the sun's ray through a point left the surface. On
  the seabed, rocks, creatures and coral it makes the dappling, and red's lines
  are widest, so the edges fringe. On the jelly it's a faint shimmer, and in the
  backdrop it makes the shafts (see [[#the-water-light][The water's light]]), so
  the shafts stand over the brightest dapples. They're the only shafts: there's
  no screen-space god-ray pass.
- Ambient occlusion. The scene renders depth to a texture that can be sampled,
  and the engine's half-resolution GTAO pass darkens contact creases and the lee
  of every rock and fish (see
  [[file:architecture.org::#the-renderer-and-its-post-stack][The renderer and its post stack]]).
  Its strength is 0.7 =uQ=.
- Jelly. The blob stops glowing like a lamp and becomes a clear ball lens that
  magnifies the reef behind it and focuses the sun on the sand (see
  [[#the-jelly-blob-frag][The jelly]]).
- Sun shadow mapping. A depth-only framebuffer and a position-only pass render
  the big casters into a 2048² depth texture, from an orthographic sun view
  fitted round the first 40 m of the camera's view. The casters are the
  boulders, the coral, the kelp, the whale and the predator. The jelly isn't
  one, because as a lens it focuses the sun instead. The seabed casts nothing
  either, since its own shadow would dim the caustics the look is tuned with.
  The shadow pass shares the prepared instance ranges with the colour pass, and
  the rocks, kelp, whale and predator deform their vertices in it just as they
  do in the colour pass. Culling is done separately against the camera's and the
  light's frusta, with conservative bounds for the animation and three rock LODs
  with hysteresis. Every lit underwater shader then projects its world position
  into the light's space and takes 3x3 taps of the map (=shadowVis=), so
  surfaces fall into shadow under the rocks, the kelp and the creatures. The
  hardware does the depth compare: the map is a texture in compare mode,
  linearly filtered and read through a =sampler2DShadow=. So each tap is itself
  a bilinear 2x2 PCF, and a penumbra grades smoothly instead of in ninths for
  the same nine fetches. The shadows fade in with =uQ=, and out between 35 and
  40 m from the eye. Scene 1 and the end card never share a frame with Scene 2,
  so they borrow the map for their key light (see
  [[#lighting-object-frag][Lighting (object.frag)]]). Scene 2
  draws the map afresh before it reads it, on every frame the sea below is
  drawn, from the crossing's first frame on and with =uQ= at 0 too. That's
  because =object.frag='s =keyShadow= reads the map whatever =uQ= says. Without
  the pass, it would compare the bubbles, the dive's burst and the marine snow
  against Scene 1's last key-light depths.

The early seconds use almost none of this (=uQ= near 0 gives a flat, murky
look), and the finale uses all of it (=uQ= near 1). The geometry stays the same,
and it's rendered as if the engine grew up over the act. The PBR, IBL, detail
normals, extinction and shadow sampling live in the same functions at the top of
=uwsurf.frag=, =fish.frag=, =whale.frag=, =algae.frag= and =coral.frag=, and the
water's light lives in a second, smaller block (below). The ambient occlusion is
a post pass in =render.c=, gated by =mc_post.ssao=, so it costs nothing in
Scene 1. The shadow map is a pass the demo draws between =mc_renderer_begin= and
the lit draws, and it hands the framebuffer back with =mc_renderer_bind_scene=.

** The water's light
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-water-light
:END:

Eight shaders share one block of code, byte for byte, between two marker
comments (=// ---- the water= ... =// ---- end water ----=), so the water lights
everything in it the same way. The eight are the backdrop (=water.frag=), the
seabed and rocks (=uwsurf.frag=), the fish, the whale, the coral, the weed, the
blob and the moon jellies. The block holds the surface's height (8.5 m up, and
the camera stays under it), the water's extinction (=wx=, red first, murky to
clear over the act), the caustic web (=cw=) and the water's phase function
(=wp=):

- The caustic web is the squared distance to the nearest point of two or three
  rotated, drifting lattices. Each lattice is the previous one turned and scaled
  by one fixed matrix, with time as the third coordinate. The web is read at the
  point where the sun's ray through the shaded point left the surface. A lit
  surface takes it to the 4th, 5th and 6th power of that distance for red, green
  and blue, each normalised to a mean of 1, so red's lines are the widest and
  fringe. The web fades to 1 where the pixel's footprint spans the lines, so far
  rocks skip it, and it gets stronger as the water clears. The weed leaves it
  out, since on thin blades it can't be seen and still costs time.
- The fog (=waterFog=, in the five surface shaders) is the sunlight left at the
  point's depth times the transmission along the ray, plus the light scattered
  in along the ray, in closed form. The sunlight falls off at half the
  extinction per metre of depth and is 1 at the blob's depth, 13 m down. The
  in-scatter is =uFogColor= × c/e × (1 - e^(-e s)), with e = c - 0.5 c d_y, so
  it brightens toward the light. The C side scales =uFogColor= to the eye's own
  depth with the same formula.
- The phase function =wp= scatters forward, half evenly and half by Schlick's
  Henyey-Greenstein with g 0.4. It's 1 across the light, about 3.4 toward the
  sun and 0.7 away from it, in the fog and the backdrop alike. That's why the
  total-internal-reflection mirror, seen looking down and away from the light,
  is dark.

The sun comes down 29° off the vertical, along (0.5, 1, 0.25), well inside the
48.6° an underwater sun can reach. The light and the shadow map share that
direction. At that slant the boulders, coral, predator and whale throw shadows
beside them, and the rocks sit on the sand instead of looking pasted on it. The
backdrop's shafts keep their own, lower slant. They're extruded along 1.2 times
the sun's slant, 34° off the vertical, because shafts show best when the eye
looks along them.

The clear water's colour is (0.04, 0.22, 0.37), a deep ocean blue. Every ray
saturates to it, so it's also the colour of the backdrop at the horizon and of
everything distant. The water absorbs red first and blue last, so the near sand
stays a pale cyan-white while the distance goes blue, which gives a change of
hue from near to far. A teal of the same brightness would pull the sand, the
rocks and the jelly toward it, and the act would come out nearly monochrome
cyan. It's a grading choice.

At story second 26 (=UW_LOOK=, 91.8 s) the camera glances up at the whale
passing overhead (=uw_look=, shared by the camera and the post). The glance
rises over story seconds 21-26 and holds through 28. The camera eases 10 m
back along its direction from the blob, rising a little as it goes. At the
held view the blob is about 28.2 m away rather than 18.2 m, so the mother and
calf have room above it. The view turns 40% of the way
from the whale to the blob, and a little up, and the lens tightens from 55° to
48°. So the whale stands against the bright water overhead, with the blob in
the foreground. The exposure eases down 0.35 for the shot. The view stops just
under Snell's window, because the window's broken rim would flicker in along the
top of the frame as lime-green chips. Then, over story seconds 28-32, the
trailing camera takes over again, since a faster swing down flashes the
window's bright rim across the frame. The props are placed against this camera,
glance and all.

The backdrop's shafts are sharp sheets, gathered from six slices along each
ray. Swept through at speed, they would strobe. When the eye trembles (the
blob's panic, about 0.3 m at about 10 Hz) or flies and turns fast (16-65 m/s,
up to 123°/s in the flight), each slice would jump from sheet to sheet between
frames. The specks fixed to view directions would sweep across the frame in
jumps, and so would the bright rim of Snell's window as the eye comes back down
from the glance. So each frame =main.c= traces the eye's path over the half
second around the current time (31 points of =camera_uw=, about 0.04 ms). It
hands =water.frag= two things in =uP= (location 0). One is the path's mean
position, which the shafts hang from, so the tremble doesn't shake them. The
other is a calm value. It's 1 while the eye moves less than about 13 m/s and
turns less than about 50°/s, and it falls as the eye goes faster, sooner near
the surface where the sheets are sharpest. As it falls, the sharp sheets give
way to the same web four times broader and softer, and the specks fade, as do
the slices nearer the lens than 2 m. The window's rim, facets and glint calm
down the same way, as a shutter would smear them. Calm is 1 from the breach to
the start of the encounter (69.7-116.7 s), apart from about 2 s at
94.6-96.7 s, as the blob turns away from the whale and the camera swings after
it.

What still jumps in the panic is its tremble shaking the whole frame, which is
intended. The seabed and the backdrop meet without a seam, because the
backdrop's in-scatter is the same formula as =waterFog=.

** How it's put together
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: how-it-put-together
:END:

=scene3.c= holds what everything in the scene shares:

- the timeline: =S3_J0= and the cues ([[#s3-j0-and-jungle-seconds][below]])
- the floor's height, =s3_ground=, which the GLSL mirrors as =s3h()=
- the tree sites ([[#the-trees-s3-trees-c-jt-vert-jt-frag][The trees]])
- the window of sky that the canopy keeps open, for the birds to cross
  (=S3_GAP_YAW=, =S3_GAP_PITCH=, =S3_GAP_COS=)
- the beach's frame of reference
- the camera ([[#the-camera][The camera]])
- the per-frame context =S3Ctx=, and =render_scene3=, which draws a frame

It then =#include=s the eight package files
([[#the-s3-packages][The packages]]), so =main.c=, =scene3.c= and the packages
are one translation unit. The packages' shaders share one block of GLSL
([[#the-s3-common-block][The s3 common block]]). The Frame block's fields, the
sun maps and the texture units are listed in
[[#s3-frame-fields-sun-maps-and-texture-units][Frame fields, sun maps and
texture units]].

The beach lies at yaw 60°, a direction the look round never turns toward, so
the beach can't be glimpsed early. =s3_along(x, z)= is the distance toward the
sea, =s3_across(x, z)= the distance to the right as you look out, and
=s3_beach_pt= converts back. The forest stops at =S3_EDGE= (32 m out). Past it,
=s3_ground= blends the rolling forest floor into a beach that slopes 6% down
through the waterline (=S3_SHORE_S=, 50 m, with sea level =S3_SEA_Y= at -1 m)
and on under the sea. =s3h()= has the same blend, so everything placed on the
CPU and grown on the GPU sits on the same sand. The glide stops at
=S3_VANTAGE= (13.5 m). The tyrannosaurus walks along =S3_REX_S= (34.5 m) and
puts a foot down every =S3_STEP= (two beats) from =S3_REX=, and the blob comes
ashore at =S3_BLOB_ACROSS= (1.5 m to the right).

=S3Ctx= is everything a package needs to draw a frame. It holds the scene's
time and the jungle time, the macro amount (0 wide to 1 at the ladybug), and the
beach part's progress (0 in the forest to 1 from =S3_GAP=). It also holds the
focus distance, the camera with its view-projection and frustum, and the pass
being drawn. =s3_setup= fills it in for any moment. The sun maps, the trees'
detail and the impostors' bake use it to look at the world at moments other
than the frame's own.

*** =S3_J0= and jungle seconds
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: s3-j0-and-jungle-seconds
:END:

=render_scene3= gets the scene's own seconds, 0 at =SCENE3_START= (154.8 s).
The first bar is white. =S3_J0= is the length of that bar, one =BAR_S=
(1.935 s), and /jungle seconds/ count from its end: =c->j= is the scene's time
minus =S3_J0=, negative in the white. Every cue is a jungle time on a bar line,
written as a whole number of bars: =S3_UP= is 7 bars, =S3_REX= is 27 and
=S3_END= is 54. The shaders that time their own action count the same jungle
seconds, from =uTime= and the common block's =s3j0=.

The clock is stretched once, at the top of =render_scene3=. The scene's seconds
go through =stretch()= with =S3_SEG= and come out as the story's, which is all
that the camera, the packages and the shaders ever see. =S3_SEG= pairs bars of
the show with bars of the story:

| Part             | Show bars | Story bars |
|------------------+-----------+------------|
| look round       |         8 |          7 |
| canopy           |         5 |          4 |
| way down         |         3 |          3 |
| ladybug          |         8 |          7 |
| take-off         |         2 |          2 |
| glide            |         4 |          3 |
| from the hush on |         1 |          1 |

The story ends up four bars behind the show, and from the beach's hush on it
runs at the show's pace. Each change of pace is eased over half a bar. So from a
quarter bar after =S3_GAP= on, show time is =SCENE3_START= + =S3_J0= + 4 bars +
=j=. That is 154.8 + 1.9 + 7.7 s + =j=, or 164.516 s + =j=. The
[[file:architecture.org::#timing][Timing]] section explains =stretch()= and how
the clocks keep to the music, and [[#its-music][Scene 3's music]]
lists the bars each beat falls on.

=S3_HOLD= is four bars of the story in which we stay crouched while the
tyrannosaurus walks out of the picture. Every cue from =S3_CLEAR= on has it
added, in =scene3.c= and in =jo_blob.vert=, =jo_blob.frag=, =ju_ground.frag= and
the surf block. The shaders carry it as a number (7.74 s, or a time that
includes it), so a change to =S3_HOLD= has to be made in all of them.

*** The packages
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-s3-packages
:END:

The work is split into eight packages. Each is one C file that =scene3.c=
=#include=s, with one shader prefix and a manifest of its own programs:

| Package      | Shaders    | What it draws |
|--------------+------------+---------------|
| [[#the-trees-s3-trees-c-jt-vert-jt-frag][=s3_trees.c=]] | =jt.*=, =jt_bake.frag= | the giant trees, their crowns, palms, saplings and lianas, the far forest, and the baked bark, leaf and sand tiles |
| [[#the-forest-floor-and-the-undergrowth-s3-under-c-ju][=s3_under.c=]] | =ju_*=     | the forest floor and the undergrowth |
| [[#light-s3-light-c-jl-and-the-engine-post][=s3_light.c=]] | =jl_*=     | the sun, the atmospheric sky and wispy clouds, the sun maps, light shafts and the grade |
| [[#the-hero-plant-and-the-ladybug-s3-bug-c-jb][=s3_bug.c=]]   | =jb_*=     | the hero plant and the ladybug |
| [[#birds-and-butterflies-s3-birds-c-jv-bird][=s3_birds.c=]] | =jv_bird.*= | the birds and the butterflies |
| [[#the-tyrannosaurus-s3-rex-c-jr][=s3_rex.c=]]   | =jr.*=     | the tyrannosaurus (=jr.comp= is the creature kit, which sculpts it along with Scene 2's animals) |
| [[#the-blob-ashore-s3-blob-c-jo-blob][=s3_blob.c=]]  | =jo_blob.*= | the blob, come ashore, and what it carries |

Each package has =make=, =draw= and =free= hooks, except the ladybug's, which
has only =draw=. =s3_make= places the trees and works out their detail, then
runs the =make= hooks at load, and =s3_free= runs the =free= hooks. =draw= is
called once per pass, with =c->pass= set to one of four values:

- =S3_SHADOW=, the still sun maps. =uViewProj= is =uLightVP= and =uQ= is 1, so
  shaders only do their cut-outs.
- =S3_LIT=, the picture.
- =S3_CHAR=, the characters' own sun map, drawn every frame. The ladybug, the
  tyrannosaurus and the blob draw their depth there the same way as in
  =S3_SHADOW=.
- =S3_LATE=, after the sky, for whatever has to look through the finished
  picture.

A frame runs in this order. =light_frame= writes the lighting terms into the
Frame block. =light_shadow= draws the sky's radiance table, then the still sun
maps if they haven't been drawn yet (a dev build redraws them every frame, so
hot reloads show), then the characters' map. Then comes the lit world: the hero
plant first (up close it hides most of the frame), then the undergrowth and the
floor, the trees, the birds, the tyrannosaurus, what the blob carries, and last
the sea, which is see-through. After that come
=light_sky= and the late pass. The late pass draws the blob's jelly, which needs
the sea and the sky behind it. The sea and wet sand reflect the sky in their
own material shaders. =light_volume= adds the shafts, and =light_post= sets
the grade and the depth of field for the engine's post chain.

Each package lists its programs in its own =s3_<pkg>.def= manifest.
=programs.def= pulls in =scene3.def=, which pulls in the eight manifests, and the
=Demo= struct gets one =mc_shader= field per entry from the same X-macro. So
startup, hot reload, the link checks and the teardown all see the jungle's
programs with no per-program code.

The jungle has no mesh files. Every trunk, leaf, blade, frond and bird is grown
in a vertex shader from =gl_VertexID= and =gl_InstanceID=, and so are the floor,
the sea and the blob. The ladybug's parts are squashed and folded copies of one
small sphere, which =jb_bug.vert= also builds. The C side only decides where
things stand and hands the shaders a uniform table. The tyrannosaurus is the
exception: at load the creature kit sculpts it into a mesh on the GPU
([[#the-tyrannosaurus-s3-rex-c-jr][The tyrannosaurus]]). The bark, leaf and sand
tiles and the far forest's impostors are baked at load too
([[#the-trees-s3-trees-c-jt-vert-jt-frag][The trees]]).

*** The s3 common block
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-s3-common-block
:END:

The /s3 common block/ is a stretch of GLSL that the jungle's shaders carry byte
for byte the same, between two marker comments:

#+begin_example
// ---- s3 common: keep byte-identical in every jungle shader (owner: s3_light) ----
...
// ---- end s3 common ----
#+end_example

The jungle shaders share it wherever they need the scene's lighting and world
helpers; the bake (=jt_bake.frag=) and creature kit (=jr.comp=) do not.
The light package owns the block, and a change to it
is made in one copy and copied over the others. The block keeps every package in
the same light. The context-mixing packer codes the copies for almost nothing,
and =genshaders.py= stores the block once
([[file:pipeline.org::#genshaders-py-bake-shaders-into-headers][genshaders.py]]).
It holds:

- the Frame block, which every scene's shaders share
  ([[#s3-frame-fields-sun-maps-and-texture-units][Scene 3's meanings for its fields]])
- the samplers for the sun maps (units 3-8) and the sky's radiance table (unit
  15), bound with =layout(binding)=, so no C code sets them
- =s3sea=, the direction toward the sea, and =s3j0=, the white's length
  (=S3_J0=)
- =s3h()=, the floor's height
- =s3shadow()=, the sun's shadow at a point
- =s3lit()= and the =s3light()= macro over it, the lighting
- =s3sky()=, the open sky, and =s3fog()=, the air between the eye and a point

Shaders that time their own action count jungle seconds as =j = uTime - s3j0=,
the same as =scene3.c=. They are the tyrannosaurus's walk and its prints, the
blob's hops and trail, and the birds.

=s3shadow()= is a soft sun shadow whose filter widens with the distance to the
blocker, so the canopy far overhead throws soft dapples and a leaf close by
throws a sharp shadow. It reads the clearing's map, or the beach's map past the
clearing, with four hardware-compared taps, each filtered over 2x2 texels.
Beyond both maps the canopy is taken to let a fifth of the sun through, and the
open beach and sea get all of it. That is multiplied by one tap of the
characters' map, which lets =uBlobFog= of the sun through because the blob's
jelly isn't solid. The characters' tap is offset by a turn hashed from the
point, over a radius that grows with how far the point lies past the
character's middle along the sun (=uGape= is that depth). So the shadow's edge
blurs with distance, as light bent by the jelly spreads out.

=s3light()= is physically based. Its highlight is GGX with Smith's
height-correlated visibility. Its diffuse is rough, using the
energy-preserving Oren-Nayar model (EON, Portsmouth et al. 2024). That model is
flatter than Lambert, brighter with the sun behind the eye, and adds the light
that scatters more than once. The diffuse uses half of each material's
roughness, so sand, moss and bark come to about 0.45. Going rougher would dim
the sun's flecks on the trunks, which we see looking up toward the sun. On top
of that come the yellow-green light that thin leaves let through from behind,
the sky and bounce ambient, and an ambient /sheen/. The sheen is what the
surface mirrors: under the canopy that's the dark trunks round the horizon and
the lit leaves overhead, and on the beach it's the open sky. It comes from
Karis's analytic fit of the pre-integrated GGX, times Lagarde's specular
occlusion.

The terms conserve energy. The highlight and the sheen get back what single
scattering loses on rough surfaces (Turquin's compensation, about 1 + 0.04
rough² for F0 0.04). The diffuse ambient gets what the sheen leaves, a factor
of 1 - E. The ambient also takes the surface's own occlusion through
Jimenez's coloured multi-bounce fit (=s3am(c, alb, ao)=), so bright sand and
lichen turn less grey in their hollows. The screen-space occlusion doesn't know
the albedo, so the share left to it is lightened the same way.

=s3lit= computes the shadow first, so that little else is live across its
texture fetches. Computed later, it pushes programs out of Intel's SIMD16 mode.
The lookup reads the sun's maps 3 cm off the surface on the sun's side, along
the normal times the sign of N·L. The normal faces the eye, so without the sign
a leaf, a feather or a wing seen from its shady side would read its own shadow
and lose the light coming through it. With it, backlit ferns and low leaves glow
yellow-green where the sun comes through them, and the macaws' underwings catch
the light.

=s3light()= widens the roughness by the normal's screen-space variance, a form
of specular anti-aliasing that keeps glints on far leaves from sparkling. Vertex
shaders share the block but have no derivatives, so =s3light= is a macro over
=s3lit()= that only a fragment shader expands. =genshaders.py= drops unused
macros and functions from each shader, so the vertex shaders lose the lighting
and whatever only it calls.

Inside the forest, =s3fog()= is a humid haze that thickens toward the ground.
For the part of a ray that lies out over the beach and the sea, it is thin,
bright sea air that scatters the sky's own light toward the eye. This gives
aerial perspective: the far sea and the far edge of the forest take on the
sky's haze, warmer toward the sun. =s3sky(d)= is that sky, the open sky's
radiance by direction, read from a table that =jl_atmo.frag= draws every frame
([[#light-s3-light-c-jl-and-the-engine-post][Light]]). The sky pass draws it.
The sea, the wet sand and the blob mirror it, and the sea air scatters it.

=s3light()= also keeps the ambient part of what it returns, and =s3fog()= turns
that into =s3a=, the ambient's share of the final colour. Every jungle shader
that lights a surface writes =s3a= in alpha, so the post's ambient occlusion
darkens only that share and the contact shadows darken only the sun's.

The beach has a second shared block, the /surf/ block, in the five shaders that
have to agree on where the water is
([[#the-beach-and-the-sea-s3-beach-c-jw-sea-and-the-sand-in-ju-ground-frag][The beach and the sea]]).

*** Frame fields, sun maps and texture units
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: s3-frame-fields-sun-maps-and-texture-units
:END:

Scene 3 gives the Frame block's fields its own meanings, listed at the top of
=scene3.c=:

- =uViewProj= / =uLightVP=: the camera and the sun (=uViewProj= is =uLightVP=
  in the sun-map passes)
- =uCam= / =uTime=: the eye, and the scene's seconds after the stretch
- =uLightDir= / =uGlow=: the sun's direction and radiance
- =uSkyColor= / =uGroundColor=: the ambient from the canopy and the floor
- =uFogColor= / =uFog=: the haze
- =uTop= / =uBottom=: the sky
- =uWobble=: the wind
- =uClarity=: the macro amount
- =uReal=: the beach part's progress
- =uRim=: the offset from the clearing's sun map to the beach's
- =uSun=: the offset to the characters' map
- =uBlobFog= / =uGape=: the share of the sun the character on show lets through
  (0.3 for the blob, 0 for the ladybug and the tyrannosaurus), and the depth of
  its middle in that map
- =uQ=: 1 in the sun-map passes, -1 in the impostors' bake, 0 otherwise

The sun has three depth maps. All three see the same sun over the same depth
range, so a point has the same depth in each. A scale and an offset take a
point's coordinates in the clearing's map to the others (=uRim=, =uSun=):

- The clearing's map, 2048² over a box 72 m across (3.5 cm texels).
- The beach's map, the same size, round a point 45 m out toward the sea. These
  two are still. A release build draws them only once, for a frozen moment half
  a second before the jungle opens, and the show draws them in its load's
  warm-up.
- The characters' map, 1024², drawn every frame (=S3_CHAR=) in a tight box
  round whichever character is on show. The box is 6 cm across for the ladybug,
  14 m for the tyrannosaurus, and 2.4 m for the blob, or 3.6 m once it carries
  the parasol.

All three are drawn through =main.c='s =shadow_begin= and =shadow_end=, which
Scene 2's sun map uses too. The [[#light-s3-light-c-jl-and-the-engine-post][Light]]
section has the rest: the frozen moment, the gaps cut for the sun, the
characters' colour target, and how the taps are filtered and turned.

The texture units are fixed for the whole scene and all bound once:

- 3: the characters' map's colour, how much of the sun each caster lets through
  (=uSunT=)
- 4-6: the three sun maps (compared)
- 7-8: the first two again, through a sampler that reads raw depth for the
  blocker search
- 9: the copy of the picture the blob's jelly looks through
- 10-12: the baked bark, leaf and sand tiles
- 13-14: the far forest's impostor atlas (colour and depth)
- 15: the sky's radiance table

The demo's 32³ noise volume sits on unit 1's 3D binding, which nothing else
uses, since the post chain binds only 2D textures there. The shaders' value
noise (=vn3=) is a single fetch from it. =noise_make= in =main.c= makes it first
thing at load, so the bakes and the seabed's placement can read it too.
After the scene is drawn, the post passes use units 0-6.

** The trees (=s3_trees.c=, =jt.vert= / =jt.frag=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-trees-s3-trees-c-jt-vert-jt-frag
:END:

=s3_tree_sites= places up to 300 trees. Eight hero trunks come first, set by yaw
and distance where the look round frames them. Rings of jittered sites follow
out to about 78 m, with giant buttressed broadleaves, palms and slender trees.
Last come up to 140 understory palms and saplings (2.5-11 m), packed thickly
round the clearing but clear of the viewer's path, the hero plant and the sky
window. The beach part's own trees follow (below). Each frame the C side picks
the trees in view, nearest first and grouped by kind, and issues one instanced
draw per part and kind. The parts are:

- lobed trunks that flare into plank buttresses
- limbs
- lianas and strangler roots spiralling up the giants
- crown leaf cards
- palm fronds with drooping leaflets
- bromeliad and fern rosettes perched on the giants
- curtains of hanging leaves
- the far forest that every gap at eye level ends in

=jt.frag= cuts the leaves out of their cards. A card has two offset layers, and
each leaf is tilted, veined and tinted on its own. Palm fronds are combs of 44
leaflets a side. Where the leaflets would come closer than about 3 px, they thin
out by nested halves (44, 22, 11, 5.5), keeping every other leaflet and widening
the ones kept, because finer leaflets flicker under the jitter. The sun's map
uses 15. =jt.frag= also gives the bark its plates, lichen and moss, and turns
leaves under about 2 px into stable clumps. Distant trees drop their lianas,
epiphytes and planks and trade their cards for bigger ones, but only in the
camera's passes. In the sun's map every tree keeps all its parts.

The planks grow out of the trunk's own centre line at each height (=tc(y /
top())=), so they flare from it as buttresses and follow its lean. A giant's
trunk wanders up to 0.07 H off its foot (2.8 m on a 40 m tree), so planks set
out round the foot would stand clear of the trunk in the air.

A plank is a slab 20-40 cm thick, thickest low down, with a rounded crest. The
slab's middle line climbs to one radius below the top edge by the shoulders and
stays there while the crest rounds over it. The section's normal is
normalize(cos across, s³ + s / 2 up), where s is the sine of the angle round
it. So the normal is level at mid-height and leans up on the upper flank, where
the moss gathers as on the trunk, and down on the lower one. Over the crest it
turns up and catches the canopy's light. Planks get 8 columns round (16 on the
near grid), so a vertex sits on each shoulder and on the crest. Seen end on, a
plank is a solid, rounded fin.

A plank's occlusion also deepens in the corner where it leaves the trunk, from
half the ambient right at the trunk to all of it 0.5 m out. It goes by how far
the point stands out of the trunk. =fin()= hands that distance over, and the
same distance lays the bark tile along the plank, one tile a metre. Neighbouring
planks look like separate fins with shaded grooves between them. The planks
cast sun shadows in the still maps too. They shade each other, the trunk they
leave and the floor, and a plank on the far side of its neighbour from the sun
lies in its shadow. At 20-40 cm thick they hold in the maps' 3.5 cm texels
without acne, and since the maps are drawn once, the shadows cost nothing per
frame.

In the picture, a giant's crown cards cut 9x9 leaves, about 0.45 m each. From
the floor 20-40 m below, coarser leaves look like paint chips against the sky.
The sun's map uses 6x6, and slender trees keep 4x4. Seen from below, a giant's
card also has a solid heart in the picture. Within a ragged disc that reaches
30-70% of the way to the card's rim, where neither layer has a leaf, a third and
deeper layer of smaller leaves shows (1.6 times the grid, with the mip to
match). This layer is darker (occlusion 0.2-0.45) and lets little light through
(0.1). Each clump is then a dark mass of foliage edged with lit, translucent
leaves, as a real crown looks from below. Cards whose leaves fall back to clumps
keep the heart, the sun's map keeps every gap, and the far forest's impostors
match because the same program bakes them.

The wind is one gust field for the whole scene, =gust(p, l)=. The same text is
in =jt.vert=, =ju_plant.vert= and =jb_leaf.vert=, and the shader baker stores it
once. It models a sea breeze blowing inland, with fronts about 31 m apart moving
at 4 m/s. The fronts are bent across the wind and broken into patches that drift
along them, shaped into gusts and calm spells, and scaled by the slow, global
=uWobble=. =l= is a delay, asking for the field as it was =l= seconds earlier.

Each tree bends downwind with the gust at its foot, 0.6 s late. The CPU works
that gust out once per tree (=jt_gust=, the same formula) and hands it over in
the fractional part of the tree's seed, so no vertex evaluates the field. The
bend follows a profile that is quadratic low down and about linear above 12 m,
and a 35 m giant's crown moves about 0.3 m in a full gust. The tree's own sway
and its leaves' flutter grow with the gust, and fronds move from the root out.
Toward the forest's edge the breeze is stronger, three times as strong for palms
and 1.5 times for other trees.

The far billboards work out the gust at their own feet, so their sway never
depends on which trees were culled. The undergrowth leans and flutters with the
gust at its root. The hero plant's leaves bob one after another as a gust
passes, 0.15 s apart, except the hero leaf the ladybug walks on. The still sun
maps hold the wind frozen at one moment
([[#light-s3-light-c-jl-and-the-engine-post][Light]]). The sway is small enough
that the crowns' still shadows don't visibly disagree with them.

The trees fill most of the frame, so they light themselves with a cheaper
=jlit()=. Next to =s3light()=, leaves get a plain glint in place of the GGX
highlight and bark gets none, and there's no sky sheen. The glint broadens
and its peak falls as the normal varies across a pixel, keeping distant leaves
steadier in motion. The shadow is two
hardware-filtered taps from the clearing's sun map, or the beach's past it, in
place of the penumbra search. The pair of taps, 2.5 texels apart, is turned at
random from point to point ([[#light-s3-light-c-jl-and-the-engine-post][Light]]).
It reads the map on the sun's side by the common block's rule (above), which
=jlit= writes as =faceforward(N, -L, N)=, so backlit crown leaves, palm leaflets
and curtains glow yellow-green against the sky. Bark gets the rough diffuse at
0.45, and the waxy leaves stay Lambert.

=jlit='s ambient adds the haze's golden light from the sun's side, =uGlow= ×
0.06 × (0.5 + 0.5 N·L)³, because the humid air glows gold toward the sun. In the
shade, which covers most of a trunk, the canopy's ambient lights every side of a
vertical surface alike. With the golden term, a trunk rounds from gold to
green-grey across its width, the bark's relief shows, and the planks' faces
toward the light glow. On a trunk's sunward flank the term nearly doubles the
ambient. It counts as ambient, so the screen-space occlusion still darkens it.

At load =jt_bake.frag= renders three material tiles once, and they stay bound on
texture units 10-12. Each is 512², RGBA8, mipmapped and repeat-wrapped, with
every channel periodic and 4x anisotropic filtering.

The bark tile is about 0.9 m square. It holds the height, a moss and lichen
mask, and the height's two slopes, baked so the lit pass needs no extra taps.
Its pattern is ridges wandering up the trunk, stretched Voronoi flakes with
cracked rims, and lenticels. Trunks wrap it 8 times round a giant and 4 times
round the rest, limbs and lianas map it by their own coordinates, and planks by
the distance out of the trunk (above). Two sines of the height warp the tile's
coordinates, shifting them round the trunk and stretching them along it, so its
repeats never line up, and this takes no second fetch. Moss and lichen come from
the mask at a quarter scale.

Each tree's bark takes a tone between a pale buff (0.19, 0.16, 0.12) and a dark
grey-green (0.10, 0.10, 0.08), chosen by its random number. A tree's planks
share their trunk's tone (=jt.vert= hands them piece 0's random), so they look
like the same wood, with the same moss and lichen. The tone is streaked where
rain runs down: the mask is fetched once more, stretched about 10 m up the trunk
and 1 m round it, and scales the tone by 0.4-1.6. Lichen crusts are pale and
thicker on some trunks, and the moss is a bright green.

The leaf sheet holds four 256 px variants of veins, tint, thickness and shape,
with a drip tip, a finely toothed margin, one-sided insect bites, a midrib and
side veins, and yellowed margins. The cards and curtains cut their leaves out of
it, one variant per leaf. The sand tile, 2 m square, belongs to the beach
(below). In each case a texture read replaces noise loops, and the mipmaps keep
the detail from shimmering at any distance.

Within 6 m the bark gets a one-step parallax offset by the 3 cm relief under the
point. Within 25 m (fading from 18 m) the baked slopes tilt the bark's normal,
so the plates, ridges and cracks shade in the sun's flecks and the haze's light.
That range takes in most of the trunks in the look round and at the vantage. As
the slopes shrink on screen, the mipmaps and the anisotropic filter average them
away, so they shade without sparkling.

Trunks and plank buttresses that are ever seen within 12 m are drawn on a finer
grid, with twice the sides and more than twice the rings, so no facets show
close up. The trunk's normal also leans with its lobes and flutes. Slender trees
ever seen within 10 m grow modelled leaves in place of cards, 24 cards' worth of
10 leaves each. Every leaf is a drooping strip folded along its midrib, cut out
of the leaf sheet and lit as a leaf with its real normal.

A tree sheds detail with distance. It drops its lianas past 20 m, its epiphytes
past 15 m, its planks past 12 and 25 m, and its outer limbs past 30 m. Past
35 m it drops half its cards (the rest get larger) and half its fronds. The
distance is the nearest the tree is ever seen from, not the current one. The
camera's path is fixed, so at load =s3_tree_lod= steps the whole scene at 0.1 s
with =s3_setup= and works out the nearest each tree's foot ever comes to the eye
while the tree is in view. That distance picks all of the tree's detail once, so
each tree keeps one level of detail for the whole scene and nothing pops in or
out as the camera moves. It reaches =jt.vert= as the last of the tree's eight
=uT= floats, and =trees_draw= sorts by it. The impostors' bake uses the true
distance.

The far forest is baked at load too. The first four hero trees go into an
atlas: the two giants (44 and 40 m), the slender tree (34 m) and a palm (15 m).
Each is moved far out of every sun map and drawn from eight sides by the same
=jt= program at full detail (=uQ= = -1). The atlas holds 8x4 views of 512x768
(21 texels a metre, 4096x3072 in all), with lit colour (RGBA16F) and depth. Each
view is drawn in an orthographic box fitted to its tree, under Scene 3's fixed
sun and sky with the haze off. Since the sun and sky never move, every side is
lit correctly.

In the picture, 240 billboards stand 46-110 m out, drawn nearest first. Each
faces the eye and blends the two baked views either side of the direction it is
seen from. The baked depth, read through a linear filter, says how much of each
pixel the tree covers, since a tree stands about 0.3-0.7 deep in its box and
empty texels are at 1. The coverage is cut with a per-pixel, per-frame dither
between 0.1 and 0.9 of a texel's coverage, as the spray's drops are, so the TAA
spreads each outline over the filtered texel. The cut stays above 0.1, because a
pixel with almost no coverage would be divided by about 0 and show as a black
block. The haze is added live.

A wall of 40 more quads, 118 m out, closes off the distance with a canopy lost
in the haze. It draws a ragged crown line, with crowns in three sizes: 12, 6 and
3 m across their cells, and at most 7, 3.5 and 1.75 m high. The small ones are
clumped on the big ones. Each crown takes its cell's hash for both its width
and its height (=sqrt(g - w²) (0.4 + g)=), so wide, tall crowns stand among
narrow, low ones and gaps, and no row of matching arches forms against the sky.
Their edges are ragged leaves at two scales, the second octave three times finer
(about 0.3 m). The wall is darker in the trunks' depths, lighter toward the
sunlit tops, and mottled.

In the beach part, the wall's quads more than 40 m out toward the sea are
dropped, or its end would stand over the water past the cove's flank as a grey
slab. The far forest keeps each quad's flat depth, because writing
=gl_FragDepth= would turn off the early depth test and cost about 3 ms. A far
quad that is culled goes as a whole: =NM= is zeroed first, so its corners all
land on the origin and the leaves' flutter can't spread them. The wall's quads
are judged at their middle. Judged corner by corner, a quad at the cove's edge
would keep one side at 118 m and pull the other to the clearing's middle, as a
wedge of wall across the look round.

The beach part reshapes the forest without moving it. =s3_tree_sites= places
the 300 forest sites as for the forest alone, then marks the ones that have to
go as gone (kind 3) without removing them. Every other tree keeps its index and
with it its seed, so the shots before the glide don't depend on the beach part.
Trees in the camera's glide (=s3_roam_dist=) step aside across it. Past the
edge, a cove 48 m wide is cleared, along with the tyrannosaurus's track and the
view out from the vantage. That view is cleared wider on its left, so nothing
stands in front of where the tyrannosaurus is first seen. Fourteen hand-placed
sites follow (=S3_MAXTREE= 320):

- the pair of giants, 10.5 m apart, that frame the view from the vantage
- a third giant left of them (28 m out, 9.5 m left), and the beast shows between
  it and the left one of the pair before it crosses the middle of the frame
- four giants along the edge, whose crowns shade the track
- two palms leaning out from the edge, and five on the sand leaning out to sea

The far forest opens across the cove, and round the cove's flanks too once the
camera heads for the beach, so the view out ends in sky and sea.

** The forest floor and the undergrowth (=s3_under.c=, =ju_*=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-forest-floor-and-the-undergrowth-s3-under-c-ju
:END:

The floor is a polar grid of 160 sectors and 80 rings round the viewer's spot,
lifted onto =s3h()=. Its rings are a couple of centimetres apart at the centre
and spread out geometrically to about 170 m. It's drawn after the plants, so
whatever stands on it hides it early, and it takes sun shadows but casts none.
=ju_ground.frag= stacks drifts of fallen leaves and twigs over damp humus. The
leaves come in three sizes, the smallest only close up, from fresh ochre to
rotting brown with the odd red one, and each piece is cut, veined and curled.
Moss carpets the rises and the trunks' feet, where the floor also sits in shade.
Once a leaf is smaller than a pixel, the detail fades to its average colour.
The fine soil and moss noise also blends toward its mean as the pixel's
footprint grows, so unresolved grains do not crawl across the floor.

Every plant is a bent strip in =ju_plant.vert=. The shader scatters 4,500 grass
tufts of 16 blades by itself. The rest come from a table of up to 1,000 entries,
placed on the CPU nearest first and clear of the trunks, the hero plant and a
polyline through the camera's keys. They are ferns with curling tips, broad
leaves (heart, arrow, holed monstera, torn banana-like paddles) and
red-and-yellow heliconias. =ju_plant.frag= cuts each leaf's outline, veins and
colour, and the cut-outs apply in the shadow pass too. Plants within 25 m cast
sun shadows. In the picture every plant is drawn on its fine grid for the whole
scene, so none changes shape as the eye comes by, and the sun's map gets the
coarse ones. For the beach part the table is filtered after placement, so the
clearing keeps its plants:

- nothing grows on the sand
- the undergrowth thins out toward the edge
- nothing tall stands in the camera's glide
- no broad leaf stands within 4.2 m of where the camera crouches for the
  tyrannosaurus. From 0.48 m up on a long lens, a seedling's leaf 4 m from the
  eye shows as a near-black two-faceted "clam" under the beast.
- sixty low creepers trail along the top of the beach

Near the beach vantage, rooted ferns bow on the tyrannosaurus's thirteen
footfalls, starting at jungle 52.26 s. The bend falls off with distance from
a point 18 m along the beach's frame, reaching about 0.25 radians at its
strongest. It changes the frond's arc itself, so the tangent normal follows
the bend. The cached vegetation shadows keep their static pose.

The growl at jungle 58.9 s releases four broad leaves from the right canopy,
0.15 s apart. Each is about 0.3 m long and uses two of the existing plant
strips, eight instances in one extra draw. They rotate and flutter into view
from above, then fall below the floor before their draw ends at 63 s. They
use opaque leaf geometry and add no shadow pass.

The beach's sand reads the baked sand tile, which holds 8 cm wind ripples over
broader swells in patches, the grain's tone, and specks of bright shell and dark
minerals. Its tiling is broken up with iq's third technique (=tnt()=). A slow
noise picks two of eight random offsets of the tile and blends them with a
height-biased step, so no 2 m repeat shows across the beach. Within 8 m, a
four-step parallax occlusion march along the view into the ripples' 1.5 cm
relief makes them stand up off the surface. Derivatives for the tile's footprint
are taken outside any per-pixel branch, as in =jt.frag=, because inside a branch
they are undefined and a frame would come out differently from run to run.

It's a bright, humid day. The sun stands about 48° up, to the front-left of the

*** The sky and the air (=jl_sky.frag=, =jl_atmo.frag=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-sky-and-the-air-jl-sky-frag-jl-atmo-frag
:END:

=jl_sky.frag= draws the sky as a full-screen pass on the far plane. It runs
after the world, so only the gaps in the leaves pay for it. It draws =s3sky=, a
limb-darkened sun disc, its aureole and its glare. There is no separate cloud
pass or cloud history buffer in the current build.

The open sky is a planet's atmosphere and not a gradient. =jl_atmo.frag= marches
it every frame into a 64x32 RGBA16F table on texture unit 15. The table's
columns go by the angle round from the sun's bearing. Its rows go by the square
root of the height above the horizon, so it's finest near the horizon, where the
haze changes fastest. Everything that looks at the open sky reads the table
through =s3sky()=: the sky pass, the reflections in the sea and the wet sand,
and the haze over the beach.

After the physical scattering, the same table blends in broad, thin wisps
from two smooth value-noise octaves. They drift slowly with time and stay
near the horizon: they fade up just above it and out between about 7° and
17° elevation, with at most a 0.32 blend. This adds work only at the table's
2,048 texels, and the existing sky lookups put the same wisps in the sea and
wet sand. They need no additional render target, history buffer or cloud
shadow pass.

Each texel is a ray from the ground through the air in 24 steps, closer together
near the eye, with Hillaire's 2020 constants:

- Rayleigh scattering (scale height 8 km)
- Mie scattering with a Cornette-Shanks phase (g 0.8, which gives the sun's
  aureole), 10% absorbed, with a scale height of 1.2 km. Its turbidity is the
  humid tropics': three times the standard in the forest, easing to the
  standard over the glide out toward the beach (=uReal=).
- ozone absorption peaking 25 km up
- the sun's light through flat layers (it stands 48° up)
- a small isotropic share for multiple scattering

The table costs about 0.05 ms a frame.

*** The sun's shadow maps
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-sun-shadow-maps
:END:

The sun's main shadow map is a fixed 2048² box covering 36 m round the clearing,
with 3.5 cm texels. A second map of the same size (=uShadow2=, texture unit 5)
covers the beach and the forest's edge round a point 45 m out. It sees the same
sun from the same depths (60-260 m), slid across. A scale and an offset in
=uRim= take a point's clearing-map coordinates to the beach map's, and
=s3shadow()= reads the beach map where the clearing's runs out. Beyond both maps
the forest's canopy is taken to let a fifth of the sun through, and the open
beach and sea get all of it.

The sun and the casters stand still, so the release build draws both maps once,
for one frozen moment: jungle time -0.5 s, the first frame the show draws under
the white. Whichever frame draws them, the whole Frame block is set for that
moment and then put back. That fixes the wind, the eye position that turns the
cards and thins the curtains of lianas, and the beach part's clearing. So the
maps come out the same whether the show, a still, a seek or the load's warm-up
draws them first. In practice the warm-up does (see
[[file:architecture.org::#the-demo-as-a-case-study][The demo as a case study]]).
The dev build redraws them every frame so hot reloads show.

A 12-texel patch over the hero leaf is cleared to a depth just under it, so a
fleck of sun lands on the leaf and the ladybug and a shaft stands over them in
the haze. For the sun's maps alone (=uQ=), =jt.vert= also drops the crown cards
within 8 m of the sun's ray through a point 2.5 m above the tyrannosaurus's
stopping place. That opens a gap in the canopy, out of view above, and puts a
pool of sun on its track, which it walks into out of the shade. From the forest
we see it near its terminator, and without the pool its dark hide would be a
flat cut-out. The same pool lights the top of the beach, so the foreground of
the beach shots is part sun and part palm and crown shade. The maps are drawn
once, so none of this costs anything per frame.

*** The characters' shadow map
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-characters-shadow-map
:END:

Things that move can't cast into maps drawn once. So a third map (=uShadow3=,
unit 6), 1024², is drawn every frame (=S3_CHAR=) in a tight box round whoever is
on show. That's 6 cm across for the ladybug while the macro rig is in, then 14 m
round the tyrannosaurus's hips, then 2.4 m round the blob, widening to 3.6 m once
it holds its things. The map has the same sun and depth range, slid across the
sun's rays, so =uSun= takes clearing-map coordinates to it. =s3shadow()=
multiplies in one tap of it, with its normal offset and bias scaled to the box.
So the ladybug's feet, the tyrannosaurus and the blob cast real shadows on
whatever lies under them, and on themselves. It costs 0.1-0.4 ms. With no one on
show there is no pass, and every lookup lands outside the box.

The map has an 8-bit colour target too, which takes 1 MB. The characters'
programs draw into it with one colour output, as they draw into the picture, so
the driver reuses the builds it made at load. With a depth-only target, Mesa's
Intel driver rebuilds each program at its first draw, and with a cold shader
cache that drops a frame as each character comes on screen. In the sun's map
their shaders return at once and write a constant, so the colour costs nothing.
For the ladybug and the tyrannosaurus it's 0. The blob writes how much of the
sun each part lets through (=uSunT=, unit 3): 1 for its jelly and
eyes, 0.5 for its parasol's cloth and 0 for the rest of what it carries (see
[[#the-blob-ashore-s3-blob-c-jo-blob][The blob ashore]]).

The jelly lets 0.3 of the sun through (=uBlobFog=, set while the blob is on
show). Its shadow's edge softens with the distance past the blob's middle along
the sun's rays (=uGape=), as the light the jelly bends spreads out. The soft edge
comes from one tap, turned by the point's hash, which the TAA averages. The
ladybug's and the tyrannosaurus's shadows stay opaque and sharp.

The post's contact shadows stay on round the blob. The jelly, its eyes and its
drops write an ambient share of 1, and the contact-shadow pass skips any pixel
at 0.999 and over, so they darken only the sand round its foot. There's no
caustic focus in the shadow. The map is centred on the blob's steady line and
not on its hops, so a focus would slide out of the shadow on every hop.

*** How the shadows are filtered
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: how-the-shadows-are-filtered
:END:

All three maps are compared in hardware (=GL_TEXTURE_COMPARE_MODE=, linear
filtering), so each tap is a 2x2 filtered compare. A sampler object reads the two
still maps' raw depths (units 7 and 8) for the soft shadows' blocker search,
taking the nearest texel both when magnifying and minifying. The search's taps
are turned by a per-pixel hash, so =texture()='s implicit level of detail mostly
minifies them. But in a quad where a cut-out leaf has discarded some lanes, the
derivatives read whatever those lanes held, which is undefined. With linear
magnification those pixels would switch filter at random, and Scene 3's frames
wouldn't repeat from run to run (see
[[file:architecture.org::#determinism][Determinism]]).

=s3shadow()= turns its four blocker and four filter taps by a hash of the
point's world position, and the trees' =jlit()= turns its two by the same hash.
That looks fixed per point, but the TAA's jitter moves the point under each
pixel every frame, and the hash is chaotic far below a pixel. So the turns
change every frame (eight jitter spots, eight turns per pixel) and the history
averages them. The trees' pair of taps sits 2.5 texels apart along the turned
direction. Fixed taps would draw the map's 3.5 cm texels as stair-steps and
double edges across the sun's flecks on trunks lit at a grazing angle. Turned,
they give soft, round edges.

The soft shadows' narrowest filter is 0.0003 uv (0.6 texel), for a blocker
within about 2.4 m. It stays that narrow so the contact shadows under close
leaves stay sharp.

*** Light shafts and haze (=jl_vol.frag=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: light-shafts-and-haze-jl-vol-frag
:END:

For the light shafts (=jl_vol.frag=), each view ray is marched through the haze
in 24 steps at quarter resolution, against the sun's map, out to at most 50 m.
The haze scatters strongly forward (Henyey-Greenstein with g 0.7, plus an
isotropic share) and is thicker near the ground. A ray glows as the square of
how much of it the sun lights. That's a stylised contrast, which keeps the real
beams and all but removes the dappled veil between them.

The lit share is measured over at least 8 m of air. A short ray to something
near, inside a beam, is lit all the way, so on its own it would square to 1. It
would then take more veil than the longer, partly shaded ray past it, and a
morpho in a sunbeam would wash out pale against the trunk behind it. So the
stretch past the surface counts for the share only. Samples under the floor
aren't air and count for nothing, so the floor and what stands on it keep their
glow. The glow itself sums only the air in front of the surface, with its last
step taken in part, so there are no contour bands.

Past the sun's map, where the canopy is unknown, the air counts as all but
shaded. A little light there would give every distant pixel the same milky
glow. The engine blurs the result depth-aware (=mc_renderer_blur=) and adds it
at the grade (=mc_post.vol_tex=, =vol=) in gold: the sun's light times
(1, 0.9, 0.65), scattering 0.24 per metre at the ground.

The haze (=s3fog= in the common block) is humid air lit two ways. From above it
takes the canopy's green light, a deep teal-green in the shade (=uFogColor=
0.09, 0.19, 0.17) that gets brighter the higher you look. It also scatters the
sun's light forward, so toward the sun it glows gold (=uGlow= × 0.012 /
(1.3 - cos θ)², with θ the angle from the sun). The opening shots look into the
light and so stand in backlit air, while looking away the haze stays cool and
deep. It's dense enough (=uFog= 0.011 per metre, thicker near the ground) that
the trunks recede in clear layers, each farther one lighter and closer to the
air's colour. Out over the beach and the sea the air is thin sea air, which
takes the sky's colour from =s3sky()=.

The shafts fade out past the forest's edge, because the open air over the
beach carries no veil. The current build omits the earlier version's 7,000
dust and pollen quads and their two shaders.

*** The grade and the depth of field
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-grade-and-the-depth-of-field
:END:

=light_post= sets up the engine's post chain (see
[[file:architecture.org::#the-renderer-and-its-post-stack][The renderer and its
post stack]]). The grade has ground-truth ambient occlusion in the wide shots
(on each pixel's ambient share, with contact shadows on the sun's share),
saturation 1.12, a light vignette and grain at 0.008. The exposure is 1.0 in the
forest's wide shots, easing to 0.8 up close and to 0.8 again for the beach part.
=render_scene3= adds the reveal's glare and the take-off's swell on top.

Looking out from the forest, the exposure stays set for the forest, so the beach
glows past the dark trunks and the tyrannosaurus is a shape against it. A
tension envelope rises from =S3_GAP= (jungle 50.3 s) to =S3_REX= + 3 s
(55.3 s), holds through the head turn, then fades from =S3_SHORE= (60 s) to
=S3_CLEAR= (67.7 s). At full strength it lowers exposure by 18%, subtracts
0.13 from saturation and adds 0.18 to the vignette. Once the animal
has gone and the camera steps out toward the trees' last row, the exposure
adapts to the sun. It does so between =S3_CLEAR= (232.3 s) and 4 s after
=S3_BLOB= (240.1 s), ending at about 0.42, while the saturation rises to 1.24.

The depth of field (=mc_post.dof=, =focus=) is the engine's quarter-resolution
bokeh gather. The macro amount drives it, up to 32 px of blur at 720 lines on
the ladybug, and it eases off in the seconds after the take-off. The focus is
the distance to the ladybug. The gather opens small, bright points out of focus
into soft, round discs: the sun in a dew drop behind the ladybug, the glints on
its raised wing cases. It also spreads the hero plant's blurred
stems and leaves over the sharp ferns behind them as the gaze comes down. How
both work is told under the depth of field in
[[file:architecture.org::#the-renderer-and-its-post-stack][The renderer and its
post stack]].

On the beach the lens lengthens, and the depth of field behaves like a long
lens's. =render_scene3= adds a blur at infinity that falls off as one over the
focus distance: 0.015 × the picture's height / (focus × tan(fov/2)) pixels. It
fades in over the blob's first 4 s, so the glimpse and the tyrannosaurus stay
sharp. The focus is the blob's distance, so the blob is always sharp. Behind it
as it waves, the blur is about 5 px at 1080 lines, so the surf and the horizon
soften and the blob stands out. The sand at our feet softens the same
way. In the last wide view it's about 1.5 px. The quarter-resolution pass runs
through the whole beach act.

** The hero plant and the ladybug (=s3_bug.c=, =jb_*=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-hero-plant-and-the-ladybug-s3-bug-c-jb
:END:

=jb_leaf.vert= grows seven glossy elephant-ear leaves on long stalks, each blade
or stalk a 40x16 quad grid. The hero leaf is 0.40 m long and holds still. Its
shape is mirrored on the CPU by =jb_leaf_at=, so the ladybug's walk and the
camera's macro rig land on its surface. =jb_leaf.frag= gives the blades a pale
midrib, curving side veins, finer veins and a net of tiny cells about 1.4 mm
across. All of them are recessed into a waxy surface by derivative bump mapping.
The bumps fade out where a millimetre is smaller than a pixel. The blades glow
when the sun is behind them.

The wax mirrors the gaps in the canopy at the brightness of the open sky at the
horizon (=uBottom=), as broad, soft sheens blurred by the blade's roughness of
0.28. Every vein groove and the net of cells is tilted out of the mirror
direction, so each one cuts a sharp dark line through a sheen. That's how the
fine relief catches the light, as it does on a real waxy leaf. With dimmer gaps
the wax would mirror next to nothing at the macro rig's 20-34°, and the leaf
would look like flat paint. Under the ladybug the leaf takes a contact shade
and a sun shadow traced against the bug's shell, which the 3.5 cm shadow map
could never resolve.

The ladybug is about 8 mm long. =jb_bug.vert= builds it in millimetres from one
unit sphere. The shader also makes the sphere, from =gl_VertexID=, as 12x12
cells on each face of a cube, with grid lines on the planes where the wing cases
fold. Each instance is one squashed or folded copy of the sphere, and together
they make:

- red wing cases with the seven spots
- the black shield with its cream shoulder patches
- cream cheeks and glossy eyes
- a ribbed belly
- six three-segment legs, brown at their joints, stepping in a tripod gait
- clubbed antennae that twitch now and then
- three dew drops on the leaf

The bug is lacquered. A clear coat over both the red and the black mirrors the
canopy's gaps as sharp windows of the open sky, at six times =s3sky=. The window
round the sun is the gap its fleck of light comes through. The coat also catches
the sun as a hard spark, which reads the share of sun =s3lit= found and so needs
no shadow lookup of its own. Both are laid on at half weight, so you see the red
first and the gloss second. At full weight the windows and the spark would cover
the raised wing cases in white at the take-off. The weight is 0.5 on the shell,
the black parts and the legs, and 0.6 on the eyes.

The red is the pigment under the coat: albedo (0.4, 0.016, 0.005), darker toward
the rim, with roughness 0.7. Its gloss comes from the coat. A brighter red would
sit on the tone curve's shoulder, which rolls bright colours toward white, and
come out salmon. The black parts take the coat at roughness 0.4, like glossy
chitin, and the shoulder patches and cheeks are a cream of about 0.5.

When the wing cases are raised for the flight they let the sun through, at
=s3lit='s translucency of 0.4. Seen from below as the bug flies off, they glow
orange-red round the black body.

The dew drops are dark at the rim, where the view bends out of them toward the
dark canopy. Their colour falls off as n·v³, scaled by 2.4 so the core keeps its
brightness, and that makes them look like water instead of lime gel.

=s3_bug_at()= gives its pose. The bug sets off as we come in, at 185.7 s, stops
to look round from 194.5 to 196.8 s, and arrives at 202.3 s as it gets ready to
fly. In that time it walks 77.7 mm along the leaf, weaving a little beside the
midrib. =jb_path()= takes its heading from the path, so it faces the way it
walks and doesn't crab sideways.

Its feet stay planted. While a foot is down it slides back under the body on a
triangle wave, at the same speed as the body goes forward, and then it swings
forward in an arch half a millimetre high. The gait's phase is the distance
walked: 245 rad for the whole walk, or 39 strides of 2 mm. So the bug stops on
all six feet at the look round and at the end. A planted foot drifts
0.3-0.7 mm/s. In the show each leg steps at most about 3.2 times a second before
the look round and 5.3 after it.

At =S3_FLY= (203.2 s) the wing cases lift and spread. The smoky, iridescent hind
wings unfold and start to beat, drawn as eight faint copies spread over the
stroke to give the motion blur of an 85 Hz beat. At 204.9 s, 1.7 s later, the
bug tucks up its legs and lifts off. It flies up and away toward the light, nose
high and weaving a little. The macro rig stays where it was and tilts up to
watch it go (see [[#the-camera][The camera]]).

** Birds and butterflies (=s3_birds.c=, =jv_bird.*=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: birds-and-butterflies-s3-birds-c-jv-bird
:END:

One instanced call draws 17 creatures, and =jv_bird.vert= generates all their
routes, shapes and wingbeats:

- the four scarlet macaws that cross the canopy gap, with red bodies, long
  blue-tipped tails, and red and dull old gold underwings. Each wing's hand
  tapers to a point, with its five primaries splayed into fingers at the tip.
- nine green parrots higher up, going the other way
- a far pair between the trunks during the look round
- two blue morphos fluttering past near the eye. They have no tail, because a
  tail strip would have zero length and zero normals, and the NaN that gives
  would flash the frame black (see the
  [[#engineering-notes][engineering notes]]).

They're drawn until 1.5 s after the gaze starts down from the canopy
(=S3_FIND=, 181.9 s).

A morpho's wing is a fan from the thorax. =jv_bird.vert= runs u out from the
thorax and v round it, from the leading edge, which is swept 0.25 rad forward,
back along the abdomen to -1.45 rad. So the forewing's apex leads and the
hindwing tucks in behind, as on a real morpho. The root is a 0.15-long stretch
of the thorax and not a point, so no normal comes out zero. A zero normal would
give a NaN, and the bloom would spread it. The wing beats from -52° to +75°, 3.5
times a jungle second, which is about three times a second in the show. The
outer wing lags the root by 0.7 rad of the stroke, so the wing bends as it beats
and its shading runs across it.

=jv_bird.frag= cuts the outline round the fan: the forewing's apex and outer
edge, a notch, then the round, scalloped hindwing. The cut is a ramp one pixel
either side of the outline, with the distance in pixels taken from =fwidth=. It
is dithered per pixel and frame like the spray's drops, so the TAA blends a
covered fraction there. A hard cut would stair-step along the scallops and the
apex as the wings beat, because it moves several pixels a frame, more than the
jitter alone can resolve.

The shader paints the top of the wing metallic blue inside a broad black rim.
The underside is a cool dark brown (0.06, 0.045, 0.04). It is dark at the root,
paler in a band round a row of five cream-ringed eyespots with white pupils, and
darker again at the margin, at 0.36, 1.6 and 0.84 of the brown. So a wing seen
from below looks like a wing. The brown is nearly neutral, so it takes the
beam's gold as a tint, and the rings are cream (0.4, 0.36, 0.28). A warmer,
paler brown looks ochre in the golden beams. Because the shafts measure their
lit share over at least 8 m (see
[[#light-shafts-and-haze-jl-vol-frag][Light shafts and haze]]), a morpho in a sunbeam shows dark wings, eyespots and a dark body
against the lit trunk behind it.

Feathers let 0.4 of the sun through, and a morpho's scaled wings 0.1. The
wingbeat has a power stroke, a folded recovery and the odd glide, and the birds
bank into their swerves. Every creature enters and leaves off-screen.

** The beach and the sea (=s3_beach.c=, =jw_sea.*=, and the sand in =ju_ground.frag=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-beach-and-the-sea-s3-beach-c-jw-sea-and-the-sand-in-ju-ground-frag
:END:

The sea that draws the waves, the sand they run up, the reflections in them and
the blob coming out of them must all agree on where the water is. So the four
shaders that need it, =jw_sea.vert=, =jw_sea.frag=, =ju_ground.frag= and
=jo_blob.frag=, carry a second shared GLSL block, the surf
block, after the s3 common block (see [[#how-it-put-together][How it's put
together]]). It sits between its own marker comments (=// ---- surf:= ...
=// ---- end surf ----=), it is byte for byte the same in all four, and the
beach package owns it. To change it, edit one copy and copy it over the others.
The shader baker stores it once. It holds the swell:

- =jwph=: its phase. The swell comes in every 7 s (6.98 s) and slows as
  the sand shelves under it, by =c = √(g d)= with the depth growing 6 cm per
  metre past the waterline. So its phase is the integral of the wavenumber, and
  crests 76 m apart far out bunch up to about 9 m apart at the waterline. They
  come in at a slight angle and bend over the bars. The phase is shifted by the
  tyrannosaurus's hold (=S3_HOLD=, 7.74 s), so the waves keep time with the
  blob's play.
- =jwg=: the sets. A slow envelope over the wave count makes the waves come in
  sets of bigger and smaller ones.
- =jws=: the swell's state at a point, meaning its height, how far past breaking
  it is, its phase and the wave's height. A wave grows as the water shallows, by
  Green's law (=H ∝ d^-1/4=). It is 0.3 m where the water is 10 m deep, times
  the set, times a variation along the crest that drifts from wave to wave. It
  grows until it is 0.78 of the depth, which is depth-limited breaking, 20%
  sooner or later over the bars. The crest is higher in places, so each wave
  breaks first at its peaks and the break peels along it. Before it breaks, its
  crest sharpens and its face steepens, and after, it is a bore half the depth
  high, with a long back and a steep front. The sharpening (=m= = 0.5) is the
  most the sea's grid can follow, and the vertices and the shading use the same
  value, so the silhouette and the normals agree.
- =jwr=: the swash. It gives where the water's edge is now (=.x=), how far this
  wave's sheet runs, and the time since its bore reached the waterline. Each
  spent wave runs up the slope as a thin sheet in 1.75 s and drains over the
  next 4.5 s. The sheet runs 3 m times the wave's size, in lobes along the
  beach.
- =jwfilm=: the film a draining sheet leaves on the sand, from its highest reach
  down to the retreating edge. It soaks in before the next sheet comes.
- =jwbed=: the seabed out past the surf, sand with dark meadows of seagrass from
  55-62 m out. The floor and the sea's own bottom, described below, both use it.

The sand belongs to the floor. Past the edge, =ju_ground.frag= thins the litter
out onto pale sand with dune relief at two scales, wind ripples and, up close,
glints from the grains. It lays a wrack line of weed, twigs and bits of shell at
the top of the waves' reach. Below that line the sand is moist, on the lower
beach and as far as this wave's sheet runs, following the edge. Moist sand is
darker and less rough than dry sand, with a faint sheen. Each sheet that drains
leaves a mirror-bright film that fills the ripples and reflects the sky, with a
lace of foam stranded at its highest reach. That mirrored sky counts in the
ambient share, so the occlusion darkens it too. Under the shallows the seabed
shimmers with caustics, and seagrass grows out past the surf. The
tyrannosaurus's three-toed prints, with a rim of pushed-up sand, appear where
its feet came down, because the shader mirrors the footholds =jr.vert= plants.
The blob leaves a wet trail up the beach.

The sea itself is one polar grid of 190 rings by 96 sectors, made in
=jw_sea.vert= from =gl_VertexID= over the half-plane seaward of the upper beach.
The rings are 30 cm apart for the first 30 m, through the surf, so a breaking
crest's silhouette is smooth instead of a row of teeth. After that each ring is
7% wider than the last, out to about 2.7 km. (The camera's far plane is 2,500 m
in this part.) The swell lifts the grid, fading out with distance where the
vertices thin. Over the swash the grid lies on the sand as a thin sheet, which
=jw_sea.frag= cuts off where the water's reach ends. The rings are drawn from
the horizon in.

For the surface, =jw_sea.frag= takes the swell's slope from the surf block and
sums the wind's waves over it per pixel. There are 17 trains from 18 m down to
8 cm, each with 1.4 times the wavenumber of the last, moving at deep-water
speeds. They are all about equally steep, with =a k= from 0.045 for the long
ones to 0.06, which gives a slope variance of about 0.028 in all. That is what
Cox and Munk measured for a breeze of about 4 m/s. Their headings step by the
golden angle, folded to within 90° of the onshore breeze, and they are
short-crested: each one's phase wanders along its crests. Gust patches (cat's
paws) scale the short ones, and over the swash sheet they calm down. Each train
is filtered against the pixel's footprint along its own direction, fading
out over 0.8-1.6 radians of phase across the pixel. A train the
pixel can't resolve skips its cosine and adds its slope variance instead, so
detail too fine to draw turns into roughness and doesn't shimmer.

The optics follow from that variance. The mirror normal is tilted toward the eye
by one standard deviation, since the facets that face the eye dominate at a
glancing angle. The Fresnel term uses water's index of 1.33 (F0 0.02), and the
sun's highlight is GGX with α² twice the unresolved slope variance, widened
further by the resolved normal's variation across a pixel. So with the sun off to the
side the glints are rare, as they are in life. Far out, the sea is the deep blue
of real water, darker than the horizon sky, and what it mirrors is the sky
itself, =s3sky= along the reflected ray. This filtering already does what a
glitter filter would, so there is none.

Whitecaps come from a second sum. Out past the surf zone, from 56-64 m, the four
longest wind-wave trains, the 18, 12.8, 9.1 and 6.5 m ones that make up the wind
sea proper, are summed a second time. They get a loop of their own because
Mesa's compiler won't unroll a single loop over all of them. This second loop
gives their heights and the crests' convergence, which is the choppy sea's
Jacobian, after Tessendorf. A whitecap forms where the Jacobian falls to about
0.8, sooner in a gust. The loop also gives its gradient and its value 0.8 s
earlier, so a crest leaves foam where it broke for about a second. Coverage is a
smooth step across that threshold, with its width set by the slopes the pixel
can't resolve and by the pixel's footprint. So it is sharp where the waves are
drawn and soft over the footprint. Where the pixel hides the waves altogether,
it becomes the chance of a whitecap, and the far sea whitens a touch. The light
through the thin crests is green in proportion to their height. That is linear,
so what a pixel can't resolve averages out instead of shimmering.

The sea draws with dual-source blending (=GL_SRC1_COLOR=, core since GL 3.3).
Output 0 adds what the water scatters and reflects, and output 1 multiplies what
lies beneath by the water's transmittance. The sea writes alpha 0 that way, so
the ambient share of what lies beneath stays as it was. The transmittance is
absorption per channel, 0.45, 0.07 and 0.04 per metre for red, green and blue,
along the refracted path down to the bottom and back up. Past 80 m the bottom
drops away. The floor's mesh ends about 80 m out, so beyond it the sea draws a
bottom of its own, wet sand and seagrass from =jwbed=. The two are cross-faded
over 60-75 m by where the eye's ray meets the floor, so there is no seam. The
water's own colour is turquoise over the sand, greener in the shallows, deep
blue far out, and a milky pale green where the surf has churned air into it.
Seen through, a wave's face crosses more water the steeper it stands, so it
looks greener and darker. Just before a wave breaks, its thin lip glows green,
lit through from behind, over the shade of the face below.

White water is computed only where there can be any: broken water, the swash's
edge, and round the blob. It is made from warped noise for the streaks and lumps
and a net of ridges for the lace. It fades into its average as the pixel's
footprint grows, so far foam averages out instead of aliasing. A wave is whitest
just after it breaks. The whole front of the bore is white, a trail decays
behind it, and a low background covers the surf zone, thinning as the bore runs
in. The wave breaks harder in patches along the beach and from wave to wave,
driven by continuous noise so that nothing pops. The puffs along its front ride
with it and take half the pattern there, so the foam's own warped noise breaks
them up. The foam's lumps are lit by the sun on top, and in the hollows by the
sky and the white around them, which gives a pale blue-grey. Finer octaves give
a torn edge, and the foam's cut-off is soft, 0.2 wide close up and widening to
0.5 with the footprint, so the edges fray.

So the crest's white water is a run of ragged, broken lobes. The swash's leading
edge is a lace. Round the blob there is a collar, plus the froth it churns up as
it breaks the surface. The collar is 0.5 and 0.3 of the foam where it cuts the
water. Any more fills it in to a smooth pale ellipse.

The sea is drawn in one pass after the floor, from the horizon in, so nearer
water blends over what lies behind it. It writes its own depth, which is the
surface pushed on along the eye's ray by up to 1.2 m but never past the floor.
It does that through a =gl_FragDepth= declared =depth_greater=, a promise to
only ever push depth farther. That keeps the early depth test, so what the trees
hide is still rejected before shading, which is worth about 1 ms in the
tyrannosaurus shot. The ambient occlusion, the contact shadows and the depth of
field all see this one smooth surface, with no blotches where the shallows meet
deeper water and no seam where the floor ends. Because that depth lies under the
surface, the blob's body under the water, drawn later in =S3_LATE=, passes the
depth test. In the far distance the sea fogs into the horizon through
=s3fog()=.

The sea and wet sand keep their Fresnel-weighted reflection of the sky's
radiance table, evaluated in their material shaders. The current build omits
the half-resolution screen-space reflection pass and its render target. Nearby
objects, including the blob, therefore no longer appear mirrored in the water
or wet sand. The swell, swash, foam, water depth and the blob's refraction
remain part of the scene.

** The tyrannosaurus (=s3_rex.c=, =jr.*=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-tyrannosaurus-s3-rex-c-jr
:END:

The tyrannosaurus is sculpted once, at load, by the creature kit (=jr.comp=),
which also makes Scene 2's fish, crabs, predator and whale. The kit is one
compute program whose pass number picks the animal and its grid as well as the
pass. =jr.comp= describes the tyrannosaurus in its rest pose as a smooth union
of about forty round cones and ellipsoids:

- the deep-chested trunk, from the tail's tip to the neck
- a wedge of a skull with jaw muscles and brow ridges
- the lower jaw
- on each leg, a great drumstick of a thigh, the calf, the long foot and three
  clawed toes
- two tiny two-clawed arms.

Each part is melted into the whole by a polynomial smooth minimum and belongs to
one of twelve bones: the trunk, the head, the jaw, each leg's thigh, shin, foot
and toes, and the arms. The folds round the neck are a small displacement.

Three compute passes turn this into a mesh. The first evaluates the field at
every corner of a 5 cm grid of 49x123x278. The second puts a vertex in every
cell the surface crosses, at the mean of its edges' crossings (/surface nets/).
It gives the vertex the field's gradient for a normal, and the two bones whose
parts lie nearest, blended by distance. The third adds a quad to the animal's
own index buffer round every grid edge the surface crosses. At load, =uw_sculpt=
in =main.c= runs the three passes for each of the five animals in turn. It uses
one dispatch size for all of them, since each pass returns early past its
animal's grid. They all write into one shared vertex buffer, which stays bound
on storage-buffer binding 5 for =jr.vert= and =fish.vert=. =uw_sculpt= reads
each animal's triangle count back, so each one is an ordinary indexed mesh drawn
with =mc_mesh_draw= or in instance batches, and then it frees the grid's two
scratch buffers. The tyrannosaurus is about 38,000 vertices and 76,000
triangles, and the whole kit takes about 60 ms of GPU time at load. =map()=, the
animals' field, has a single call site, which the vertex pass loops over for the
field, its three normal steps and the vertex. So the GPU compiler inlines the
five animals only once, and the kit compiles in 0.3-0.7 s cold. A change to
=jr.comp= shows only after a restart, as there is no hot re-sculpt.

The animal is about 13 m long, with its hips about 3.55 m up. Each leg melts
into the body only, never into the other leg: the body's field is united with
each leg separately, and the two results are joined by a plain minimum. If the
legs melted into each other, the skin between them would follow both thigh bones
half and half, and the stride would stretch it into a web. The thighs are slim
from side to side, with the cone squeezed to half its width and the drumstick
0.35 m half-wide. From the front they hang apart under the belly, and each melts
into the body over 40 cm, which leaves a fillet behind it.

=jr.vert= poses it every frame. Its hips travel along the edge at 3 m/s and stop
in the gap between the two trunks at 58.1 jungle seconds (222.6 s), where it
looks at us. =s3_rex_at= on the CPU, =across()= in the shader and the prints in
=ju_ground.frag= all agree on this. From =S3_REX= a foot comes down every
=S3_STEP=, left and right in turn. Each leg reaches its foothold by two-bone IK
while the body bobs, sways and rolls over the stance foot and the tail swings
the other way. Each vertex follows its two bones, blended linearly. The trunk
bends along its length for the tail's waves and for the neck turning with the
head. The head and the jaw turn rigidly with the look, and each leg's bones are
carried rigidly from their rest joints onto the IK's.

The skull is wide low down and narrow on top, so it looks like a tyrannosaur
from any side, head-on included. It has broad cheeks and jaw muscles behind
small forward-looking eyes under brow ridges, and a deep, narrow snout. The
cheeks reach down over the hinge of a lower jaw that is deep at the back and
U-shaped in section. The closed mesh culls its back faces. In the gap the
tyrannosaurus turns almost directly toward the camera from jungle 56.2 to
57.4 s and pitches its head down. The jaw opens wider on the two snorts at
57.93 and 58.54 s. It growls, then turns away from 59 to 60.4 s and walks on.

Its hide (=jr.frag=) is a dark, rough brown of albedo 0.05-0.11, paler
underneath, with near-black bands, 85% darker, across the back and the tail that
fade down the flanks. A brighter olive looks like a khaki clay toy in the shade.
The hide is mottled and pebbled with scales. Fine scale noise blends toward
its mean as its pixel footprint grows, and the relief fades from 0.06 toward
zero where it cannot be resolved. The scales still show on the long lens. The hide has
a little sheen, at roughness 0.65 (any glossier and its back goes pale in the
shade), and is creased with folds. All of this is computed from the rest-pose
position, so it rides with the body. A wet amber eye sits in its socket, and
teeth are painted along the mouth's seam on the side of the head. The mouth
darkens as the jaw drops.

A broad fill from the horizon sky lifts the head's ambient light, strongest
on upward-facing parts. It follows the head's rest coordinates and joins
=s3amb=, so the occlusion still darkens the eyes, jaw folds and creases.

At grazing angles the hide mirrors the bright beach and sky behind it: =s3sky=
along the reflected ray, with Schlick's Fresnel, dimmed where that ray finds the
shaded floor. That makes a rim which draws its back, head, neck and tail against
the background. The rim's window counts N.y with R.y, so it leaves out the
undersides, which only graze the horizon from under the body. Without that, seen
edge-on, the belly and the thighs' undersides mirror the bright horizon as a
cyan glow between the legs. The rim also leaves out the skin between the thighs
under the belly, which the legs and the belly close in, and there the rim and
the hide's occlusion are cut to a fifth. The rim is sky light, so it counts with
the ambient share, which the screen-space occlusion darkens in the folds. It
stays out of the sun's share because the contact shadows, marching from the
flank behind the near thigh, would find the thigh and leave a dark slit where it
meets the body.

Two flaws are left. At the stride's forward extreme, at 223.2 s, there is a thin
dark band where the back of the near thigh meets the belly. Better skinning,
with dual quaternions or corrective weights, would fix it. And a sliver of sky
shows on the inner thigh, 0.2 px wide at 1080p, where the mirror is already
cut.

A little light also comes through the thin parts on its far side from the sun,
such as the snout's tip, an arm and the toes. This is Barré-Brisebois's
translucency, with a thickness taken from the curvature of the interpolated
normal. At 21 m it amounts to a few warm pixels.

The animal is lit the way the open shade at the forest's edge would light it. It
stands 2.5 m out past the edge, open to the sky over the beach, so its back and
head take the sky's light at an occlusion of 0.9 and its belly stays dark at
0.3. With an albedo of about 0.05, a low occlusion all over turns it into a flat
black cut-out, and a high one lets the open sky light its flanks grey. Where it
stands in the pool of sun (see [[#light-s3-light-c-jl-and-the-engine-post][Light]]),
a rim of sun draws its back, neck and head, so its silhouette stands out from
the sky. The rim is =uGlow= × the sun's share × (1 - N·V)⁴ × N·L. In the shade,
as it first walks into view (217.7 s), it stays a dark shape. Seen from the
ferns, with only half the haze over it, it is still a dark shape against the
bright sand.

The eye's cornea is a dome. Its normal is the point's offset from the eyeball's
centre, which sits 5 cm in under the skin and is carried by the head's bone, and
=jr.vert= hands that centre over. So the hide's mirror term gives the eye a
small catchlight of the open sky over the beach on its upper part, moving as the
head turns. Its reflectance is 0.2 head-on, far above a real cornea's 3%, to
make the small highlight readable in the shade. It mirrors the sky's
radiance table. The eye gets no glint of the sun, because it stands in shade in every
frame, with the brow and the head between it and the sun.

It draws itself into the characters' sun map every frame (=S3_CHAR=), so it
casts its real shadow and shades itself. It also multiplies in a contact patch
of darker floor under its body and planted feet.

The camera stages it (see [[#the-camera][The camera]]). The draw starts four
seconds before =S3_REX=, while the whole animal is still offscreen. Its
appearance is continuous motion through real trunk occlusion: tiny glimpses
around jungle 50-52 s, then the head and shoulders before the body clears the
trees. There is no opacity fade. As it comes, the camera crouches into the
ferns, looks up and lengthens the lens, so the beast stands against the sky
in the gap. Each footfall (=s3_rex_step=, on the music's beats) drops the eye
and dips the look. The drop has a 0.16 m gain attenuated by
=1 / (1 + distance² / 225)=. The jolt rises over 40 ms, 1/24 of a step or
about 2.5 frames at 60 Hz, before it
decays. A drop within a single frame looks like a skipped frame instead of a
thump. There are thirteen jolts, one for each of the music's
footfalls, and they stop only once the thirteenth has died away.

The first snort, at 57.93 jungle seconds (222.4 s) in =jr.vert='s jaw and in
the music, makes us flinch. A Gaussian pulse with a 0.24 s width moves the
eye 0.35 m backward and 0.12 m upward at its peak, and lifts the look with it.

** The blob ashore (=s3_blob.c=, =jo_blob.*=)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-blob-ashore-s3-blob-c-jo-blob
:END:

This is the blob of Scenes 1 and 2, the soft, glossy, slightly translucent blue
jelly with two dark eyes. Here it is about a metre tall (=S3_BLOB_R= 0.48 m) and
has grown two stubby arms, which are the jelly itself swelling out of its sides
the way Ditto's do. Body and arms are one surface. The signed distance of the
squashed ball is melted with a smooth minimum into a round cone per arm, so each
arm grows out of a soft fillet with no seam. Each half of the jelly is a 72x48
fan of rays cast from inside its shoulder. That origin is inside both the ball
and the arm, so every ray crosses the surface exactly once, even with the arm
raised. The fan runs from the outward normal there to the seam down the blob's
middle, where the two halves' rays meet at the same points. The vertex shader
bisects each ray onto the surface and takes the normal from the field's
gradient. One instanced draw makes the two halves, the two eyes and the things
the blob carries, which come below. It draws into the characters' sun map too
(=S3_CHAR=, from a grid half as fine), so it casts a real shadow on the sand and
shades itself.

=s3_blob_at= gives its steady line, which the camera aims and focuses on. At
=S3_BLOB= the blob is just under the surface about 13 m out. It comes in and up
out of the sea, then up the beach to the wet sand about 4 m above the waterline,
facing the land. =jo_blob.vert= times the performance around that line off
=uTime=. Its cue times are jungle seconds less the hold (=S3_HOLD=, four bars or
7.74 s), and =jo_blob.frag= counts the same way. The blob rises, shakes off the
water, then hops up the beach, nine hops at one a beat. Each hop is a wind-up
squash, a stretch as it takes off with its arms up, and a squash on landing.
Once it's ashore its bottom flattens against the sand. It settles, blinks, looks
left and right, turns back to the camera and lifts both arms to wave. After the
play it shivers once more on the dry sand to shake itself dry, a 6% wobble at
11 Hz from 262.8 to 263.5 s. Nothing flies off it there, 3.4 m up from the
swash's edge, because only landings in the water splash. Spray there would look
like water out of nowhere.

The play is a table of hops, =JO_RUNS=. Each run starts on a jungle beat,
counted in beats since =S3_J0= (bar 38 is beat 152). It gives the number of
hops, the beats each takes, how far each carries the blob toward the sea or back
up the beach, how high, how far each turns it, and how hard each landing
splashes. The nine hops ashore are the first run. Then the blob turns round to
the sea, hops down after a draining wave into the last of it, and bounces. It
makes its two-beat jump over the next wave's foaming front, stamps, turns back
and hops up the beach. Then it turns half round and takes a 25 cm step down
toward the sea, to 46.65 m out, to sit and watch, with a last bounce as a wave
laps round it. That step puts the last landing in the swash, with the sheet
running 30 cm past it as it lands. The beats were picked against the surf
block's swash, so the water really is where the blob lands. A wave comes every
6.98 s, and the set grows: three successive sheets run up to 47.6, 46.8 and
46.3 m out. =s3_blob_at= integrates the table for the blob's steady line and its
turn. =blob_draw= hands =jo_blob.vert= the run in progress (hops into it,
height, step, turn) and the time since the last run ended, for a settling
wobble. So the shader shapes each hop, and none of the play is hard-coded
there. The music's hop plucks and splash hisses follow the same table (see
[[#its-music][Its music]]).

For the splashes, =jo_splashes= finds the two latest landings in the water, with
where, how long ago and how hard. Only landings in the water splash. They go to
=jo_blob.vert= for the spray and to =jw_sea.frag= for the water. In the water
each landing makes a ring of foam running out from it, of radius 0.5 + 1.3
√age m, with churned white at its heart, fading over about a second. The ring
and the heart are lace and froth, weighted 0.4 and 0.5 and held under 0.55 of
the foam, so they don't saturate into one flat disc of the foam's sky-lit lilac
hollow colour.

While spray flies, the blob's draw gets two more instances. Each throws up to
about 2,800 drops ballistically from a ring round the landing. The drops are
1-6 cm across, skewed small (h.z × h.w² of the range) so that there are a third
as many big drops. Each drop is drawn as a quad turned to its velocity across
the view and stretched by how far it flies in a 60th of a second, with its
coverage spread over the streak. So the splash is a crown of streaks thrown up
and out, where the fast drops are long streaks and the slow ones are still
drops. A drop is never drawn smaller than 1.5 px. A smaller drop's coverage
becomes its opacity instead, dithered, so the spray doesn't shimmer. Drops fade
over 0.7-1.2 s and are gone once they're below the water or the sand.

=jo_blob.frag= shades a drop as a dithered disc. It refracts the picture behind
it, mirrors the sky by Fresnel and catches the sun, using the same copy of the
picture as the body. Only the mist is white from churned air. The mist is the
drops under 1.5 px, whose coverage is their opacity, so they stay white and
soft. A big drop is clear: the picture through it is undimmed, and the sky at
its rim is at 0.4. No drop is drawn more than 0.7 opaque. The picture it
refracts is copied before the blob is drawn, so a solid drop over the jelly
would show the sea behind it as a white or sea-green disc. At 0.7 the jelly
shows through and the TAA blends it. At 1:1 the spray is a ring of glinting,
translucent beads round the blob.

The blob's jump into the swash (at 257.7 s, =JO_PROPS=) throws a straw sun hat,
a beach parasol and a folded deckchair up out of the water. It comes out of the
water for the last time with them. The hat sits at a slight tilt on its crown.
The parasol is in its right hand, with the pole leaning in over its head, and
the chair hangs from its left hand by the top bar. They are two more instances
of the blob's own draw, which =jo_blob.vert= numbers first: 0-1 are what it
carries, 2-3 the jelly, 4-5 the eyes and 6-7 the spray. So they ride the blob's
frame through its hops, its squash, its turns and the shiver, and they ride its
two arms, which take a holding pose as it lands.

The 72x48 grid's rows are split into parts, each one a lathe (a profile turned
about an axis) or a straight tube. Instance 0 holds the canopy in 22 rows, the
pole in 4 and the hat in 22. Instance 1 holds the chair's six rails and bars,
two rows each, and its canvas in 6. A part's normal is taken across its own
surface by finite differences, from two more evaluations of the point, so no
part needs a normal written out.

The canopy's ribs run out from a hub 1.2 m up the pole, bowed down toward their
tips. The cloth sags between them and is pulled in at their tips, and a valance
runs round its edge, with a hem that =jo_blob.frag= cuts into scallops. Once
caught, the parasol opens over half a second (258.1-258.6 s) as the ribs swing
out from along the pole. It has eight red and white panels. The pole is wood,
pointed at its foot, with its finial poking out of the hub. The hat is a round
crown and a wide brim that droops and waves. It sits just above the jelly,
because any lower the jelly's crown pokes through the brim. It is braided straw
in rows about 1.2 cm wide, each row its own shade with a groove between them,
under a navy band. The chair is a deckchair's two frames of varnished wood, with
the striped yellow and white canvas folded between them. It hangs off the hand,
turned a little out from the blob's side, and swings as each run of hops stops.
The landing's splash throws all three up: for 0.45 s they rise from under the
water in front of the blob, slowing down into its hands and onto its head.

They are opaque, so they draw with the world (=S3_LIT=). The copy of the picture
that the jelly bends is taken after them, so they show through it, and the wet
sand mirrors them. =S3_LATE= draws them again with the jelly, since the instance
range starts at 0, but the depth test, =GL_LESS= on the very same depths, passes
none of it. In the characters' sun map (=S3_CHAR=) they cast real shadows on the
sand, and the canopy casts one over the hat and the blob. Once the blob carries
them, the map's box round it widens from 1.2 m to 1.8 m to take in the canopy.
The map's colour target lets the driver reuse the shader builds it made at load.
It also holds how much of the sun each thing lets through (=uSunT=, unit 3, read
in the s3 common block's =s3shadow=):

- 1 for the jelly and its eyes, which keep letting =uBlobFog= (0.3) of the sun
  through
- 0.5 for the parasol's cloth, which lets 0.15 of the sun through. The shade
  under a beach parasol is a light one. With an opaque cloth the hat under it
  goes a greenish grey.
- 0 for the rest.

From the shade, the cloth and the canvas glow with the sun coming through them,
and the straw glows a little.

The blob is drawn late (=S3_LATE=), after the sea and the sky. The demo copies
the finished picture into =jelly_copy= (unit 9, the whole frame, as in Scene 2),
and =jo_blob.frag= bends that copy through the blob's body under the coat's own
lighting. The beach, the surf and the sky behind it are refracted per colour
channel and absorbed by the blue along the chord through the body. Each
channel's lookup is a vertical tent of three taps, with the middle one counted
twice, spaced 2% of the picture's height times the chord's thickness apart. On
the long lens the sea is horizontal streaks, and the ball flips a tall stretch
of it and squeezes it into tight stripes. The tent melts the thin stripes into a
few soft bands, while the broad bands of foam and water stay. The streaks are
mostly the mirrored horizon in the swash and the surf, which is how a long lens
shows a real sea.

The blob writes an ambient share of 1 in alpha, as its eyes and drops do, so the
post's contact shadows pass over it. Otherwise their march takes the ball and
the spray in the depth buffer for solid blockers, and blackens patches of the
jelly. The jelly glows where the sun comes through it and mirrors the sky in its
coat. It is wet and streaming as it comes out of the sea, and again as it comes
out of the swash. Where it is still under the surf, it is shaded the way
=jw_sea= shades the seabed, with the water's transmittance along the view, its
scattered colour, and the sky mirrored at grazing angles. A ring of foam forms
where the swell laps at it. The eyes are glossy black lenses that catch the
sky.

out, height over the floor and distance across. It carries the eye from under

=s3_roam_dist=, the distance to it, and a tree in the way is pushed aside or

We hold, crouched, while it walks on out of the frame, and stand once it has

* The soundtrack
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-soundtrack
:END:

"Hello World" has no audio files. Its music is one song, 278.7 s long, that the
engine's software synth (=src/synth.c=) computes while the show runs. It's 144
bars at 124 BPM, in four movements and a coda: one movement for Scene 1, two
for Scene 2, one for Scene 3, and the coda under the end card. This section covers
the synth and its voices, the song section by section, how the picture keeps
time with it, and how to change the music for a demo of your own.

Bars count from 0 at the start of the show. A bar of 4/4 at 124 BPM lasts
240 / 124 = 1.935 s, so bar n starts at n × 1.935 s. A range such as "bars
26-44" starts on the downbeat of bar 26 and stops on the downbeat of bar 44, so
it's 18 bars long. [[file:architecture.org::#timing][Timing]] covers the clocks
the picture runs on and how they meet these bars.

** The softsynth (src/synth.c)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-softsynth-src-synth-c
:END:

The synth uses no samples, and every sound is computed. A sequencer steps
through the song in sixteenth notes, accurate to the sample, and reads compact
one-bar patterns and chord tables. An arrangement on top sets each instrument's
level, the filter and the reverb send for every section, and ramps them across
it.

=mc_synth_make= allocates everything up front, about 650 KB. =mc_synth_render=
then fills a buffer with interleaved stereo floats and moves the song on. It
doesn't allocate, lock or do I/O, so it's safe on the audio thread that
=mc_audio_start= starts. The output depends only on the seed: the same seed
renders the same song to the bit, however the frames are split into blocks.

The seed sets the pad's start phases and detune and starts the synth's noise
generator. So it moves only incidental detail, such as the hats' grain, which
birds call and when, and where the kalimba's notes sit in the stereo field. The
notes, the structure and the timing never change. At the end the song doesn't
loop. The sequencer holds its last step and the arrangement its last values,
so whatever is still sounding rings out (see
[[#how-it-ends][How it ends]]).

*** The voices
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-voices
:END:

They're built from a few standard parts: band-limited (polyBLEP) saw
oscillators, ADSR envelopes with exponential decays, and a Zavalishin
state-variable filter, which gives low-, band- and high-pass at once.

- The kick is a sine whose pitch falls from 150 Hz to 48 Hz, with a 4 ms click
  of noise on top. In the jungle it starts at 95 Hz with a softer click, which
  makes it a frame drum.
- The hi-hats are noise through a 9 kHz high-pass, closed (40 ms) or open
  (180 ms), with velocities from the pattern and a slight alternation left and
  right. In the jungle the same voice is a shaker.
- The bass is a saw through a resonant low-pass. An envelope snaps it open on
  every note, from 130 Hz up to 2.7 kHz, and closes it again with a 90 ms decay.
  The finale and the jungle add a sub, a sine an octave under the bass.
- The lead is three detuned saws through a low-pass: a pluck in Scene 1 and a
  slow bell in the descent. Hook B and the coda add a second lead an octave up.
  Both feed a ping-pong delay.
- The pad is the chord's three notes, each on two detuned saws, the left one
  flat and the right one sharp, through a resonant low-pass on each side. Its
  cutoff is the arrangement's main sweep.
- The finale adds a snare, band-passed noise over a 190 Hz thump, and a
  supersaw: seven saws detuned by up to 1.9% and spread across the stereo field.
  The supersaw goes through a low-pass at the pad's cutoff, so it opens and
  closes with the arrangement. Even at 6 kHz its aliases stay about 55 dB under
  its harmonics.
- The jungle adds a six-tine kalimba. Each tine is two damped resonators run as
  rotating phasors: the note, and a fast-fading 6th partial. A new note takes
  the quietest tine and is added onto what it still holds, so nothing clicks.
  The kalimba feeds the delay too.
- The jungle also has bird calls, claves, cicadas and wind. A bird is a gliding
  FM sine with a soft attack, and it whistles, trills or gives a macaw's
  falling squawk. Squawks are likelier when the birds are busiest. The claves
  are a 2.55 kHz sine that dies within about 30 ms.
- The cicadas are band-passed noise buzzing at about 87 Hz, swelling separately
  left and right. The wind is noise band-passed between 500 Hz and 1.9 kHz as
  it gusts.
- The beach adds the surf. On each side, low-passed noise rushes up and draws
  back on a wave of its own, every 7.3 s on the left and 8.9 s on the right.
- The tyrannosaurus has footfalls and breath. A footfall is a sub sine falling
  from 95 to 36 Hz under a crunch of litter. Its breath is two longer,
  band-passed snorts centred at 750 Hz and a growl: a 48 Hz saw and noise
  through a 220 Hz low-pass, with a rasping 23 Hz tremolo. The growl rings
  out with an approximately 1.35 s exponential decay.
- The footfalls and the breath draw on a noise generator of their own, so they
  don't reshuffle the birds and the wind.
- A noise riser, band-passed noise whose centre climbs with its level, leads
  into the drops and makes the reverse swells.

The leads and the supersaw rest through the jungle, from bar 80 until the end
card at bar 139, which keeps the jungle cheaper to compute than the dance tune.

*** The delay and the reverb
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-delay-and-the-reverb
:END:

The delay is a stereo ping-pong of three sixteenths, a dotted eighth (0.36 s),
with a feedback of 0.42. The dry signal goes in on the left and the echoes
cross from side to side.

The reverb is a stereo feedback delay network. On the way in, the send loses
its lows below about 150 Hz, so the tail doesn't boom, and two of Freeverb's
allpasses smear its attacks. Then come eight delay lines of 32-61 ms, all prime
lengths. Each line is read at its end through a one-pole low-pass, standing in
for the air's damping so the highs die first, and through a gain.

An 8x8 Hadamard matrix, done as three butterfly passes and scaled by 1/√8,
mixes the eight outputs. It's orthogonal, so the mixing loses no energy, and
every line feeds every other. The mix is written back with the send added. Each
line's gain is 10^(-3 len / (rate × rt)), so all eight fall 60 dB in the same
rt whatever their length.

Each length also wanders by up to 6 samples either way on a slow sine of its
own, a fraction of a cent of pitch, and is read with linear interpolation. That
keeps the tail from ringing metallic. The even lines sum to the left and the
odd ones to the right. That gives two different mixes of one tail, so a mono
send comes out as decorrelated stereo. The shortest line (32 ms) doubles as the
pre-delay.

Each movement voices the reverb its own way (=RVV=), and the gains and the
wander are updated every 64 samples. The damping takes the highs out faster
than the rt, so the tails measure shorter (T20 from an impulse, by Schroeder's
backward integration):

| Movement                     | rt    | Damped from | T20   |
|------------------------------+-------+-------------+-------|
| Scene 1 and the coda         | 2.6 s | 8 kHz       | 2.1 s |
| The descent: space, the deep | 4.5 s | 2.2 kHz     | 3.0 s |
| The finale: the reef         | 3.0 s | 3 kHz       | 2.1 s |
| The jungle                   | 1.4 s | 11 kHz      | 1.2 s |

The pad, the leads, the supersaw, the kalimba and the birds go to the reverb at
the section's space level (=P_SPACE=). So do the snare and the claves, the only
drums that do. The finale's snare picks up the reef's dark tail, and more of it
as the outro opens out. The kick, the hats, the bass and the ambience stay dry.

*** The arrangement and the mix
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-arrangement-and-the-mix
:END:

The song has 29 sections, and the arrangement is data. Every section's levels
sit in one X-macro list, =RAMPS=, of 278 entries. An entry names a section and a
parameter. It moves the parameter from a start value by some amount over a
share of the section, in a straight line or along a smoothstep. A parameter
that a section doesn't mention is 0 there.

The 18 parameters are the instruments' levels, the pad's cutoff, the riser, the
reverb send and the depth of the sidechain pump. The list is expanded into six
column arrays, one per field, because the packer codes columns of bytes better
than rows of structs. It has more than 255 entries, so each section's first
entry is a 16-bit index. [[#editing-the-arrangement][Editing the arrangement]]
gives the format.

The render loop works out the targets for every sample and follows them through
a smoother of about 5 ms, so a level that jumps at a section boundary doesn't
click.

The mix has two buses. The drums, the riser and the tyrannosaurus go to a dry
one. Everything else, from the bass to the reverb's return, goes to a musical
bus that a sidechain ducks on every kick. It ducks by up to 0.8 of the
section's pump level and recovers over about 160 ms. Scene 1 and the coda pump
lighter (up to 0.65) than the finale (0.8-0.85).

A peak limiter then catches anything over 1.1 at once and lets go over about
40 ms, so the full finale doesn't drive the last stage hard. That stage is a DC
blocker and a =tanh= soft clip, which never goes past 0.92.

floor, and the quiet sections hold it low. The intro rises from 0.26 to 0.48

*** The first movement: Scene 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-first-movement-scene-1
:END:

Scene 1 is a bright dance tune in A major, 26 bars long: intro 4, build 4, hook
A 4, breakdown 4, hook B 7 and outro 3. Its groove is four on the floor, with
offbeat sixteenth hats, a bouncy bass and a plucked lead on an arpeggio.

- The intro is sparse, with no kick and no bass. The pad rises from a whisper,
  the filter opens, the hats fade in and then the lead. A reverse swell, the
  riser over the intro's last 14% (about 1.1 s), leads into the bang and is cut
  on its first kick.
- The build brings in the kick and the bass, eases in the sidechain pump, and
  ends on a riser into the hook.
- Hook A is the full groove.
- The breakdown is a breather with no kick or bass, while the filter sweeps
  slowly back up. A riser leads into hook B.
- Hook B is the bigger reprise, with the lead doubled an octave up.
- The outro collapses back toward the intro. The kick, the bass, the hats and
  the lead fade out in turn, and the filter closes from 2.6 kHz to 280 Hz.

*** Scene 2's music: the descent and the finale
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: its-own-music
:END:

The second and third movements score Scene 2's long journey (bars 26-80). The
second, 18 bars in D minor, goes with the fall from orbit, the dive and the
murky deep. The third, 36 bars in C major with its anthem held long, carries
the scene from the clearing water into its faster, more realistic finale.

The second movement is the same sixteenth grid stripped right back. There are
no hats, a slow bell replaces the plucked lead, the bass holds long roots, and
the kick plays a half-time heartbeat. Its four sections follow the picture:

- Space (5 bars) is dark and drowned in reverb, with the pad held low at 0.5
  under the bell.
- The dive (5 bars) fades the heartbeat in and lifts the pad from 0.5 to 0.88.
  It starts from space's level, so the dive's first beat has no step. A riser
  over the last 45% leads into the splash.
- The splash (2 bars) is the hit. The kick and the bass fall away, and the
  resonant filter closes from 3.2 kHz to 500 Hz.
- The deep (6 bars) is muffled and calm, its filter under 1 kHz. The pad is
  back at 0.5 and sinks to 0.23 over the last 40%, and the bell fades out. A
  reverse swell over the last 18% (about 2.1 s) leads into the third movement
  as the water clears.

The third movement is the finale. It's a four-on-the-floor groove with a
backbeat snare, a fat supersaw lead, a sub and heavy pads, under the strongest
pump. It has three sections:

- The build (7 bars) brings in the kick, then the hats and then the snare, over
  a rising sub and a riser. The pad and the supersaw share one cutoff, which
  goes from 1.2 to 3.6 kHz. In bar 50 a snare roll leads into the peak,
  eighths turning into sixteenths and swelling from 0.3 to 1.8 times a plain
  hit.
  Within that build, the whale reveal opens the music out from 88.1 to 96.8 s,
  strongest around 91.9-92.9 s. It raises the held pad and reverb space while
  easing back the supersaw, kick and hats. The C-major harmony and existing
  voices carry the swell.
- The peak (26 bars) has everything in, and it carries the whole finale: the
  predator's stalk, the chase, the blob's transformation and its flight. The
  cutoff holds 4.2 kHz through the first half. Through the back half, from bar
  64, it opens to 6 kHz over a riser that climbs to 0.7.
- The outro (3 bars) resolves and tails off. Two beats of snare sixteenths lead
  into it in bar 76, as the riser tops out. The cutoff closes from 4.2 to
  1.5 kHz and the reverb opens out. Over the last 2.5 s (from 57% of the outro)
  a riser swells as the picture burns out to white.

Two things happen inside the peak without being sections of their own: the
stalk and the swell.

The stalk is bars 60-63 (116.1-123.9 s), from just before the predator appears
to the near miss. The anthem drops to a heartbeat. The supersaw goes out, the
pad's filter shuts to 500 Hz, and the sequencer plays the second movement's
pattern rows. That's the half-time kick and short roots on the bass and the sub,
with no hats and no snare.

The kick on bar 63's third beat, at 122.903 s, lands 5 ms after the near miss.
From 123.39 s, as the blob bolts, a beat of swelling snare sixteenths plays
alone, and that's the drop. On bar 64 everything comes back at once with the
filter open again, and the chase gets the full anthem.

In the code the stalk is an override. For those four bars =arrange()= zeroes
the supersaw and sets the cutoff, and =on_step= picks the second movement's
patterns, while the chords and the reverb stay the finale's. A section of its
own would split the peak in three. It would also shift every section number
that the arrangement and the jungle index by, and move the peak's ramps, which
run in percent of the peak.

The swell runs over the peak's last quarter, from bar 71 (137.5 s, 2.2 s before
the blob takes off) to the outro's downbeat (149.0 s). The supersaw itself
swells from 1.0 to 1.4, so the flight lifts on the anthem. It's there because
the opening filter alone makes the supersaw brighter rather than louder. On the
downbeat the supersaw is back at 1.0, the riser cuts, and the cutoff drops from
6 to 4.2 kHz.

*** Scene 3's music: the jungle and the beach
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: its-music
:END:

The fourth movement (bars 80-139) is a warm piece in D major. Open, rootless
sevenths (Dmaj7 / Bm7 / Gmaj7 / A add9) sit over a deep, ringing bass, and a

Cicadas, wind and bird calls fill the forest, and the surf the beach. The music
darkens for the tyrannosaurus, lifts into A major for the blob's return,

- The white (1 bar, =BARS_WHITE=) is weightless. The finale's riser dies away,
  the pad opens, and high, bell-like tines ring long. Wind and cicadas fade in,
  so we hear the forest before we see it.
- The look round (8 bars) begins the forest at =BAR_WOODS= (bar 81), which is
  Scene 3's =S3_J0=. The filter settles warm, the cicadas swell and the first
  birds call. The bass, the shaker, the frame drum and the claves come in one
  by one.
- The canopy (5 bars) brightens, with the birds overhead at their busiest:
  whistles, trills and macaw squawks. In the bar before it, the frame drum and
  the claves answer each other in a fill.
- The descent (3 bars) thins the groove out.
- The ladybug (8 bars) leaves the kalimba almost alone, an octave up, over a
  soft pad, the insects and a few birds.
- The take-off (1 bar) lifts everything on the dominant, A add9, under a riser.
- The resolve (1 bar) comes home to D as the ladybug vanishes into the light,
  and the groove starts back.
- The glide (4 bars) travels with the shaker, the frame drum and the kalimba in
  eighths, and the drums drop away toward the forest's edge.
- The beach's hush (1 bar), from jungle second 50.3 at the first sight of the

- The tyrannosaurus (8 bars) keeps the forest quiet beneath a stronger held
  dissonant drone. D/E♭ bass and sub pulses sound every two beats, tightening
  to every beat from jungle second 55.2 to 59.0 as the head turns. Pad
  brightness, level and a filtered-noise swell rise into the first snort.
  The dark harmony and pulse hold through the departure and the wait, until
  the relief at jungle second 67.7.
- The relief (2 bars) brings the forest and the waves back up on A add9, the
  pivot into A major, with the kalimba fading back after the tension eases.
- The blob (5 bars) comes ashore to Scene 1's hook. Its arpeggio and its
  A-E-F♯m-D progression are now on the kalimba, over the jungle's hand
  percussion and the surf.
- The wave (2 bars) holds on A while the blob waves, with a rising run of high
  kalimba notes each bar and the pad bright.
- The play (6 bars) comes in on a frame-drum-and-claves fill like the canopy's.
  It brings the groove back up and the sea louder, under Scene 1's hook.
- The end (4 bars) closes on D-E7-A, held on A, as the blob sits and the last
  wave laps at it. The pad, the kalimba, the waves and the forest fade out with

** Changing the music
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: changing-the-music
:END:

There's one song, in =src/synth.c=, and it belongs to the engine, not to the
demo. The Makefile links =src/synth.c= and =src/audio.c= into every demo under
=demos/=. So a demo of your own plays "Hello World"'s song until you change
that file, and demos that share a tree share the song.

You can edit the song's tables and keep the synth's voices and effects, or
replace the file with a synth of your own. Either way the picture has to be
timed to the new song.

*** How the picture reads the music
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: how-the-picture-reads-the-music
:END:

The picture never looks at the synth's state: no note events, no levels. It
reads one number, the playback position, and times everything from it. Each
frame, =main.c= takes its clock from =mc_audio_time()=, the seconds of the song
that have been heard, so bar n of the song is at n × 240 / BPM seconds of the
show. [[file:architecture.org::#the-master-clock][The master clock]] explains
how the position is estimated and what happens without audio.

So everything that should land on the music is a time in bars. =main.c=
defines =BPM= (124) and =BAR_S=, one bar of 240 / =BPM= seconds, and writes its
cues as multiples of =BAR_S=. =T_BANG=, the first kick of the build, is
=4 * BAR_S=, and the scenes are 26, 54, 59 and 5 bars long.

For motion that follows the beat, =mc_scene.h= has the timeline helpers. Fill
an =mc_timeline= with the time, the length and the tempo, then:

- =mc_beats()= gives the time in beats.
- =mc_beat_pulse()= gives a 0..1 pulse that jumps to 1 on each beat and decays,
  faster for a higher sharpness.
- =mc_window_active()= and =mc_window_phase()= switch a span of time on and give
  the 0..1 progress through it.

"Hello World" uses a smooth sine of =mc_beats()= for Scene 1's pulsing, because
a hard pulse makes the motion stutter. It uses =mc_window_phase()= for fades
such as Scene 1's collapse.

#+begin_src c
mc_timeline tl = { t, DUR, BPM };                               /* main.c's clock */
float sb = 0.5f + 0.5f * sinf((float)mc_beats(&tl) * MC_TAU);   /* a smooth beat */
if (mc_window_active(&tl, 4 * BAR_S, 4 * BAR_S)) { /* the build, bars 4-8 */ }
#+end_src

*** Where the song is written down
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: where-the-song-is-written-down
:END:

Everything is in =src/synth.c=, most of it near the top:

| What                 | Where                                | What it holds                                                                 |
|----------------------+--------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| The tempo            | =SONG_BPM=                           | 124. The grid is sixteenths, =STEPS_PER_BAR= 16 to a bar of 4/4.              |
| Section lengths      | =BARS_*=                             | Bars per section. =BAR_*= work out the starts and =TOTAL_BARS= the length.    |
| The section table    | =SEC_BAR[]=, =SEC_COUNT=             | Each section's first bar, 29 sections, closed by =TOTAL_BARS=.                |
| Chords               | =PROG[6][4]=                         | A root (a MIDI note) and three offsets per chord, four chords to a row.       |
| Patterns             | =PAT[4][6][16]=                      | One bar per movement: kick, hat velocity (0-7), open hat, snare, bass, arpeggio. |
| Envelopes            | =ENV_BASS=, =ENV_LEAD=, =ENV_BFILT=  | Attack, decay, sustain and release, in seconds, per movement.                 |
| The arrangement      | =RAMPS=                              | Every section's levels and sweeps (below).                                    |
| The reverb           | =RVV=                                | The rt and the damping per movement.                                          |
| The jungle's kalimba | =KAL4=, =JHARM=                      | Its steps, octave and ring per section, and the chords from the take-off on.  |
| Fills                | =FILL=, in =on_step=                 | Extra kick and snare hits in five bars, as 16-bit step masks.                 |
| One-off events       | =on_step=                            | The stalk (=BAR_STALK=), the footfalls, the snorts and growl, the hop mask, =SPLASH= and =END_MEL=. |

The bass row holds semitones above the chord's root an octave down, and =REST=
(127) means no note. The arpeggio row indexes the chord's three notes over two
octaves (0-5), from an octave above the root. The movement's lead plays it: the
pluck, the bell, the supersaw or the kalimba.

=on_step= picks the movement from the section number. Sections 0-5 and 28 play
the first movement's tables, 6-9 the second's, 10-12 the third's and 13-27 the
jungle's. The movement chooses the =PAT= and =PROG= rows, the envelopes, the
reverb's voicing and the voice that plays the arpeggio.

A few other places count sections by number too: where each progression
restarts, =KAL4[sec - 13]=, =JHARM[sec - 18]=, and =sec >= 24= for the kalimba
playing Scene 1's arpeggio. Changing notes and levels inside the 29 sections
touches none of this. Adding or removing a section means changing =SEC_COUNT=,
renumbering =RAMPS= and moving these tests.

Two more things to know. In =synth.c= the =S3_= and =SCENE3= names
(=BARS_S3_BUILD=, =BAR_SCENE3= and the rest) belong to the third movement,
Scene 2's finale, not to Scene 3. The jungle's are =BARS_JUNGLE=, =BAR_JUNGLE=
and =BAR_WOODS=. And =SEC_BAR= and =FILL= hold bars in a byte, so a song can be
at most 255 bars long, about 8 minutes at 124 BPM.

For a demo of your own, start with the events tied to "Hello World"'s picture,
which you'll want to delete or retime. They're the stalk, the fills, the
footfalls, the snorts and the growl, the hop plucks, the splashes and the coda's
melody.

*** Editing the arrangement
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: editing-the-arrangement
:END:

Two entries from =RAMPS=, one from the intro and one from hook A:

#+begin_src c
F(0, P_HAT|SM, 40, 60, 0, 450)   /* intro: the hats fade in from 0 to 0.45 over its last 60% */
LEVEL(F, 2, P_KICK, 1000)        /* hook A: the kick at full level throughout */
#+end_src

=F(section, parameter, a, w, v0, d)= moves the parameter from v0 to v0 + d
while the section's progress runs from a% to (a + w)%, and holds it before and
after. =|SM= makes the move a smoothstep instead of a straight line.
=LEVEL(F, section, parameter, v)= holds v for the whole section. Levels are in
thousandths, 1000 being the instrument's full level, and =P_CUT= is in Hz.

The parameters are =P_KICK=, =P_HAT=, =P_BASS=, =P_SUB=, =P_LEAD=, =P_LEAD2=
(the octave layer), =P_PAD=, =P_CUT= (the cutoff of the pad and the supersaw),
=P_SUPER=, =P_SNARE= and =P_PUMP= (the sidechain's depth). Then come =P_SPACE=
(the reverb send), =P_RISER=, =P_KAL=, =P_BIRD=, =P_AMB= (the cicadas), =P_WIND=
and =P_SURF=. In the jungle, =P_KICK= is the frame drum, =P_HAT= the shaker and
=P_SNARE= the claves.

Some rules come from the way the list is stored:

- A parameter the section doesn't name is 0 in it. Each section has to list
  every instrument it wants to hear, and the cutoff too.
- Keep the entries in section order, and give every section at least one.
  =mc_synth_make= finds each section's first entry by scanning the list, and
  =arrange()= reads from there up to the next section's first.
- v0 and d are each stored in a signed byte. Levels are kept in hundredths, so
  they have to be multiples of 10 from -1280 to 1270.
- The cutoff is kept in steps of 50 Hz, so v0 and d must each stay within
  6.35 kHz either way, though v0 + d can go higher. Nothing warns when a value
  doesn't fit: the cast wraps it round.
- A ramp whose d isn't 0 needs a w above 0.

*** What has to stay in step with the timeline
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-has-to-stay-in-step-with-the-timeline
:END:

=synth.c= and =main.c= each write down the tempo and the song's layout, and
nothing checks one against the other. When they disagree, nothing fails: the
picture just lands off the music. These have to match:

- The tempo: =SONG_BPM= in =synth.c= and =BPM= in =main.c=, 124 in "Hello
  World".
- The scenes' lengths. =main.c='s =SCENE1_DUR= to =SCENE4_DUR= (26, 54, 59 and
  5 bars) go with the synth's =BAR_SCENE2=, =BARS_SCENE2= + =BARS_SCENE3=,
  =BARS_JUNGLE= and =BARS_END=, so =TOTAL_DUR= matches =TOTAL_BARS=.
- Scene 1's cues, =T_BANG= to =T_OUTRO=, and the synth's =BAR_BUILD= to
  =BAR_OUTRO=.
- The stretch tables, =S2_SEG= in =main.c= and =S3_SEG= in =scene3.c=, which
  change pace at the song's section changes. If a section changes length, the
  stretch has to change with it, or the cues after it slide.
- The events timed to the picture: the stalk and the predator's cues, the
  footfalls and =S3_REX=, the hop mask and =SPLASH= and =JO_RUNS=, and
  =END_MEL= and =END_LAND=.

[[file:architecture.org::#defined-twice-main-c-and-synth-c][Defined twice]]
has the full table, and the times that the jungle's shaders carry as literals.

The song's length matters too. The release build exits when the clock reaches
=TOTAL_DUR=, and the capture build writes that much audio and silences the
rest. A song longer than the show is cut off there. A shorter one holds its
last step and rings out while the picture goes on.

A tempo that differs shows as drift. At 1 BPM off, the show's last bar lands
more than 2 s away from the music's.

A demo of your own can ask the synth for its tempo instead of keeping a second
copy. =mc_synth_bpm()= returns =SONG_BPM=, and =mc_synth_length()= the song's
length in seconds. [[file:tutorial.org::#5-add-the-synth][The tutorial]] fills
its =mc_timeline= from them. "Hello World" doesn't, because its cue times are
compile-time constants.

*** Replacing the synth
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: replacing-the-synth
:END:

Any =src/synth.c= that defines the five functions of =mc_audio.h= will do:
=mc_synth_make=, =mc_synth_free=, =mc_synth_render=, =mc_synth_bpm= and
=mc_synth_length=. A few rules carry over from this one:

- =mc_synth_render= runs on the audio thread, so it mustn't allocate, lock or
  do I/O. Allocate everything in =mc_synth_make=.
- It writes interleaved stereo floats at 44.1 kHz (=MC_SAMPLE_RATE=), each one
  finite and between -1 and 1.
- It has to render the same samples however the frames are split into blocks.
  The audio thread asks for 1024 frames at a time and the capture build for
  4096.
- =tools/checks/synth.c= tests the last two rules over the whole song, and
  =python3 tools/verify.py= builds and runs it.
- In the packed build, library calls go through the hashed import table in
  =include/microcosm/mc_imports.h=. For maths it lists =sinf=, =cosf=, =tanf=,
  =tanhf=, =expf=, =exp2f=, =powf=, =floorf=, =ceilf=, =acosf= and =atan2f=,
  with =calloc= and =malloc= for memory.
- Add any other library function you call to that list, or the payload imports
  it by name, which costs more bytes. Include =mc_imports.h= after the system
  headers. [[file:pipeline.org::#keeping-libc-dropping-the-crt-and-the-import-table][The
  pipeline]] explains the table.

*** What the synth costs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-the-synth-costs
:END:

In the 64,365-byte version, before the current size reductions, =synth.c= took
about 4.5 KB of its 61,026-byte stream in
this tree's build, as
[[file:pipeline.org::#codecost-py-what-each-function-costs-in-the-64k][codecost.py]]
counts it. That's about 3.4 KB of code and 1.1 KB of tables. The biggest pieces are
=mc_synth_render= (1.65 KB), =on_step= (0.62 KB) and the arrangement.

The arrangement's 278 ramps take 0.71 KB in their six columns, about 2.6 bytes
each. The patterns take 88 bytes and the chords 40. =audio.c= adds another
0.44 KB. The synth's float constants sit in the shared constant pool and aren't
counted per file. The current packed file has 2,299 bytes to spare.

In memory it takes 661,344 bytes, allocated once. Almost all of that is the
delay's two rings of 65,536 samples (512 KB) and the reverb's eight lines
(128 KB).

In time, the capture build renders the whole song in 8 to 10 s on the
i7-8550U laptop, about 30 times real time. Playing it live takes about 3% of
one core.

* Engineering notes
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: engineering-notes
:END:

These notes apply across the scenes. The renderer they lean on, from the TAA to
the warm-up at load, is described in
[[file:architecture.org::#the-renderer-and-its-post-stack][The renderer and its post stack]].

- Depth is cleared with the mask on. =mc_renderer_begin= sets
  =glDepthMask(GL_TRUE)= before its clear, because with the mask off a depth
  clear silently does nothing and each frame would inherit the last one's
  depth. =mc_renderer_end= turns the mask back on at the end.
- Instance buffers are sized up front. =demo_make= reserves =NBODY + MAX_LOGO=
  instances on every shape's GPU buffer (=mc_mesh_reserve_instances=) and
  allocates CPU instance arrays to match, so nothing reallocates mid-animation.
  The rounded box draws both the orbiting bodies and the logo cubes, which is
  why every buffer is sized for both. Scene 2's props, fish and creatures are
  reserved for their own budgets in the same place.
- Scene 1 and the end card draw their sky first, as a full-screen pass with
  the depth test and depth writes off. The grid is drawn additively, depth-tested
  but with depth writes off, so the bodies hide the grid and the grid can't hide
  them (see [[#the-sky-sky-frag][The sky]] and
  [[#the-additive-synthwave-grid-ground-frag][the grid]]).
- The supersampling is set per scene. The windowed main renders the cheap
  frames, Scene 1 and the end card, at 2160 lines high, and Scenes 2 and 3 at
  1620: 200% and 150% of a 1080p picture (=demo_fit=). It switches on the
  near-black cuts into Scene 2 and into the end card, where the TAA's history
  starting over can't show. The rule is set in lines rather than as a
  percentage, so a 1440p desktop gets 150% and 112%. A 4K one gets 100% and
  75%, the same frames as at 1080p instead of four times the work. A picture
  under 1080 lines counts as 1080, and the scale stops at 200%.

  =MC_RENDER_SCALE=25..200= sets one percentage for the whole show instead, and
  80 suits an integrated GPU. The headless screenshot main and
  =tools/verify.py= keep one scale throughout (80%, or =--scale=), and so does
  the capture main (200%, or =MC_CAP_SCALE=). The jitter depends only on the
  frame's time, so a frame rendered on its own is always the same plain frame.
- The release is set up the way a compo screen wants it. It opens fullscreen at
  the desktop's resolution with a blank pointer, and the screen is black from
  the moment the window opens (see
  [[file:architecture.org::#the-platform-seam][The platform seam]]). The picture
  stays 16:9 on any screen, because every shot is composed for that shape: in
  Scene 3, for one, two trunks frame the beach. Each frame the renderer is sized
  to the largest 16:9 rectangle in the window, centred between black bars. The
  load's last step, =demo_warm=, draws eight of the show's frames that are never
  shown, so that no program has to be built, or rebuilt for a new kind of
  target, during the show.
  The dev build stays a 1280x720 window, and letterboxes too if a window
  manager tiles it to another shape.
- Historical frame costs, before the current size reductions. These were
  measured on 28 October 2026 with
  =tools/verify.py --profile --width 1920 --height 1080 --frames 16= on the
  reference laptop's Intel UHD 620. Each is the median wall time of 16 frames at
  that moment, finished with =glFinish=. The middle column is the 80% that suits
  an integrated GPU, and the last is the release's own scale at 1080p.

  | Moment                             | Show time | At 80%  | Release scale   |
  |------------------------------------+-----------+---------+-----------------|
  | Scene 1, the breakdown             | 30.0 s    | 15.6 ms | 62.2 ms (200%)  |
  | Scene 2, just under the surface    | 73.3 s    | 20.9 ms | 56.3 ms (150%)  |
  | the deep                           | 104.0 s   | 25.8 ms | 66.9 ms         |
  | the chase                          | 126.1 s   | 35.3 ms | 78.3 ms         |
  | the flight                         | 149.5 s   | 34.2 ms | 80.1 ms         |
  | Scene 3, the look round            | 163.6 s   | 40.9 ms | 102.7 ms (150%) |
  | the canopy                         | 177.3 s   | 33.2 ms | 85.9 ms         |
  | the ladybug                        | 192.3 s   | 30.7 ms | 91.8 ms         |
  | the tyrannosaurus                  | 220.3 s   | 35.9 ms | 98.9 ms         |
  | the blob ashore                    | 242.1 s   | 40.4 ms | 112.5 ms        |
  | the blob at play, spray in the air | 257.8 s   | 42.2 ms | 122.1 ms        |

  Scene 1 at 200% costs about what Scene 2's cheapest sampled frame costs at
  150%, which is why it can have the higher scale. The release's scale is meant for a party
  machine's discrete GPU. One 25-40 times as fast as the UHD 620, such as an
  RTX 5080, should draw the worst of these frames in 3-5 ms of the 16.7 ms a
  frame has at 60 Hz. That was an extrapolation for a target competition GPU,
  not a measurement on an RTX 5080; the recording was made on the UHD 620.
  The revised visuals need fresh profiling. =MC_RENDER_SCALE=80= is there for
  a GPU that can't sustain the default scale.
- Several guards keep a still picture still, and the jungle's foliage needs
  them most. The bright pass drops any pixel that isn't finite, so a stray NaN
  stays one pixel. The TAA checks last frame's depths over 4x4 texels before it
  lets its history go, so an edge whose depth only flips under the jitter keeps
  it. The grain is added in the display's steps, where it can't make the
  shadows boil. The ambient occlusion rebuilds its normal from the depth texels
  beside the pixel, so it draws no dashes along silhouettes. Contact shadows
  fade out far from the near plane and under the depth of field's blur. The
  motion blur skips a velocity that isn't a number and takes at most 16 taps a
  side. The palm leaflets thin out by halves as they shrink (see
  [[#the-trees-s3-trees-c-jt-vert-jt-frag][The trees]]).
- Fades are dithered. The grain fades out with the picture, so the present
  step adds its own triangular dither of one step either way to the 8-bit
  write, whatever the fade. The =ldr= target before the TAA holds half floats,
  so the history never stores 8-bit steps for the dither to break up. The
  dither shrinks to nothing within a step of pure black and pure white. So the
  black between the scenes and at the end, and the burn-out's white, stay at 0
  and 255.
- There's one value noise. The shaders' value noise is a single helper, spelled
  the same in every file so that =genshaders.py= stores it once. =vn3(p)= is one
  fetch from the 32³ volume of random bytes that =noise_make= fills first thing
  at load (unit 1's 3D slot, wrapping, trilinear). Its coordinate is eased
  through the cubic inside its cell, so the filter leaves no creases. In 2D it
  reads the volume's first layer (=vn3(vec3(p, 0.0))=), where the fetch lands on
  texel centres in depth and the blend is plain bilinear.

  The sampler blends with 8 bits of sub-texel weight, so a value moves in steps
  of 1/256 of its cell's corner difference. That's fine enough for every use
  that sharpens it, such as the blob's normals (differenced across a sixth of a
  cell), the coral's and the weed's relief, and the sea's foam edges. It's too
  coarse, though, to difference into the slope that a mirror-sharp sun
  follows. So the sand's =vnd=, which gives its value and its exact slope, keeps
  its hash. The beach's sea keeps its own =vn= too, because there the draw
  decides which waves break white beside the blob as it plays.

  The volume repeats every 32 cells, which is wider than any use spans where its
  pattern could show. The hashes that remain either draw per cell or per
  instance (stars, specks, leaves, litter, the bakes' Voronoi cells) or have to
  tile at periods the volume can't (=jt_bake.frag='s =pn=). The seabed's rock
  tile is on unit 2, because a program can't read a 2D and a 3D texture from
  one unit.
- Shaders hot-reload in the dev build. =programs.def= lists all the
  vertex/fragment pairs, including the shared colour and shadow dependencies and
  the material baker. It pulls in =scene3.def=, which pulls in each jungle
  package's =s3_<pkg>.def=. Content hashes are polled four times a second.
  Changed programs and replacement material textures are installed together, and
  only once the whole update has succeeded. If it fails, the working set stays,
  and the update is tried again when the source next changes. Every uniform the
  C side sets sits at a fixed =layout(location)=, and every sampler off unit 0
  at a fixed =layout(binding)=. So a replacement program needs nothing looked up
  again, except that =uwsurf='s =uSandOnly= default is set again. The Makefile
  passes in the selected demo's shader root (=MC_SHADER_ROOT=). The release
  build embeds all the shaders and compiles out the disk watching.
- The windowed dev build has a transport for scrubbing the timeline. SPACE
  pauses, LEFT and RIGHT jump 5 s back or forward, and UP doubles the playback
  speed and DOWN halves it (from 1/8 to 4 times). The first keypress takes the
  clock over from the audio, and every change logs the time, the speed and the
  section's name. A seek backward reseeds Scene 1's collision sim and replays it
  forward to the new time (=sim_seek=), since the sim only runs forward. All of
  this sits under =#if !defined(MC_RELEASE) && !defined(MC_HEADLESS)=, so the
  shipped binary doesn't carry it (see
  [[file:architecture.org::#the-demo-as-a-case-study][The demo as a case study]]).

* How it ends
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: how-it-ends
:END:

The show plays once. The master clock climbs straight through all three scenes
and the end card and is clamped at =TOTAL_DUR=, about 278.7 s. =post.fade= takes
the end card to black over its last three beats as the coda dies away. When the
clock reaches the end, the release build exits. The program ends with the show
instead of holding a black frame forever, and it tears nothing down first. The
OS releases the window, the context and the audio thread, and =MC_TIDY= keeps
that cleanup out of the payload. It leaves by the =exit_group= system call
(=mc_exit=) and not libc's =exit=, so no library's exit handler runs. That way
nothing tears the GL driver or the audio library down under the audio thread,
which may still be in the middle of a write.

The music rings out too. Past the end, the sequencer holds its last step and
the arrangement its last values, so whatever is still sounding decays instead
of the song starting over in the audio already queued. The coda's last tenth of
a second is at -47.8 dB (RMS), the first tenth after the end at -47 dB, and it's
down to -65 dB by 0.8 s. The dev build stops the audio once and keeps running,
frozen on black, so the transport can still inspect the ending. =ESC= quits
early in any build.
